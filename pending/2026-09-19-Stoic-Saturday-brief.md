### 2. A UN mission found grounds to believe both sides committed atrocities
**This is the week's heaviest story and the likely centerpiece.**
- A **UN fact-finding mission** (reported Sept 17) found **reasonable grounds to believe the United
  States committed the war crime of indiscriminate attack** in a strike on the **Shajareh Tayyebeh
  primary school in Minab**, Hormozgan province, on **28 February** — a US Tomahawk that collapsed the
  roof, killing **156 civilians including 120 children**. The school was "clearly identifiable";
  investigators said the failure was more than negligence: the US "directed the strikes ... while being
  aware of a substantial risk of striking a civilian object and acting recklessly."
- The same report cited a strike on a "clearly identifiable sports complex and residential area" in
  **Lamerd** the same day, killing or injuring **22 civilians**. (CENTCOM said in March that US forces
  launched no strikes into Lamerd that day.)
- **The same mission also found that Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity** during the
  crackdown on protests that began in late December 2025 — unlawful killings, torture, arbitrary
  detention, enforced disappearances. Official toll: **3,038 killed, 25,000 injured**; the mission
  believes the real figure is far higher.
- Trump said in June that "nobody" had purposely attacked a girls' school; the Pentagon elevated its
  inquiry but has published no findings.
  <https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/17/iran-school-bombing-un-mission-us-military-behind-attack>
- **Why it matters for the theme:** one investigation, two indictments. The honest telling requires
  naming your own side's crime in the same breath as the adversary's — which is the harder discipline.

## Candidate themes (pick one at 16:30)

**A. Judgment, not events — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 5.**
"Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they take of them." The Fed hike was the
feared thing; the market absorbed it in a day. The Stoic claim is not that events are harmless but
that the disturbance is added by us. Pairs with items 1 and 3. **Note:** closest in spirit to last
week's "expect the splash," so watch for overlap.

**B. Justice is not partisan — the harder discipline (RECOMMENDED).**
The UN mission indicted both sides in one document. The Stoic virtue of justice (*iustitia*) does not
license you to prosecute only your opponent's crimes. Marcus Aurelius: "If it is not right, do not do
it; if it is not true, do not say it." This is the freshest theme, it fits Phil's established
willingness to criticize his own side, and it lands on the week's single most important story.
Strongest fit with the site's credo and with the editorial stance of the recent essays.

**C. The things we call unwatchable — premeditatio malorum, forward-facing.**
Hormuz, Bab al-Mandab, the Sunday German vote: the week is full of *named, dated* risks that the
audience can rehearse in advance. **Risk:** overlaps last week's rehearsal theme directly. Probably
discard.

**D. Two water now closed — the discipline of not panicking at the price.**
Oil above $108, two chokepoints shut, and yet markets ended the week up. Stoic calm as a claim about
what you can price versus what you can only endure. **Risk:** adjacent to B; would work as a
secondary thread under B rather than its own theme.
