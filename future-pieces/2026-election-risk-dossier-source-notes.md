## 0. The framing problem

The honest spine is a split: **everything intended to change the rules is losing, and everything intended to change the story is winning.**

- Rule-making and data campaigns are 0-24 in court, with both executive orders blocked, the USPS rule stayed, the SAVE overhaul vacated, and 24 consecutive DOJ voter-roll losses.
- Trust collapsed: 77% post-2024 → 60% (Jan 2026) → 55% (Sept 2026), and only 49% expect free and fair midterms. (UCSD / Center for Transparent and Trusted Elections, Sept 15, 2026.)
- The residual risk concentrated layers are: **presence** (agents at polls), **enforcement** (prosecutions aimed at officials and voters), and **certification** (post-Election-Day delay and delegitimization).

## 1. What is actually in force on Nov 3, 2026

**Nothing much changed for voters.** Brennan Center, Michael Waldman, Sept 9, 2026: "for voters, very little has actually changed. Congress and courts blocked all those bad ideas. The SAVE Act never passed. As a general matter, citizens can cast ballots as before."

The one surviving EO provision, per Brennan Center's tracker: §2(b) of the March 2025 order — the DHS/DOGE voter-file review — "underway in 16 states."

### Executive orders

**2025 EO — "Preserving and Protecting the Integrity of American Elections" (March 25, 2025).** Provisions: (a) §2(a) forced the EAC to add a documentary-citizenship requirement to the federal voter registration form; (b) §2(b) ordered DHS/DOGE to review state voter files; (c) §2(d) required federal voter-registration agencies to assess citizenship; (d) §3(d) imposed the same on the Federal Post Card Application for UOCAVA voters; (e) §4(a) conditioned EAC funding on state compliance; (f) §4(b) ordered VVSG revision and rescission of prior equipment certifications; (g) §7(a)/(b) targeted states with post-Election-Day mail-ballot receipt grace periods; (h) mandated 5-year election-record retention against a 22-month statutory floor.

Status (Brennan Center, updated June 24, 2026): §2(a) permanently blocked Oct 31, 2025 — three federal courts and a final judgment holding the president lacks authority to alter election procedures unilaterally. §2(d), §3(d), §4(b) blocked. §4(a) blocked as to AZ, CA, CO, CT, DE, HI, IL, MA, MD, ME, MI, MN, NJ, NM, NV, NY, OR, RI, VT, WA. §7(a)/(b) blocked as to plaintiff states, with a declaratory judgment in *Washington v. Trump* that the federal election-day statutes do not establish a national ballot receipt deadline. Live cases: *LULAC v. Executive Office of the President*, *California v. Trump*, *Washington v. Trump*.
https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/status-trumps-2025-anti-voting-executive-order

**2026 EO — "Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections," EO 14399 (March 31, 2026).** §2(a): DHS, through USCIS and with SSA, to compile and transmit "State Citizenship Lists" of confirmed adult citizens residing in each state, no fewer than 60 days before each federal election (i.e. **Sept 4, 2026**). §2(b): AG to prioritize prosecution of officials who "issue Federal ballots to individuals not eligible to vote in a Federal election," expressly including those acting "even unknowingly." §3: USPS rulemaking to police ballot mail — envelope standards, unique per-voter barcodes, and a state-submitted "Mail-In and Absentee Participation List" the Postal Service may refuse to deliver outside. §5: withholding federal funds from noncompliant states. §2(c): 5-year retention of voter-participation records.
https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/03/ensuring-citizenship-verification-and-integrity-in-federal-elections/

**DHS Implementation Memo (June 8, 2026), signed by Secretary Mullin.** Approves a "federated portal" through which state election officials "will be able to download separate outputs from USCIS, SSA, and DOS" with "name, date of birth, and relevant agency identifiers." Defers the citizen-facing transparency and redress function until "later in 2026," **after** the state-facing portal goes live — expressly to meet the EO deadline. Claims authority under 8 U.S.C. §1373 — the same statute the *LWV* court had rejected six days earlier.
(Quoted in the EPIC complaint, PP. 16–18.)

**USPS final rule — *Ballot Mail for Federal Elections*, 91 Fed. Reg. 54966 (Aug 26, 2026).** 95 pages. Requires election-mail logo, automation compatibility, a "specialized barcode unique to each voter," USPS envelope design review, and state upload of voter data to a Postal Service portal. Noncompliant mailings "will not be accepted and will be returned to the" state. (Per Alito's dissent in No. 26A305.)

**Litigation arc:**
- April 3, 2026 — 23 Democrat-led states, voting-rights groups, Democratic organizations sue.
- June 25, 2026 — Judge Indira Talwani (D. Mass.): key provisions unconstitutional; "The court is unaware of, and no party has proffered, any delegation Congress has made that would permit the USPS to regulate election mail."
- Aug 24, 2026 — SCOTUS lifts the injunction on narrow procedural grounds (premature; no final rule yet). Explicitly does not reach legality.
- Aug 26, 2026 — final rule published.
- Aug 27, 2026 — Talwani TRO; Sept 4, 2026 — converted to preliminary injunction.
- Sept 13–14, 2026 — Judge Carl Nichols (D.D.C.), a Trump appointee, independently finds USPS lacks authority.
- **Sept 14, 2026 — SCOTUS denies the stay, No. 26A305 (filed Sept 14; reported Sept 15).** Unsigned: "The Government is unlikely to succeed on the merits of its challenge to the District Court's preliminary injunction." Kavanaugh concurs: "there is at least a fair prospect that the final rule falls within the Postal Service's statutory authority," but applying it in 2026 "would be arbitrary and capricious" because officials lack time. Alito dissents, joined by Thomas.
https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/26a305_4g15.pdf

**Underlying legality is unresolved for future cycles.** Kavanaugh's concurrence plus the Alito/Thomas dissent signal a live merits question, and the USPS says it continues building the portal.

### Mail-ballot receipt deadlines survive

***Watson v. Republican National Committee*, No. 24-1260 (June 29, 2026), 5-4.** Barrett for the Court (joined by Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, Jackson): the federal election-day statutes "do not set a deadline for ballot receipt"; "The electorate's choice is made when voting is complete, not when ballots are received." The Court read UOCAVA to presuppose state receipt-deadline authority and rooted its reading in the Elections Clause and the constitutional electoral-college design. Alito dissented, joined by Thomas and Gorsuch, with Kavanaugh in part.

Roughly 30 states count at least some absentee ballots mailed by Election Day but received after. This removed the single biggest pre-election threat to late-arriving-ballot counting.
https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1260_g3cn.pdf

**But the postmark problem is real.** In a Federal Register notice effective Dec 24, 2025, USPS said it may not postmark mail the same day it takes possession. Because of network consolidation (Delivering for America / Regional Transportation Optimization), mail may not reach a processing facility the same day it is mailed. That matters in the 18 jurisdictions (14 states + DC, Guam, PR, USVI) that count postmark-by-Election-Day ballots received later. Postmaster General David Steiner told the Election Center conference in January 2026 the rule had caused "a lot of confusion," and advised voters to mail a week early or request a manual postmark at a post office.
- Votebeat, Jan 7, 2026: https://www.votebeat.org/2026/01/07/us-postal-service-usps-postmark-rule-impact-mail-voters-absentee-ballots/
- Votebeat, Jan 12, 2026 (conference): https://www.votebeat.org/2026/01/12/election-official-conference-2026-midterm-concerns-postmarks-mail-ballots-interference/
- Brookings, Dec 30, 2025: ~50% of post offices more than 100 miles from their processing center; Mississippi and West Virginia >95% of ZIP codes at high risk of postmark delay. https://www.brookings.edu/articles/when-a-postmark-no-longer-tracks-mailing/

## 2. The data campaign: 0-24 and counting

**DOJ demanded full statewide voter rolls from 48 states + DC; sued DC and 30 states; and has lost every decided case — 0-24 as of Sept 17, 2026.** The Sixth Circuit affirmed the Michigan dismissal (June 24, 2026). DOJ settled with Oklahoma. Roughly 16 states provided or committed to full unredacted lists (with DL/SSN): AL, AK, AR, FL, IN, IA, LA, MS, NE, OH, OK, SC, SD, TN, TX, WY. Alaska and Texas signed the confidential MOU; Mississippi, South Dakota, and Tennessee refused to sign but still handed over rolls.

Two independent grounds have defeated DOJ: (i) no "basis" shown, as Title III of the Civil Rights Act of 1960 requires; and (ii) voter rolls are not records that "come into possession" of election officials within the statute's meaning.

Judge Mary Kay Lanthier's Sept 17, 2026 Vermont decision is the cleanest statement: "The Attorney General's demand letter simply states '[t]he purpose of this request is to ascertain Vermont's compliance with the list maintenance requirements of the NVRA and HAVA[,]' without identifying a 'basis'... Indeed, the United States has provided no factual basis for its demand, as it has not alleged that Vermont is out of compliance with any federal election law." And: "Consistent with several other courts, this court holds that Title III of the Civil Rights Act does not apply to the Attorney General's request and that, even if it did, the United States' written demand is insufficient under Title III."
https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/doj-is-0-24-in-its-voter-roll-grab-after-vermont-demand-rejected/

Judges have been unusually direct about motive. The Oregon court found inconsistent arguments and contradictory public statements evidenced "ulterior motives" and that the "presumption of regularity that... [the DOJ] could be taken at its word... no longer holds." The Maine court found DOJ's promises to protect data and not build national databases "almost immediately undermined" by the executive order requiring a mass "citizenship list."
https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/federal-courts-reject-trump-administrations-attempts-obtain-private-voter

**The confidential MOU.** DOJ asked states to agree it would "test, analyze, and assess states' [voter rolls]" and send back a list of voters to be removed **within 45 days**. Brennan Center's analysis: this conflicts with the NVRA's notice-then-wait-two-federal-election-cycles rule for moved voters and with the NVRA 90-day quiet period (52 U.S.C. §20507(c)(2)(A)). The MOU also permits sharing data with contractors "not bound by the safeguards."
https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/confidential-agreements-show-trump-administrations-plans-states-voter

**The master database is in litigation.** *EPIC v. USCIS* (D. Md., Case No. 8:26-cv-03457-PX, filed Aug 31, 2026; announced Sept 1). Plaintiffs: EPIC, represented by Protect Democracy and CREW. Defendants: USCIS and Director Joseph B. Edlow; SSA and Commissioner Frank Bisignano; DHS and Secretary Markwayne Mullin; State and Secretary Marco Rubio.

Seven counts: APA excess of statutory authority; APA contrary to the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. §1306(a)(1); 42 U.S.C. §405(c)(2)(C)(viii)(I)); APA contrary to the Privacy Act (no SORN, no notice-and-comment, 5 U.S.C. §552a(e)(4), (11)); APA contrary to the Privacy Act (unauthorized disclosure, §552a(b)); arbitrary and capricious; contrary to constitutional power; and separation of powers / Elections Clause / Voter Qualifications Clause / Seventeenth Amendment.

The complaint's most quotable line: "Defendants' actions are flagrantly unlawful. No statute authorizes Defendants to share and centralize vast volumes of Americans' sensitive personal data across government agencies to create and disclose the 'State Citizenship Lists.'"

Relief sought includes vacatur, deletion of the lists, publication of required Privacy Act notices, and an injunction against any use of the data.

Documented defects in the underlying data:
- SSA's citizenship data reflects status **when the person applied for an SSN** and does not update on naturalization; SSA "did not begin to consistently maintain citizenship information until 1981," so data for people over 45 is incomplete. (SSA Assoc. Gen. Counsel letter, July 13, 2023.)
- SSA's own policy manual states SSA "does not have the legal authority to disclose information about U.S. citizens to DHS," and if a request spans citizens and noncitizens, "only information about the aliens can be disclosed."
- USCIS guidance: SAVE "may not be able to confirm that individual's U.S. citizenship" for acquired citizens who lack a Certificate of Citizenship.
- Error rates: "as high as 81% in some localities" (ProPublica / Texas Tribune, Feb 13, 2026).
- DHS's own Privacy Threshold Analysis acknowledged the SAVE modifications were "not in compliance with" the Privacy Act and suffered "shortfalls in data accuracy."
- The *LWV* court vacated the SAVE modifications on June 22, 2026 ("*League of Women Voters v. DHS*", No. 25-cv-3501-SLS, 2026 WL 1784297 (D.D.C.)), on Social Security Act, Privacy Act, APA, and statutory-authorization grounds; appeal docketed (D.C. Cir., No. 26-5243).

**Individual harms are concrete.** EPIC member Anthony Nel, a derived U.S. citizen in Denton County, Texas, "is one of many Texas voters whose voter registration was already wrongly cancelled by local election officials in Texas because of their use of the Modified SAVE system." He proved citizenship and was reinstated but fears recurrence.

**The lists were due Sept 4, 2026.** As of Sept 16, Pennsylvania had **not** received its list despite DHS's Sept 9 email promising it. Whether any state has received one is unresolved — the difference between an announced plan and a live data transfer. (Votebeat, Sept 16, 2026.)

### The money lever — the finding none of the six researchers surfaced

This is arguably the most consequential administrative action of the cycle, and it operates entirely outside the courts that have blocked everything else.

**DHS, through FEMA, made compliance with five election measures a condition of more than $1 billion in Homeland Security Grant Program funds (published July 10, 2026).** FEMA "will withhold 20% of the grant recipient's total HSGP award until they submit proof of compliance with these requirements and the Department verifies that proof."

The five conditions:
1. **Submit a plan for transitioning away from voting systems that use bar codes or QR codes to count votes**, in favor of "equipment that accepts hand-marked paper ballots."
2. **Conduct a manual audit of at least 5% of all ballots cast after each federal election.**
3. **Reconcile the number of voters who participated with the number of ballots cast.**
4. **Within 120 days of accepting the award, use the SAVE system to verify the citizenship status of everyone in the state voter registration database** — with a footnote acknowledging the June 22, 2026 *LWV* vacatur and offering "an immigration records search consistent with the requirements of 8 U.S.C. § 1373" as the interim workaround.
5. **Use SAVE "or another authorized government system" to verify the citizenship of anyone working at polling places or operating election systems** — "all individuals involved in the administration of elections."

Mullin's framing: *"Election security is national security and protecting the Nation's critical infrastructure is a top priority. ... These new requirements for homeland security grant recipients will preserve election integrity and ensure that Americans can trust the results."*

The NOFO (FY2026 HSGP, DHS-26-GPD-067-00-98): **$1,064,000,000 in program funding, 56 expected awards**, posted June 24, 2026, archived Aug 23, 2026. State Administrative Agencies are the eligible applicants.
- https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/07/10/dhs-requires-states-adopt-common-sense-election-security-measures-receiving-federal
- https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/2d6d8098-795d-4ba6-a92f-1171a66ed66d

**Why this matters more than the executive orders.** Every rule-making attempt died in court because the president has no Elections Clause authority. Conditioning a *voluntary* grant program is a different legal animal — DHS is not purporting to regulate elections, it is saying "if you want the money, do these things." And the conditions include two things no court has ordered and that Congress never enacted: mass SAVE screening of entire voter rolls, and citizenship verification of poll workers.

Note also what conditions 2 and 3 actually ask for: post-election manual audits and ballot reconciliation are *good* election practice, and condition 1 (hand-marked paper ballots) is the position of many voting-security advocates. That is the design — a mix of uncontroversial hygiene with two provisions that would implement the SAVE Act by administrative condition. Anyone writing about this has to separate the two.

**DHS also threatened to withhold emergency preparedness and counterterrorism funding from noncompliant states**, which is the same lever applied more bluntly.

**Mullin's letters to four secretaries of state (July 17, 2026)** went to California's Shirley Weber, New Jersey's Dale Caldwell, Nevada's Francisco Aguilar, and Pennsylvania's Al Schmidt, demanding responses within two weeks and asking them to "confirm their intentions to collaborate with DHS." The letters used the "may be as many as" construction: CA 190,832; NJ 35,152; NV 15,903; PA 14,576. https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/07/17/dhs-secretary-markwayne-mullin-sends-letters-secretaries-state-warning-about-non

### ICE is buying a national voter database

**ICE announced a contract worth $2–5 million for "the handling and secure delivery of publicly available voter registration files and voter history files to support Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) fraud detection and data segmentation activities."** The contractor must be able to obtain data from all 50 states, and ICE is seeking voting history "for each federal election specified by the government, including general, primary, runoff, and special federal elections."

The contracting officer is Michael Royer, section chief at HSI. The solicitation says the contractor will not be asked to collect party affiliation, but that could change if "explicitly directed in writing by the contracting officer." The contract term expires Sept 30, 2026.

**Related procurement:** 404 Media reported in July 2026 that ICE was set to pay **Thomson Reuters $125 million** for access to its CLEAR database — which aggregates names, addresses, property records, driver's licenses, social media profiles, and voter registration records from public and private sources.

**And ICE has gone around the procurement route:** Axios reported in June 2026 that ICE agents obtained individual voter rolls directly from election officials in two counties in Texas and North Carolina.

**The through-line WIRED reported a year earlier:** DOGE was building a master database using DHS data plus SSA, IRS, and voting data from Pennsylvania and Florida. A senior DHS official's quote: *"They are trying to amass a huge amount of data. It has nothing to do with finding fraud or wasteful spending... They are already cross-referencing immigration with SSA and IRS as well as voter data."*

**DHS also directed ICE to deport noncitizens who vote — with no criminal conviction required** (June 9, 2026, letter from DHS General Counsel James Percival). The Immigration and Nationality Act's removal provisions for illegal voting and false claims to citizenship "even allow for the removal of aliens legally in the U.S. if they illegally participate in our elections. A criminal conviction is not required to invoke these provisions." Percival: *"Illegal voting by aliens dilutes the votes of American citizens and undermines our democracy. It must have consequences."*
https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/06/09/dhs-directs-ice-deport-aliens-who-vote-american-elections

**This is the enforcement layer's real edge.** The deportation directive reaches lawful permanent residents. Combined with the SAVE database's documented error rates, the mechanism is: an erroneous match → a registration flag → a civil immigration consequence with no conviction required. That is the pathway that makes the data-quality problem matter to individuals, not just to aggregate statistics.

### Mass challenges — and the new federal ally

**At least 185,232 voter challenges were filed in 2024** (Brennan Center, from dozens of public-records requests; the real number is "likely higher"). That follows hundreds of thousands in the previous two cycles.

**The Gwinnett County case is the whole argument in one fact.** In 2022, one organization — VoterGA — filed 37,000 challenges. It took between 5 and 10 experienced elections staff working "all day, every day, six days a week" to evaluate them. In the end, **not a single ineligible voter was removed.**

The Allegheny County quote is worth stealing: a Republican member of the board of elections said, "I can't tell you how many hours of my life I've wasted trying to debunk" the challengers' false narratives.

**What's new in 2026 is federal backing.** Three specific threads:
1. The **DOJ MOU** "specifically allows it to share sensitive data with any 'contractor' who 'perform[s] duties related to the Department's list maintenance verification procedures.'" That is the pipe from federal data to private challengers.
2. **DOJ reportedly met with EagleAI's founder, Rick Richards, in 2023** to see the tool demonstrated. His account: "They like it."
3. In March 2025, **two DOGE team members** communicated with a political advocacy group seeking to "find evidence of voter fraud and to overturn election results in certain States," and one signed a "voter data agreement" with the group. Whether Social Security data actually moved is unresolved.

And the tools keep evolving: EagleAI's creators launched **ELLY** with "modifications and enhancements," pitched through the North Carolina Election Integrity Team to the North Carolina State Board of Elections. ELLY is the same tool Colorado secretary of state candidate James Wiley used to claim up to 25% of Colorado registrations have problems.

**The historical frame is the Brennan Center's, and it is a strong one:** challenge laws were "one of many devices states used to undermine the promise of the 15th Amendment." Poll taxes and literacy tests were outlawed in the 1960s; felony disenfranchisement and voter challenge laws survived "perhaps because they were not so closely associated with Jim Crow." The illustration: in 1946, Georgia gubernatorial candidate Eugene Talmadge distributed challenge forms and urged supporters to "disqualify and mark off the voters' list three-fourths of the Negro vote in this state."

### The state-law layer, in detail

Brennan Center's State Voting Laws Roundup (updated Aug 12, 2026) is the authoritative tally. The headline: **19 states have enacted 44 restrictive voting laws since January 2025, surpassing the previous record of 43 set in 2021-22.** Lawmakers in at least 41 states considered no fewer than 302 restrictive bills in 2026 alone.

The core group of repeat offenders: **Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, Kansas, Louisiana, Montana, Texas, and Wyoming** have each enacted at least 5 restrictive laws since 2021.

The specific mechanisms, several of which are worth naming because of how concrete the harm is:

**Proof of citizenship, with a bifurcated-roll trapdoor.**
- **South Dakota and Utah** now require prospective voters to show a passport or birth certificate to register in *state and local* elections. Those who cannot "will be able to vote only in federal elections." That is the NVRA workaround in operation: because federal law bars documentary proof for federal registration, the restriction applies to the state ballot and splits the electorate.
- **Utah's law** requires officials to review *existing* registrations for citizenship. Impacted voters get **30 days** to respond with documents before removal. Utah's own pre-enactment review found **zero** instances of noncitizen voting.
- **Kentucky** lets the state enter agreements with federal agencies to flag suspected noncitizens. Flagged voters must produce one of a handful of documents; otherwise they cast a provisional ballot and sign an affidavit. The law "does not require any additional verification by state officials or specify precautions federal agencies would take."
- **Florida** (effective 2027) requires comparing registrations against DMV records, and anyone whose citizenship cannot be verified, "including almost any registrant without a driver's license," must present documentary proof. It also requires anyone whose name on their citizenship document differs from their ID to submit proof of a legal name change, "a burden on many citizens, especially married women who have changed their surnames."
- **Mississippi** extended proof-of-citizenship from "both state and federal records indicate noncitizen" to "either" source failing to confirm.

**Voter ID narrowing.** Florida removed debit and credit cards, student IDs, retirement center IDs, neighborhood association IDs, and public assistance IDs. New Hampshire removed student IDs. Utah repealed utility bills and bank statements as ID. Nebraska cut the provisional-ballot ID window from a week to three days.

The Kansas provision is the most novel: a law that **invalidates driver's licenses reflecting a gender identity differing from sex assigned at birth** — and because driver's licenses are among the few accepted forms of voter ID in Kansas, "the new law would leave many transgender voters without an acceptable voter ID." It is under challenge.

**Challenge expansion — the suppression instrument made statutory.**
- **South Dakota** now lets any voter in the same county challenge another's registration based on suspected noncitizen status.
- **West Virginia** lets officials challenge residency on a new, stringent set of factors, and challenged voters "risk having their voter registrations canceled if a notice of the challenge is returned undeliverable or they do not present proof of residency in person within 30 days."

That second one matters because it is the quiet mechanism: an undeliverable notice plus a short response window plus mail that is already unreliable equals removal without the voter ever knowing.

**The Mississippi trigger law.** Mississippi currently accepts ballots postmarked by Election Day and received within five business days. Its legislature passed a law that would move the receipt deadline to the day *before* Election Day if the Supreme Court had ruled against the state in *Watson*. It did not trigger, because the Court upheld the grace period on June 29, 2026, but the statute remains on the books.

**And the counter-movement is real.** At least 7 states (Maine, Oregon, South Dakota, Tennessee, Utah, Vermont, Wyoming) have enacted legislation to bolster election security and guard against interference. Several are specifically aimed at federal agents at polling places.
- **Virginia repealed** the ability of an individual voter to challenge another voter's registration.
- **Washington** made frivolous challenges harder and prohibited challenges by voters from another county.
- **Virginia** extended mail-ballot cure deadlines and allowed emergency replacement ballots for voters who never received one within 10 days.
- **New Hampshire** now requires at least one polling station per 15,000 registered voters in presidential elections; previously cities could have as few as one regardless of population.
- Several states enacted State Voting Rights Acts. **Virginia** prohibits drawing districts that minimize the voting power of voters of color and expanded early voting hours on two Sundays.

And one notable asymmetry in the data: states enacted **46 expansive laws** in the cycle beginning January 2025, barely half the 85 enacted in 2021-22. The restrictive side set a record; the expansive side halved.

https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/state-voting-laws-roundup-may-2026

### Why Pennsylvania and Wisconsin count slowly — it is a feature of state law

Brennan Center's "Roadmap to the Official Count in the 2026 Election" (updated Sept 2, 2026) is the clearest explainer of the full post-election sequence, and one detail in it is load-bearing for election night:

**"Pennsylvania and Wisconsin are two of just six states that do not allow election officials to begin processing [mail ballots] until Election Day. States that do not begin processing mail ballots prior to Election Day are generally slower to release unofficial results than other states."**

Most states — including Arizona, Georgia, Michigan, and Nevada — allow pre-processing upon receipt or on a designated date before Election Day. That difference is state law, not malfunction, and it means the two states most likely to decide the House majority are structurally the two most likely to have a slow, shifting count.

The same guide supplies the full ten-step sequence (receiving, processing, tabulation, reporting, provisional adjudication, cure, canvass, audit, recount, certification) and two useful facts: about **98 percent** of votes will have a paper record, and **at least 25 states** have a postelection cure process letting voters fix mail-ballot errors, with windows as long as two or three weeks. It also notes that **25 states** require an automatic recount when the margin falls within a set range, and **41 states** let a losing candidate request one.

Brennan Center's summary of the certification step, worth quoting: *"the act of certifying the election is mandatory — it doesn't matter if the person charged with this duty doesn't like the results. State officials are required by law to certify the candidate with the most votes as the winner. If there is any attempt to interfere or delay the certification process, there are processes in place to ensure that the results will be certified on time."*

**But monitors have no special access.** Brennan Center's Sept 15, 2026 primer is the cleanest statement:
- *Congressional observers* — authorized by the Confirmation of Congressional Observer Access Act (52 U.S.C. §21083a). May observe, may question officials, but may not handle ballots, touch equipment, advocate, or jeopardize voter privacy; **state and local officials may remove them** for intimidation or disruption.
- *Federal observers* — VRA-based; require a court order or settlement. As of 2026 only **three** jurisdictions qualify: Union County, NJ; Pawtucket, RI; St. Landry Parish, LA.
- *DOJ staff monitors* — **no statutory authority for special access.** In many states they cannot enter a polling place without state or local consent; they are subject to state election-observer law; they must identify themselves; they **can be removed** for violating state or federal election law.
https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/roles-and-rules-federal-election-monitor-programs

### Three documented 2026 incidents of federal agents at polling sites

1. **Las Palmas Library, Bexar County, Texas (May 2026, early voting).** Nine ICE agents swarmed a car in the library parking lot. The library was a polling site. When the Bexar County sheriff learned of it, he went to the scene and reportedly told the agents to leave. They did.

2. **Simi Valley, California (June 2026, California primary).** Several ICE agents were spotted in the parking lot of a polling place. A witness from an immigrant support network reported the sighting; park rangers arrived and persuaded the agents to depart.

## 4. The certification layer: dates, duties, and remedies

**Exact 2026 deadlines, all seven battleground states** (jointly published *Election Certification Processes and Guardrails*, All Voting is Local / Brennan Center / Campaign Legal Center / Protect Democracy, updated Aug 3, 2026):

| State | County canvass/certification | State certification |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona | County boards by **Nov 19** | Secretary of State **Nov 23** |
| Georgia | Counties **Nov 9, 5 p.m.** | Secretary of State **Nov 20, 5 p.m.** (17th day) |
| Michigan | County boards **Nov 5–17** | Board of State Canvassers **Nov 23** (20th day) |
| Nevada | County canvass **Nov 13** | SoS + Supreme Court justices **Nov 24** |
| North Carolina | County canvass **Nov 13** | State Board **Nov 24** (may adjourn to **Dec 4**) |
| Pennsylvania | Counties **Nov 23** (third Monday) | **No statutory deadline** for the secretary |
| Wisconsin | Counties deliver to WEC **Nov 17** | WEC chair **Dec 1** |

Georgia runoff, if needed: **Dec 1, 2026**; county re-certifies **Dec 7**; SoS re-certifies **Dec 18**.

**National baseline:** 41 states set a hard state-level certification deadline, 7 tie it to a trigger, and **Pennsylvania and Rhode Island set none**. Median fixed window 22 days; fastest Delaware (2 days); slowest New York (42 days). (MultiState, https://www.multistate.us/elections/certification-101)

**Certification is ministerial everywhere examined, and no refusal has ever stuck.** The general guide: state statutes use "shall" language and "courts across the country have long acknowledged that officials have a mandatory, nondiscretionary duty to certify elections by the statutory deadlines."

Brennan Center documents "more than 30 rogue local officials in Arizona, Colorado, Georgia, Michigan, New Mexico, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, Utah, and Virginia" refusing since 2020; in every instance the election was certified — by court order, by reversal under threat of one, or both.

Michigan's constitution is explicit: "It shall be the ministerial, clerical, nondiscretionary duty of a board of canvassers, and of each individual member thereof, to certify election results based solely on: (1) certified statements of votes from counties..." Mich. Const. art. II, §7(3).

**Statutory substitution provisions:**
- **Michigan:** if a county fails to certify by day 14, it must immediately deliver all records to the Board of State Canvassers, which "shall then meet immediately and make the necessary determinations and certify the results not later than the twentieth day." **All costs are borne by the refusing county**, and the entire county board and staff must be present throughout. Mich. Comp. Laws §168.822(2).
- **Colorado:** Colo. Rev. Stat. §1-10-104(3).
- **North Carolina:** the state board may "secure the originals or copies [of the missing abstracts] from the appropriate clerks of superior court or county boards of elections" at county expense, and may **remove county board members** for "neglect or failure to perform duties." N.C. Gen. Stat. §§163-182.5(c), 163-22(c).
- **New Mexico** gives voters a private right of action: N.M. Stat. Ann. §1-13-1(C).

**Key holdings:**
- *Adams v. Fulton County*, No. 24CV011584, 2024 WL 4592443, at *4 (Ga. Super. Ct. Fulton Cnty. Oct. 14, 2024): local officials "**must** certify and **must** do so by a time certain. There are no exceptions."
- *Republican National Committee v. Eternal Vigilance Action*, 321 Ga. 771, 808 (2025): Georgia Supreme Court invalidated a State Election Board rule that would have let certifiers delay for an undefined "inquiry."
- *Lewis v. Lilly*, No. CL24000440-00, 2024 WL 5709710, at *4–6 (Va. Cir. Ct. Nov. 4, 2024): "[t]he personal beliefs of members of a local board of elections cannot derail the electoral process for the entire Commonwealth."
- *Crosby v. Fish*, 563 P.3d 143, 148 (Ariz. Ct. App. 2024): county officials who fail to certify are **not** protected by legislative immunity.

**Mandamus has worked, repeatedly:** Cochise County, AZ (2022, secretary of state's special action and a voter petition, both granted); New Mexico (2022); Washoe County, NV (2024, where the petition prompted a 4–1 reversal before being dismissed as moot); a Pennsylvania congressional candidate (2022). Most states also have a Rule 70 analogue letting a court direct *another person* to perform the act at the refusing party's expense.

**Criminal exposure is real but slow.** Arizona prosecuted two Cochise County supervisors. Peggy Judd pleaded guilty in October 2024 to a misdemeanor and received 90 days' unsupervised probation and a $500 fine. Tom Crosby's felony case was still awaiting trial as of April 2026, with trial unlikely before early 2027.

**Arizona is actively loosening this framework — flag it.** HB 2440 (2025), which would have barred prosecution of supervisors voting against certification in claimed good faith, passed both chambers and was vetoed by Gov. Katie Hobbs. SB 1003 (2026) would rewrite the duty so supervisors "acknowledge without prejudice" rather than canvass; it passed the Senate 16–11 in February 2026 and stalled on a tie vote in a House committee in March 2026. A state ruling also struck the election-manual language calling the county canvass a "non-discretionary duty," holding the manual goes beyond the statute — so whether the duty rests on statute or only on the manual is still being litigated.

**How to describe the 2026 threat precisely:** it is **delay**, not reversal. Protect Democracy: refusing to certify "accomplishes two things... First, it amplifies election conspiracy theories and further undermines public confidence in our election system. Second, it can lead to delays in the post-election process, particularly if state officials and candidates aren't prepared to respond quickly."

**Do not say 2026 refusals have happened. None can occur until after Nov 3.**

### Congressional certification (presidential only — note for 2028 context)

These matter for the dossier as the *architecture* the country built after 2020, not as a 2026 mechanism. Congressional elections are certified by the states under state law and seated under Article I, §5.

- Electors appointed on election day: 3 U.S.C. §1.
- Electors meet: first Tuesday after the second Wednesday in December = **Dec 15, 2026** (3 U.S.C. §7).
- Certificate of ascertainment issued by the state executive: no later than 6 days before electors meet = **Dec 9, 2026** (3 U.S.C. §5(a)(1)).
- Certificate must bear the state seal and "contain at least one security feature" (3 U.S.C. §5(a)(2)(C)).
- Conclusiveness: the certificate "shall be treated as conclusive in Congress," except certificates issued or revised by state or federal judicial relief before the electors meet, which "replace and supersede" others (3 U.S.C. §5(c)(1)).
- Expedited review: venue is the federal district where the state capital sits; a **three-judge panel**; direct, expedited Supreme Court review (3 U.S.C. §5(d)).
- Joint session: **Jan 6, 2027, 1:00 p.m.**, Hall of the House (3 U.S.C. §15(a)).
- VP role: "shall be limited to performing solely ministerial duties" with "no power to solely determine, accept, reject, or otherwise adjudicate or resolve disputes" (3 U.S.C. §15(b)).
- Objection threshold: writing, signed by "at least one-fifth of the Senators duly chosen and sworn and one-fifth of the Members of the House of Representatives duly chosen and sworn" — roughly 20 senators and 87 representatives.
- Only two grounds: electors not lawfully certified under §5(a)(1), or a vote "not been regularly given" (3 U.S.C. §15(d)(2)(B)(ii)).
- Sustaining requires "separate concurring votes of each House."
- Reduced majority: if a state appoints fewer electors than entitled, or a ground-(I) objection is sustained, the Twelfth Amendment majority threshold is **reduced by that number** (3 U.S.C. §15(e)(2)). That means a sustained objection *lowers* the bar for the remaining candidate rather than simply voiding votes — worth noting as a structural oddity.
- Note: the 2022 Act repealed and replaced the old 3 U.S.C. §5 safe harbor. What sources now call the "safe harbor" is the §5(a)(1) issuance deadline plus §5(c)(1) conclusiveness.
- ECRA is a statute, not a constitutional amendment; Congress could rewrite joint-session procedure by simple majorities.

## 5. House control of its own elections

**Article I, §5, cl. 1:** "Each House shall be the Judge of the Elections, Returns and Qualifications of its own Members."

***Powell v. McCormack*, 395 U.S. 486 (1969)**: the Article I §2 qualifications (age 25, seven years' citizenship, inhabitancy) are exclusive and cannot be supplemented under §5.

**Federal Contested Elections Act, 2 U.S.C. §§381–386:**
- §382(a): notice of contest filed with the Clerk and served on the contestee **within 30 days** after the result is declared.
- §382(b): notice must "state with particularity the grounds" and claim the right to the seat.
- §383(a): contestee answers **within 30 days**.
- §386(c): contestant takes testimony within 30 days after the answer; contestee within 30 days after that; contestant rebuttal within 10 days.
- Committee on House Administration has jurisdiction; traditionally a bipartisan three-member task force investigates; the House decides by simple majority and may affirm the contestee, **seat the contestant**, or **declare a vacancy**.
- The contestant bears the burden and "must overcome the presumption of the regularity of an election."

**The precedent that matters:** *McIntyre v. McCloskey*, Indiana's 8th District, **1985**. The state certified Republican Richard McIntyre the winner; the House voted to **seat Democrat Frank McCloskey** after a House-ordered recount gave McCloskey a **4-vote** margin. Before *Dornan v. Sanchez* (1996), the House last considered a contested election in 1985.

**Timing implication.** Because the 30-day notice clock runs from the state's *declaration* of the result, and the notice/answer/deposition ladder runs 30 + 30 + 30 + 30 + 10 days, a contested House seat can remain unresolved well into 2027. - Sept 28, 2025 — Gov. Mike Kehoe signs **HB 1**, dismantling the Democratic-leaning 5th District and dividing Kansas City across multiple districts.
- March 24, 2026 — Missouri Supreme Court, 4-3, upholds HB 1 in *Luther v. Hoskins*.
- Dec 9, 2025 — People Not Politicians submits **more than 305,000 signatures** (the requirement ranges 106,384–115,720 depending on which six districts clear their thresholds).
- **Aug 4, 2026** — Secretary of State **Denny Hoskins** refuses to place the referendum on the ballot, calling it unconstitutional. The 2025 map is used for the August primary.
- **Sept 3, 2026** — Missouri Supreme Court **unanimously** reverses: HB 1 "was never in effect and will not go into effect 'unless and until approved by the voters at the November 2026 general election.'" The court calls Hoskins's delay-and-expense arguments "particularly misplaced given the secretary's delay created" the problem.
- **Sept 8, 2026** — Justice **Kavanaugh denies** Missouri's emergency stay without referring it to the full Court. Less than an hour later, Chief U.S. District Judge **Stephen Clark** (E.D. Mo.) issues a TRO in a separate federal suit ordering use of the 2025 map.
- **Sept 10, 2026** — SCOTUS stays Clark's order, restoring the **2022** map — nine days before the federal deadline to send military and overseas ballots.
- Same day — Missouri Supreme Court **holds Hoskins in contempt** for a Sept 8 email instructing officials to use the 2025 map.
- **Sept 16–17, 2026** — Missouri Republicans ask the **8th Circuit** to revive the 2025 map and remove the referendum; argument heard Sept 17.

**Net effect: the 2022 map governs November.** Proposition A remains on the ballot, so voters will decide whether to adopt the HB 1 map for future cycles — a "yes" vote upholds HB 1; a "no" vote repeals it and keeps the 2022 map.
https://ballotpedia.org/Missouri_Proposition_A,_Congressional_Map_Referendum_(2026)

**One structural point worth making:** under HB 1, "the old map had six seats that were R+10 or redder and two seats that were D+10 or bluer — and no seats in the competitive zone in between. ... The new map, however, features seven districts with a Baseline of R+10 or redder and leaves just one seat that is D+10 or bluer." (Inside Elections, via Ballotpedia.) That is the purest illustration of what mid-decade redistricting does: it does not shift the median voter, it deletes the competitive middle.

### Voting Rights Act after *Callais*

***Louisiana v. Callais*, No. 24-109 (April 29, 2026), 6-3, Alito.** Louisiana's second majority-Black district was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. Three changes make vote-dilution claims nearly unwinnable: (1) plaintiffs' illustrative maps must satisfy the State's legitimate goals *including partisan goals and incumbency protection*; (2) plaintiffs must show racial bloc voting "cannot be explained by partisan affiliation"; (3) the totality-of-circumstances inquiry must rest on **present-day intentional** discrimination, discounting historical and "societal" evidence. CRS: "Section 2 is violated only when the evidence supports a strong inference that the State intentionally drew its districts to afford minority voters less opportunity because of their race." Kagan dissented; Thomas, joined by Gorsuch, would have held Section 2 does not regulate districting at all.
https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-109_21o3.pdf ; CRS LSB11431: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11431

The analytical move came from ***Alexander v. South Carolina State Conference of the NAACP***, 602 U.S. 1 (2024), 6-3, Alito: a state may defend a map as a **partisan** gerrymander to defeat a racial-gerrymandering claim. *Callais* extended that logic to Section 2 itself.

**Downstream:** within hours Florida passed a new map; within days Louisiana and Tennessee halted redistricting; Alabama moved to reverse a two-year-old remedial map. Brennan Center frames the likely effect as "the biggest contraction in Black political representation since the post-Reconstruction backlash" — advocacy framing, flagged.

**The 2023 contrast is the cleanest way to explain the shift.** In *Allen v. Milligan*, 599 U.S. 1 (2023), the Court held 5-4 that Alabama's map likely violated Section 2 and later refused to disturb a court-drawn remedial map. But on June 2, 2026, the Court cited *Callais* **and Purcell** to stay a district-court order blocking Alabama's 2023 map, allowing it for 2026 — "too close to the election." Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson dissented.

**Still pending before November:** Georgia's congressional VRA challenge (consolidated, at the Eleventh Circuit; the one state Ballotpedia still lists as having a map subject to ongoing litigation); Alabama's state Senate map (Eleventh Circuit; the 2021 map in force for 2026); the Texas SAVE/DPS suit; and Missouri at the Eighth Circuit.

### Eligibility rules in force for 2026

**Documentary proof of citizenship:** **Arizona, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Utah, Wyoming** require it from all registrants in 2026. Louisiana has a law on the books, not implemented. Florida's takes effect **2027**. Kentucky and Mississippi require it from some voters.

Two structural wrinkles worth explaining:
1. The NVRA bars proof-of-citizenship requirements for *federal* registration, and only six states are exempt (ID, MN, NH, WI, WY via Election-Day registration; ND because it has no registration). So most proof-of-citizenship laws can apply only to state elections and force **bifurcated rolls**.
2. New Hampshire's implementation produced documented failures — married women unable to register without a marriage license, at least one voter turned away three times, and 123 voters turned away at municipal elections.

**Voter ID:** seven measures on 2026 ballots (AZ, CA, CO, NV, NC, OH, OK). Oklahoma approved State Question 846 on Aug 25, 2026, 55–45.

**Who is affected:** roughly **21.3 million** U.S. citizens of voting age (about 9%) do not have proof of citizenship readily available; about **3.8 million** have none at all. Racial disparity: ~8% white vs ~11% citizens of color. (Brennan Center / VoteRiders / University of Maryland survey, n=2,386, Sept–Oct 2023, MoE ±2.6%.)

Kansas's similar 2010s law blocked more than 30,000 citizens before being struck down in 2018 — against 39 confirmed noncitizen registrations in 14 years.

**Volume of change:** 19 states and 44 restrictive voting laws since January 2025, surpassing the prior record of 43 in 2021–22. (Brennan Center State Voting Laws Roundup, updated Aug 12, 2026.)

## 7. Administration capacity

**Election-official turnover is historically high.** Bipartisan Policy Center data (via EAC) show turnover among local election officials reached **40.9% in 2024**, with more than half of chief local election officials in their roles under five years. Issue One found **50% of counties in 11 Western states lost their chief local election official since November 2020**, with 53 departures in 2025 — nearly the 55 after 2020. 76% of departures were voluntary. **80% of closely-decided 2020 counties saw turnover vs 40% of blowout counties.**

**Pay is the structural problem, not just politics.** Local election officials average ~$50,000 against ~$70,000 median for comparable public-sector executive roles. CERA certification costs $5,508. Sixty-hour workweeks are common in peak periods.

**2026 departures, weeks before the election:** Chatham County, GA election supervisor resigned Sept 15, 2026; Anson County, NC director resigned after three months; Chester County, PA director resigned March 2026; Pulaski County, AR board chair resigned; Nevada's Clark County registrar cited threats — family targeted, police checking the home hourly.

**Threats (Brennan Center 2026 survey, n=834, Jan 27–Feb 26):** 32% threatened, harassed, or abused (down from 38% in 2025); of the 14% who reported being threatened, 65% were threatened **in person**, most commonly at their offices. Nearly 1 in 4 are concerned about being assaulted at home or work. 52% are concerned about staff safety. More than half worry threats will make retention and recruitment harder. **75% say their state or local government has NOT provided extra resources to offset federal cuts.**

**Poll workers.** 2024 EAVS: more than 772,000 poll workers served in the 2024 general election; **48% of jurisdictions** reported difficulty recruiting. No 2026 national census exists yet. Jurisdiction-level: Clark County, NV preparing 4,000 workers for 23 long-term early-voting sites, 69 short-term sites, and 137 Election Day vote centers; Maricopa County, AZ hiring 4,000+ for 200+ polling centers (Sept 16, 2026); Washoe County, NV at ~85–90% recruited; Michigan's Chamber of Commerce launched a statewide recruitment campaign Aug 13, 2026.

Brennan Center's framing numbers for context: **130,000+ polling locations; 10,000 career election officials; ~800,000 poll workers.** At least 44 states require poll workers to live in the precinct or county; 42 states require bipartisan teams.

**Equipment.** As of Sept 15, 2026 the EAC had certified only a handful of VVSG 2.0 systems (VotingWorks VxSuite 4.0 certified Sept 15, 2026; Hart Verity Vanguard 1.1, June 26, 2026; Smartmatic VSR1 2.1, Nov 26, 2025). Only three systems were under test for VVSG 2.0. Most certified listings still trace to VSS 2002 or VVSG 1.0. In April 2026 the EAC proposed end-of-life for 45+ older models from ES&S, Hart, Unisyn, MicroVote, Clear Ballot, Premier, and Liberty Vote/Dominion.

**Vendor consolidation.** Dominion Voting Systems was sold and rebranded **Liberty Vote** in October 2025, consolidating voting equipment and e-pollbook power (KNOWiNK) under one owner, Scott Leiendecker. No vendor exit found in 2026 — the market consolidated rather than contracted.

**Paper trails are the backstop.** More than 95% of registered voters have a verifiable paper trail; fewer than 4% lack one.

**CISA: effectively dismantled for elections.**
- Workforce fell from ~3,400 to ~2,500; 2,324 of 3,292 positions filled as of June 2026.
- 17 election-security staff placed on administrative leave Feb 2025; election-security activity paused pending a review whose findings were never released.
- The FBI's Foreign Influence Task Force was disbanded Feb 5, 2025 (Bondi's first day); ODNI's Foreign Malign Influence Center was gutted and ODNI cut more than 40%; the State Department's Global Engagement Center closed April 2025.
- CISA did **not** stand up its Election Day situation room for the Nov 2025 elections — the first time in years.
- Mullin testified June 3, 2026 that CISA will target ~2,800 employees and proposed **$700M+** in additional FY2027 cuts; the FY2027 budget proposes eliminating election security entirely.
- No Senate-confirmed CISA director since Trump took office; Nick Andersen is acting. Half of CISA's ten regional directors serve in acting capacities.
- Remaining election staff are reportedly hesitant to assist officials "for fear of professional retribution."
- CISA says it is rehiring (~330 positions, ~180 offers expected); Warner calls it "insufficient."

**EI-ISAC defunded.** CISA terminated a ~$10M cooperative agreement with the Center for Internet Security in March 2025. The EI-ISAC served ~3,700 election jurisdictions in 2024 (all 50 states had joined by 2018). It now runs on a paid-membership model; state and local officials say rural offices cannot afford the fees. It planned to restart a situation room for the 2026 primaries using private vendors.
https://www.cisecurity.org/ei-isac

**~$39.6 million in FY2026 election-security funds Congress mandated in April 2026 have not been distributed by DHS/CISA.** Sens. Padilla and Rep. Morelle demanded release by Sept 9, 2026, citing the FY26 DHS Appropriations joint explanatory statement (Pub. L. 119-86, April 30, 2026, p. 75) directing continuation of CISA Election Security Advisors and the EI-ISAC. Democrats argue the Impoundment Control Act of 1974 requires it; no rescission request has been filed.
https://www.padilla.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/26.09.02-DHS-CISA-elections-EI-ISAC-funding-letter-FINAL.pdf

**The trust collapse is the real story.** Arizona's Secretary of State deliberately did **not** tell CISA about a 2025 pro-Iranian intrusion into his office's websites — routing through state homeland-security officials without naming his office, saying "I don't trust them." Michigan's deputy secretary of state: CISA relationships "destroyed"; local officials emailing CISA get no response.
https://www.theatlantic.com/national-security/2026/08/trump-iran-elections-cyberattacks/688297/ ; https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/04/federal-drawdown-election-support-destroyed-ongoing-relationships-experts-say/413181/

### CISA's own vulnerability assessment

**CISA's July 2026 election report (declassified July 10) documents systemic, not incidental, weaknesses.** In pen-testing and red-team work from 2019 to 2024, "CISA assessors gained full network control within hours or days" of state, local, tribal, and territorial election networks. Findings: flat networks, weak MFA, unpatched legacy software, sloppy identity management, limited logging, insufficient segmentation, and reliance on false "airgap" assumptions (machines are connected via vendor support tunnels). Certification rules can block timely patching — some regimes bar patches for months before an election. The report is explicit that paper ballots and manual audits are the backstop.

Handle this carefully: it is an authentic CISA product *and* a document surfaced inside a contested July 2026 rollout. It is a 2019–2024 retrospective, not a 2026 assessment.
https://www.globalsecurity.org/intell/library/reports/2026/0717/1-voting-system-vulnerabilities/cisa-election-report.pdf

### The confidence numbers

- **UC San Diego / Center for Transparent and Trusted Elections (Sept 15, 2026):** national vote-count confidence fell 77% (post-2024) → 60% (Jan 2026) → **55%** (Sept 2026). Only **49%** expect the midterms to be free and fair. **76%** are concerned AI deepfakes will affect the midterms. 37% expect ICE officers at voting sites in their area (45% Hispanic, 44% Black, 41% Asian American, 33% white). 89% hold their confidence level "at least moderately certain[ly]" — the views are entrenched.

**Deepfakes are now standard campaign content.**

Gretchen Whitmer about to be run over by a tractor (labeled); Senate candidate Mike Rogers released an unlabeled AI "Hulk" video. https://www.citizen.org/article/tracker-legislation-on-deepfakes-in-elections/

**Chatbots: good at arguing, bad at facts.** The Brennan Center tested ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Claude, Perplexity, and DeepSeek (Feb–Aug 2026) against six conspiracy theories — noncitizen voting, rigged machines, fraudulent officials, mail-ballot fraud, post-Election-Day counting, and the Starlink theory. All six disputed all six theories, even under repeated pushback. But **half of responses contained an inaccuracy or bad citation; one in three had a factual error; one in three had broken or misleading citations.** Claude falsely stated "ballots are only tabulated when political party observers are present" — no state requires this. Grok miscited a real document. Perplexity said the 2026 election had already happened. The same tools generated convincing fake noncitizen-voting images, a fake DHS memo, and a reproduction of a Russian 2024 disinformation video from a single public tool, mostly without jailbreaking.
https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/does-ai-fight-or-fuel-election-disinformation

### The cyber record: what has and has not happened

**No confirmed 2025–26 ransomware or cyberattack has been shown to have touched vote tabulation or altered a vote total.** The documented 2026 incidents are election-*adjacent* local-government intrusions.
- **Winona County, Minnesota** — ransomware discovered Jan 22, 2026, second attack Apr 7, systems restored by Apr 23; possible personal-data exposure with credit monitoring offered; the Minnesota National Guard Cyber Protection Team "tripled" recovery staff. No vote-system breach reported.
- **Foster City, CA** — a 2026 municipal ransomware incident cited by Check Point.
- **Arizona Secretary of State** — a suspected pro-Iranian intruder defaced websites displaying results dating to 2016, replacing candidate photos with an Iranian ayatollah's image, the day after U.S. strikes on Iran's nuclear program. Contained by state staff.

**The historical breach list (DHS, July 2026)** is worth having for context: Russia probed voter-registration databases in **all 50 states** (2016), stole ~500,000 records from a state board of elections (July 2016), and hacked a voter-verification software vendor (Aug 2016); Kennesaw State exposed ~7 million Georgia voter records including DL/SSN (March 2017); Iranian IRGC actors obtained voter data in at least one state (Sept 2020). **Brennan Center's structural reassurance (May 5, 2026):** no evidence voter-registration databases were corrupted in any prior election including 2020. All states now use dedicated IT security teams, MFA, restricted access, system audits, and **offline encrypted backups**; VRDBs are separate from tabulation. Ballot requests and early voting act as integrity tests that surface corruption before Election Day.

**Check Point's June 2026 finding is a good texture detail:** 4,010 "vote"-domain registrations between April 13 and May 14, 2026, against ~1,140 "election" domains — a burst of typo-squatting and phishing infrastructure keyed to the cycle.

## 11. Violence, intimidation, and suppression — the final layer

### The federal protection apparatus has been dismantled

**The DOJ Election Threats Task Force has no permanent leadership, and its supporting architecture is gone.** The head of the FBI's Elections Threats Task Force resigned in March 2025 and the role was unfilled as of April 2025. DOJ eliminated its centralized election-year command post, discontinued mandatory election-law training for prosecutors (not held since 2024), and restricted state officials' access to threat briefings. The 24-hour FBI election-week rapid-response operation that fielded bomb-threat, hacking, and grid-failure calls is disbanded, with response dispersed to 93 U.S. Attorneys — which former officials warn invites partisan inconsistency.
https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/trump-doj-curbs-efforts-to-safeguard-states-from-election-crimes

**The task force's cumulative output through its last published accounting was modest**, and the structural reason is worth understanding: of more than 2,000 threats referred to the FBI by election workers, DOJ opened about 100 investigations, charged 20 people, and won 15 convictions, losing one case. Colorado's secretary of state alone referred 1,140 threats since January 2023, producing 13 investigations and one prosecution.
https://abcnews.com/US/doj-task-force-formed-2021-fight-election-threats/story?id=113355654

The reason conversion is so low is the **"true threats" standard**. Prosecutors must prove a statement would reasonably be perceived as threatening, and the 2023 Supreme Court decision in *Counterman v. Colorado* added a subjective-awareness requirement. A vast majority of hostile communications are First Amendment-protected. A Nevada threat case ended in acquittal.

**Flag this as CONTESTED:** I found no document confirming outright abolition of the task force, nor one confirming its continuation. Issue One (Feb 2026) describes its status as "currently uncertain." Do not write "the task force is gone" as fact.

### Threats and the chilling effect

**2026 levels are at or near records.**
- Brennan Center 2026 survey (n=834, ±3%): **32%** experienced threats, harassment, or abuse because of their job; **52%** concerned about colleagues' safety; **23%** concerned about being assaulted at home or work; **45%** concerned about politically motivated investigations; **74%** said online false information makes the job harder or more dangerous.
- **45% of threats go unreported** (Brennan Center 2024).
- BDI-CivicPulse Q1 2026 (460+ officials): 51% insulted, 39% harassed, **17% threatened**, 1% physically attacked in the quarter. Minority officials were far more likely to worry about attacks (33% vs 9%); women more than men (18% vs 8%).
- **The chilling effect is now measurable and at a record:** **76%** of local officials reported reducing at least one key civic activity because of hostility, up from 73% in Q3 2025 and 66% in Q2 2025. Specifically: 43% less likely to seek re-election, 47% less likely to work on controversial topics, 64% less likely to use social media.
https://bridgingdivides.princeton.edu/updates/2026/hostility-against-local-officials-continuing-elevated-levels-2026-disproportionately

**The named departures tell the story better than the rates do.** Cathy Darling Allen (Shasta County, CA) retired in 2024 saying she had "been diagnosed with heart failure" and that "an essential part of recovering... is stress reduction." Her successor resigned for health reasons within a year. Joe Gloria (Clark County, NV) left after police checked his home hourly. Lisa Marra (Cochise County, AZ) described conditions as "physically and emotionally threatening"; that county then had five elections directors in two and a half years. Pinal County, AZ had four directors in four years.

**2026-specific:** Chatham County, Georgia's elections supervisor resigned **September 14, 2026** — six months into the job, three weeks before early voting — citing board non-engagement and "backlash" after flagging wrongdoing.
https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/09/16/chatham-county-elections-supervisor-resigns

**Protective infrastructure.** 35 states criminalize intimidation of or interference with election workers; 6 criminalize doxxing them; 10 allow address confidentiality. Durham County, NC converted an old Kroger into a consolidated facility at a cost of **$26 million**, with bulletproof glass at the front desk, control-access doors, perimeter cameras, duress buttons, a separate mailroom exhaust system for hazardous chemicals, and ballistic doors. The motivation was concrete: in the 2024 primary a person barred from voting after polls closed said he would "vote with a bullet." The director now reports "actual cases of people being threatened with harm, intimidated, and people having guns at drop boxes."
https://issueone.org/interviews/meet-the-faces-of-democracy-derek-bowens/

Demand is highest for training, not hardware: 36% of officials pointed to **conflict-resolution and de-escalation training** as what would reduce their concern; officials who received it described it as highly effective.

### Armed presence: what has actually happened

**Three ICE incidents at 2026 polling sites** (San Antonio in May, Simi Valley in June, Syracuse on June 23) — detailed above. In Syracuse, two agents entered the library polling site to serve a warning on a poll worker about her social media posts, telling her to remove the post or face "federal and state prosecution." The Onondaga County elections commissioner said state law limits who may enter a polling place and this did not appear to be an emergency exception. The New York attorney general's office is reviewing.

**New: a sitting state legislator is under felony investigation for displaying a firearm at a polling place** on June 9, 2026 primary day. South Carolina Rep. Michael Rivers (D-Beaufort) is the suspect in a SLED investigation of one count of pointing or presenting a firearm at a person at the Green Pond Community Center in Colleton County. The charge carries up to five years.
https://www.postandcourier.com/politics/michael-rivers-firearm-altercation-sled-investigation/article_5060aad6-3857-42d3-8341-e86d722068ab.html

**New: a pre-emptive lawsuit.** Common Cause, LULAC, UnidosUS, and the City of Denver sued in the District of Columbia on **September 10, 2026**, seeking a declaration that armed federal agents at polling locations are unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §592. Denver's county clerk has begun training staff on de-escalating interactions with federal agents and identifying plainclothes agents; the complaint notes the threat is already harming poll-worker recruitment.
https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/civil-rights-group-and-city-of-denver-ask-court-to-block-any-plans-for-armed-federal-agents-at-polls-during-midterms/

**And the stated posture.** Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche publicly asked, "Why is there objection to sending ICE officers to polling places?" DHS Secretary Mullin told a confirmation hearing he would send ICE to polls for a "specific threat." White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said in February 2026 she could not "guarantee that an ICE agent won't be around a polling location in November." Steve Bannon characterized the March 2026 ICE deployment to airports as a "test run." DHS and ICE officials have told states on calls that ICE would not be at polls — but **none has put that in writing** in response to the secretaries' March 9, 2026 written request to Mullin.

**Exact statutory text worth having.** 18 U.S.C. §592 (verified against Cornell LII): "Whoever, being an officer of the Army or Navy, or other person in the civil, military, or naval service of the United States, orders, brings, keeps, or has under his authority or control any troops or armed men at any place where a general or special election is held, unless such force be necessary to repel armed enemies of the United States, shall be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than five years, or both; and be disqualified from holding any office of honor, profit, or trust under the United States."

Two interpretive points: because it covers "armed men" of the "civil service," it reaches **federal law-enforcement agents, not just troops**. And the original "keeping peace at the polls" exception was **removed in 1909**, so civil-disorder justifications fail.

**One caveat flagged by the researcher:** no court has squarely interpreted the phrase "any place where... [an] election is held." Brennan Center argues plain meaning should cover election offices and ballot-counting facilities as well as polling places. That is contested.

**A genuinely useful state-level model.** California Attorney General Information Bulletin 2026-DLE-12 (May 5, 2026) confirms firearms are banned within **100 feet** of polling locations, drop boxes, and vote-counting sites, **including for concealed-carry licensees and federal agents**, and cites Penal Code §§69, 71, 422, 67/67.5, 518–523, 240–243 and Elections Code §§18502, 18540, 18541, 18544, 18546, 18564, 18568, 18575.
https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/2026-dle-12.pdf

Several Democratic-led states have moved to bar federal immigration enforcement near polling sites: bills offered or announced in California, Connecticut, New Mexico, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Virginia, and Washington. New Mexico's passed, largely mirroring §592. California's would bar enforcement within 200 feet of polls and let county officials extend poll hours after violations; Virginia's sets 40 feet; Connecticut's 250 feet; Washington's gives election officials authority to deny entry to non-public areas absent a warrant.
https://stateline.org/2026/03/05/blue-states-push-to-ban-ice-at-the-polls-amid-federal-voter-intimidation-fears/

**A caution for the dossier:** the researcher found **no documented 2026 case of armed private individuals or militia members at a polling place.** The most recent well-documented armed-voter case is from 2020 (Justin Dunn, Charlotte). Armed militia presence at drop boxes and polling places is a 2020–2022 documented phenomenon and a 2026 forecast, not a current observation.

### Mass challenges: the numbers in full

- **At least 185,232 challenges filed in 2024** (Brennan Center, after 42 public-records requests). Only about **3 percent** were clearly accepted — meaning challenges contributed almost nothing to list maintenance while consuming enormous staff time.
- True the Vote claimed 645,610 challenges across 1,322 counties by July 2024.
- **Georgia: 364,541 challenges filed in a single day, December 18, 2020**, by True the Vote, resulting in no removals. At least 92,000 in 2022, of which about 89,000 came from six people (producing roughly 2,208 removals). About 47,000 in Gwinnett County alone.
- **Maricopa County: 39 mass challenges covering 49,730 voters.** Texas: 15,000+ in Tarrant, 12,000+ in Collin, 17,000+ in Denton; one person challenged 9,600 in Travis County.
- **The judicial verdict, which is the best single quote in this section:** a federal court in Georgia found True the Vote's list "utterly lacked reliability... verges on recklessness... the list was shoddy and rife with errors." *Fair Fight Inc. v. True the Vote*, 710 F. Supp. 3d 1237, 1274–75 (N.D. Ga. 2024).
- **Tools:** EagleAI, True the Vote's IV3, Check My Vote, The People's Audit, VoteRef (which publishes voter rolls for 32 states and D.C.). The underlying data sources — change-of-address matches, Google scrapes, obituaries and Find a Grave, commercial address databases, property records — generate false positives on military personnel on assignment, college students, seasonal residents, and a nun on temporary assignment.

### Poll watchers in 2026

States that loosened guardrails after 2020: **Texas (2021)** makes it a crime for poll workers to take "any action" rendering observation "not reasonably effective," and limits removal of watchers to violations personally observed by the presiding judge or that are criminal offenses. Election workers report not understanding the law, and some have quit. Florida and North Carolina also expanded watcher access.

The scale is the story: Trump claimed in May 2026 that an "Election Integrity Army in every single State" helped win 2024 and that allies would build a "much bigger and stronger" one in 2026. The RNC reportedly aims to exceed the 230,000 volunteers used in 2024. Cleta Mitchell told Bannon she is "arming the army of patriots."

Countermeasure: **the Confirmation of Congressional Observer Access Act (2024, 52 U.S.C. §21083a)** codified congressional observers — they may observe fully and question officials, but are barred from handling ballots, touching equipment, or electioneering, and state and local officials may remove those who intimidate or disrupt. Senator Padilla and Leader Schumer launched a first-ever Senate election-observer program for 2026.

Useful benchmark for what rules actually constrain watchers: in **California**, poll watchers may not communicate with voters in line or within 100 feet, may not challenge or question voters' qualifications (only precinct-board members may challenge, on limited statutory grounds), and may not wear peace-officer or security-guard uniforms.

### The federal purge program, at scale

**DHS has run at least 67 million voter registrations through the expanded SAVE system** — 60 million in a year plus 7.4 million from North Carolina — primarily from Republican-controlled states. USCIS identified about **24,000 potential noncitizens**; the Civil Rights Division said about **350,000 apparent deceased**; North Carolina's own check added 34,000 potentially deceased.

Put that in ratio: even if every flag were verified ineligible, noncitizens would be about **400 per million registrations**. At least 25 states have used SAVE since April 2025. Six or more federal lawsuits have been filed over SAVE checks.
https://apnews.com/article/trump-voter-eligibility-purge-noncitizens-disenfranchised-8f78773f583e4404136707c62acc648a

**The quiet-period math matters.** The NVRA's 90-day bar on systematic removals means the blackout began on or about **August 5, 2026** for the November 3 election. Any systematic purge after that date is presumptively unlawful. Ohio's SB 293, which requires monthly SAVE and BMV checks, was frozen by a judge in August 2026, weeks before the registration deadline (*LWV of Ohio v. LaRose*, S.D. Ohio, filed Feb 13, 2026).

### Quieter suppression: the polling-place numbers

**Tarrant County, Texas** — 1.3 million registered voters, trending purple — voted 3–2 on party lines on September 1, 2026 to cut Election Day sites from **316 to 224** and early-voting sites from 50 to 47. That is **92 fewer Election Day locations**. One ZIP code (76103) would have no site at all. The election administrator said the map concentrates "where voters choose to vote"; Commissioner Roderick Miles called the reduction "modern day poll taxes." After the August 4 meeting, one attendee was carried out by deputies and another arrested a week later.
https://fortworthreport.org/2026/09/01/advocates-demand-increased-voting-access-as-tarrant-county-considers-cutting-polling-sites/

**Lubbock County** closed two Election Day sites for the May 2026 runoff because of a poll-worker shortage. **Karnes County** consolidated from nine sites to eight in August 2026 after losing two veteran election judges.

**Mail-ballot rejection is the sharpest number in this section.** California's June 2026 primary rejected **148,241 mail ballots — 1.73 percent**, the highest rate in a decade, against 1.56 percent in both 2022 and 2024. The breakdown: **93,479 rejected for lateness** (up from about 70,000 in 2022 and 76,000 in 2024), about 44,000 for signature mismatch, 8,300+ with no signature, 743 because the voter had already voted.

Kim Alexander of the California Voter Foundation on the cause: ballots "rejected due to lateness" are "caused primarily to be caused by being postmarked too late to count, not because they arrived too late to count." That ties directly to the USPS postmark change described in §1.
https://apnews.com/article/california-primary-rejected-mail-ballots-voting-2291684fe4544cab2301f8965abc8f79

**And curing works, which is the actionable finding.** A University of Pennsylvania study of the 2024 election found voters were **25 percentage points** more likely to have their vote counted if told of a disqualifying error *before* Election Day, **10 points** more likely if given a chance to cure before rather than only on Election Day, and **25 points** more likely when deficient ballots were automatically returned or replaced so the cure could be done by mail. Delaware County's director said automatic replacement roughly doubled the cure rate. The caveat: about **20 percent of Pennsylvania mail-ballot requestors do not supply an email**, so they may never be notified.
https://www.votebeat.org/pennsylvania/2026/06/03/mail-ballot-notice-cure-study-university-pennsylvania-study-2024-election/

**Provisional ballots, for calibration** (EAC EAVS Deep Dive, 2006–2016 data — label as precedent): provisionals are about **1.1 percent** of ballots cast in midterms and 1.8 percent in presidential years; roughly **79 percent were counted in midterm years**. Most common rejection reason is voter not registered in the state (about 44 percent of rejections), then wrong jurisdiction (10.6 percent) and wrong precinct (7.7 percent).

### January 6 as precedent

**Blanket clemency.** On January 20, 2025, Trump granted clemency to roughly **1,600** people convicted of or awaiting trial for January 6 offenses. Fourteen Oath Keepers and Proud Boys sentences were commuted (Rhodes, Meggs, Minuta, Nordean, Bertino, Biggs, Pezzolo); the rest received "full, complete, and unconditional" pardons. **Recidivism, which is the underreported datum.** By June 2026 Lawfare counted **at least 97** clemency recipients subsequently arrested, charged, or convicted — about **1 in 16** — including at least 14 charged with sex crimes or child sexual abuse material, six with domestic violence, and 20+ with DUI or public intoxication. CREW counted at least 33 by December 2025.

**The law-enforcement verdict is the strongest counter-signal available.** The Fraternal Order of Police, which endorsed Trump three times, and the International Association of Chiefs of Police jointly said the pardon of "individuals convicted of killing or assaulting law enforcement officers... sends a dangerous message that the consequences for attacking law enforcement are not severe." 83 percent of Americans opposed pardoning violent participants (Reuters/Ipsos, Feb 2025).

Greg Rosen, who led the Capitol Siege section before it was disbanded, resigned saying the pardons "send... that political violence towards a political goal is acceptable in a modern democratic society." ## 13. The candidate the midterms will actually test

Article I, §4: "The Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections for Senators and Representatives, shall be prescribed in each State by the Legislature thereof; but the Congress may at any time by Law make or alter such Regulations."

The president has no role. Every adverse ruling in this entire corpus — Talwani in Massachusetts, Nichols in D.C., Lanthier in Vermont, the SAVE vacatur, the six circuits that dismissed DOJ's voter-roll suits — rests on that clause. The system held because the clause is text, and because dozens of state and county officials of both parties chose to follow it.

The question the dossier should end on is not whether the rules survived 2026. It is whether the *belief* that the rules are worth following survives 2026 — and that is decided at the ballot box, not in court.
