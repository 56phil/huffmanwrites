**Question:** What did the Chiefs' best afternoon of the season cost them?

**Answer:** Both of the receivers who made it work. Kansas City is 4-0 and alone atop the AFC going into its bye, with Tyquan Thornton's season in doubt, Rashee Rice's hamstring unresolved, and the name Tyreek Hill back in the conversation.

## The game just played

The Chiefs beat the Raiders 30-27 at Allegiant Stadium on Sunday to move to 4-0, and it was the narrowest win of their season and the least comfortable.

Start with what Las Vegas did. The Raiders ran 78 plays to Kansas City's 58. They held the ball for 35 minutes and 42 seconds. They outgained the Chiefs 437 yards to 399, threw for 365, and never let Kirk Cousins get sacked. Cousins completed 31 of 52 passes for two touchdowns. Brock Bowers, playing through an illness, caught six for 86 yards and a score. Michael Mayer caught eight for 82 more. Matt Gay made all four of his field goals, from 32, 48, 44 and 48 yards, and scored 13 of the Raiders' 27 points.

They lost anyway, for three reasons. They turned the ball over twice, they went 0 for 2 in the red zone, and they could not tackle Kenneth Walker III.

Walker carried 22 times for 177 yards and two touchdowns, and the Chiefs needed every one of them. Late in the third quarter he broke a 76-yard run that Taron Johnson chased down at the 8-yard line, a carry Next Gen Stats clocked at 21.21 miles per hour. That drive ended early in the fourth quarter with Patrick Mahomes finding Thornton from 8 yards out, the first time all season the Raiders had been scored on in the fourth quarter.

Gay answered with field goals of 44 and 48 yards, the second with 4:53 left, and Las Vegas led 19-17 with the game still undecided. Mahomes answered with a drive of his own, and Walker finished it from 2 yards out with 3:21 remaining. The two-point pass failed, so the lead was 23-19 rather than 25-19.

That distinction lasted nineteen seconds. Dareke Young fumbled the ensuing kickoff, Kansas City recovered, and Walker went 22 yards for the score to make it 30-19 with 3:02 left. Bowers scored from 20 yards out with 1:46 on the clock, Cousins hit Cody White for the two-point conversion, and the Raiders had the ball down three with a minute to work. Their last drive reached midfield before Cousins was intercepted.

The shape of the afternoon is in the snap counts and the penalties. Kansas City ran 20 fewer plays than Las Vegas and won by three. It took eight penalties for 59 yards and converted five of thirteen third downs. It also won the turnover margin 2-0, its fourth straight game without losing a fumble.

There was one more piece of business. Left guard Kingsley Suamataia did not start, and Andy Reid would say only that the decision was disciplinary. Mike Caliendo took his place.

> "That's between Kingsley and I. We'll figure it out, and down the road, we'll be better as a team for it and Kingsley will be better for it."

— Andy Reid, [postgame, via the Associated Press recap, October 4](https://www.espn.com/nfl/recap?gameId=401872976)

Mahomes was 15 of 30 for 225 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions, and he was honest about the shape of it. It was not always going to be pretty, he said, and staying with the process is what makes it come.

He is 6-0 in starts at Allegiant Stadium. Reid is 7-1 there, and with Sunday's win he passed Josh McDaniels and Antonio Pierce for the most wins of any head coach at the venue.

## The state of the team

Four games in, Kansas City is 4-0, the AFC's only unbeaten team, 2-0 in the division, 4-0 in the conference, and seeded first. The point differential is plus-41. The five teams behind them at 3-1 are Jacksonville, Buffalo, Cleveland, Baltimore and, still, Las Vegas.

The offense is averaging 29.5 points and 421.5 yards a game, and it has been rebuilt around the run. Kansas City is gaining 162.3 yards a game on the ground at 5.5 a carry, and Walker's 537 rushing yards lead the league. ESPN's four-word summary of the team in its Week 5 power rankings, which puts the Chiefs second and up one spot, is "In Walker we trust." Mahomes is completing 64.1 percent of his passes for 1,037 yards, nine touchdowns and two interceptions, a 106.2 rating, on 32 attempts a game.

The situational numbers are the ones that have decided games. The Chiefs have scored on 89 percent of their red-zone possessions and reached the end zone on 63 percent of them. Their turnover differential is plus-five, and they have fumbled three times without losing one. Third down is the soft spot: 22 of 50, better than last season and still ordinary. So are the penalties, now 31 for 255 yards in four games.

Then there is the pass rush, which is the same complaint this team carried all of last season and has not yet answered. Kansas City has four sacks in four games, and on Sunday it did not get to Cousins once while he threw 52 times. The defense has 21 tackles for loss and four interceptions, so it is not passive. It simply is not finishing. The counterargument is that the Chiefs have not needed to: they have led for most of the season, and a team playing with a lead rushes the passer less. The counterargument to the counterargument is that they trailed for a good part of Sunday and still could not get home.

What the Raiders did to them through the middle is worth watching too. Bowers and Mayer combined for 14 catches and 168 yards, and a defense that has handled everything else has not handled tight ends.

## Injuries and personnel

Sunday cost the Chiefs the two receivers around whom the passing game had been built. Together, Thornton and Rashee Rice had caught 24 passes on 31 targets for 409 yards and three touchdowns in four games.

**Tyquan Thornton.** He dislocated his left ankle in the fourth quarter on the 55-yard catch that set up the go-ahead score, tackled from behind by safety Treydan Stukes, and left the field on a cart. He left the stadium on crutches. Ian Rapoport of NFL Network reported that the team expects him back in 12 to 16 weeks, with surgery scheduled this week and the hope that he returns at the end of the regular season or in the playoffs. He had five catches for 111 yards and two touchdowns before he went down, the best afternoon of his career.

**Rashee Rice.** Rice left in the first quarter with a right hamstring injury after a 7-yard catch, tried to continue, and went to the locker room before halftime after a long conversation with the trainer. He was held out the rest of the game as a precaution. Reid described the injury as a tweak. ESPN reported the team does not consider it serious and that the bye could let him avoid missing a game at all. The Athletic's Mike Silver was less optimistic, saying Rice could be out several weeks, a report NBC Sports read as speculation about how long a hamstring typically takes. The bye is what makes the difference: Kansas City does not play again until October 18, and by then the question answers itself.

**Tyreek Hill.** This is where the week turns. Hill is 32 and a free agent, coming off a dislocated knee with multiple torn ligaments, including the ACL, suffered with Miami in Week 4 of last season. The Dolphins released him in February. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, told ESPN's Adam Schefter on Monday that talks with several teams were beginning.

> "I would anticipate Tyreek potentially signing with a team over the next few days."

— Drew Rosenhaus, [via ESPN, October 5](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50109680/tyreek-hill-expected-sign-team-soon-agent-says)

Kansas City is among the teams involved and has stayed in contact. The case for it is not nostalgia. Hill spent six seasons here, caught 62 touchdowns and won a Super Bowl, he knows Reid's offense, and the Chiefs have not had a 1,000-yard receiver since they traded him. The team believes he is targeting a November return, and a deal would likely be for one year with incentives. Kansas City had just over $3 million in salary-cap space entering Monday, which is not much, and it is also enough.

The awkward part is the calendar. The Chiefs may have to decide on Hill before they know what Rice's hamstring is going to do. Signing a 32-year-old coming off a catastrophic knee injury to cover for a receiver who might be fine in two weeks is a bet on the season, not on the month.

Elsewhere, the roster is thin in the familiar places. Josh Simmons was listed as questionable on October 6 for a back injury that has cost him every snap of the season. Cooper McDonald remains on injured reserve and Omarr Norman-Lott remains out.

## The next game

Los Angeles Chargers at Kansas City, Sunday, October 18, 3:25 p.m. Central, on CBS at Arrowhead. It is the Chiefs' first home game since they beat the Colts on September 20.

The Chargers are 0-4, 0-2 in the division and seeded fourteenth in the AFC, and they have lost four straight: 26-14 to Arizona, 26-14 to Las Vegas, 24-16 at Buffalo and 30-23 at Seattle. They have scored 67 points and allowed 106. They lost by twelve to the same Raiders team that just left Kansas City with a three-point loss.

The line reflects it. DraftKings lists Kansas City at minus-7.5 with the total at 45.5 and the money line at minus-395 for the Chiefs and plus-310 for the Chargers. ESPN's Matchup Predictor gives Kansas City 72.0 percent and Los Angeles 27.8 percent. Those are ESPN's numbers, not this site's, and they are not a forecast this report is making.

Los Angeles is also hurt. The feed lists safety Derwin James Jr., defensive tackle Dalvin Tomlinson, tight end Charlie Kolar, receiver Brenen Thompson and guard Kayode Awosika all as questionable.

Three things to watch when it comes.

**Whether Rice plays.** Two weeks of rest is a real treatment for a hamstring, and the difference between a receiver room with Rice in it and one without him is the difference between a team that can run its offense and a team that has to invent one.

**Who lines up opposite Xavier Worthy.** Before Sunday the answer was Thornton. Now it is Jalen Royals, Nikko Remigio, rookie Cyrus Allen, or a name that is not on the roster yet. Reid praised receivers coach Chad O'Shea for keeping the rotation functional on Sunday, and said the group handled it because the scheme is the same for all of them.

**The pass rush against a bad offense.** If the Chiefs cannot sack the quarterback against a winless team at home, the problem is not the opponent.

## What to watch beyond Sunday

The schedule breaks kindly and then stops. After the Chargers come road trips to Seattle and Denver, then the Jets at home, then Atlanta and Arizona, and then Buffalo on Thanksgiving night. The stretch from late November is the real season: Buffalo, the Rams, Cincinnati, New England, San Francisco, and a final two weeks against the Chargers and Raiders. Kansas City will know by then what kind of team it is. What it does in the next four games determines what it needs to be.

The trade deadline is November 10, and the Chiefs now have a reason to look at it that they did not have a week ago.

The season-long markets have spent the last eight days repricing Kansas City upward. Kalshi puts the Chiefs at 63.5 percent to win the AFC West, up 11 points, on 2,938 in 24-hour volume. Its AFC championship price is 16.5 percent, up 3, its playoff qualifier is 88.5 percent, up 6, and its Super Bowl price is 8.5 percent, up 1. The venue also prices Kansas City at 26 percent to take the AFC's first seed, which is the market's way of saying that the easiest path through January is the one the Chiefs currently hold.

Polymarket reads it the same way and a little more generously: 64 percent to win the division, 86 percent to make the playoffs, 20 percent to win the AFC, 10 percent to win the Super Bowl, and 11 percent for Patrick Mahomes to win the MVP. Kalshi's win-total ladder crosses fifty percent between twelve and thirteen wins, at 55 percent for twelve or more and 40 percent for thirteen or more, which puts the market's expected total somewhere just past twelve. Last week the same ladder expected something near eleven and a half.

A 4-0 start has bought Kansas City two things: a two-week rest at the moment its receiver room fell apart, and a first-round bye it does not currently have to chase. The market has noticed both. What the market cannot price is whether the Chiefs walk out of the bye with Rashee Rice healthy, with Tyreek Hill on the roster, with neither, or with both. That is the week.
