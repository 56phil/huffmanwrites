**Question:** What did the Chiefs' 35-12 rout of the Jets cost them?

**Answer:** A sore throwing elbow and a hamstring, on the afternoon that ended a two-game losing streak. By the deadline, the cost had a price: a fourth-round pick and a fifth-round pick to Indianapolis, with a seventh coming back, for a slot receiver.

The score stopped being interesting sometime in the third quarter. The afternoon did not. Patrick Mahomes left the field late in the second quarter holding his right arm after David Bailey bent it on a strip-sack, and Xavier Worthy pulled up in the fourth quarter with a hamstring. Kansas City is 6-2. Whether it stays there depends on a joint nobody can see.

## The game

New York led 12-0 on four Jason Sanders field goals, the last from 54 yards with 2:16 left in the first half. It was the most misleading 12-0 in recent memory: the Jets finished the day with 15 first downs and 229 yards, and their only route to points was Sanders's leg.

Kansas City answered with two Walker touchdowns before halftime and led 14-12 at the break, then scored 21 more after it. The third quarter told the story, with two Chiefs touchdowns and minus-9 yards for the Jets.

Kenneth Walker III finished with 18 carries for 102 yards and four catches for 41, and he scored three times, twice through the air and once on the ground, the third three-score game of his career. His first touchdown came on the first play of the second quarter. His second was a 25-yard screen. Travis Kelce caught four passes for 72 yards and scored on a fourth-and-goal lateral from the 1. That catch total pushed Mahomes and Kelce to 9,365 yards together, past Philip Rivers and Antonio Gates for the most by a quarterback and tight end.

Mahomes finished 23 of 31 for 242 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions, a 117.9 rating, and the line flatters the game. He averaged 2.8 air yards per attempt, the second-lowest figure of his career, according to NFL Next Gen Stats. Kansas City threw short, ran for 163 yards at 5.1 a carry, and converted all four of its red-zone trips. The offense did not need to be vertical against this team, and it was not.

The defense produced five sacks, two from George Karlaftis, and held New York to 3.7 yards a play and 2 of 12 on third down. Drue Tranquill made 13 tackles and Nick Bolton 12. Three penalties for 20 yards, after eleven for 105 in Denver, were the quietest good news of the day. The Jets held the ball for 31 minutes and lost by 23.

That is the shape of a good team beating a bad one, and it is worth saying plainly that the Chiefs have not had many of those this season.

## The state of the team

At 6-2 the Chiefs are second in the AFC West and fifth in the conference. Denver is 7-2 and has won five straight. Las Vegas is 4-5 and the Chargers are 0-8. Nine games remain.

The season numbers describe a good offense and a defense that has been better than its sack count. Kansas City averages 25.3 points and 392.4 yards, 254.6 through the air and 137.8 on the ground at 5.1 a carry, and allows 17.9 points a game. The point differential is plus-59; Denver's is plus-12. Third down is 40 of 96, 41.7 percent, and red-zone touchdowns are 63.3 percent. The turnover margin is plus-four, with 11 takeaways and seven giveaways.

The pass rush is still the gap between the record and the roster. Fourteen sacks in eight games is 1.75 a game. Five of them came Sunday, and the question is whether that was the front four finding something or the Jets' offensive line giving it away. The next month is the test.

## Injuries and personnel

Mahomes's elbow is the season's central fact until it is not. The hit came from Bailey, with Will McDonald IV and Darrell Jackson Jr. arriving as well; Mahomes flexed the arm on the sideline, threw warmup passes, and returned without missing a snap. An MRI showed no significant damage, according to NFL Network's Ian Rapoport, [as ESPN reported](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50434564/chiefs-qb-patrick-mahomes-day-day-elbow-injury). Reid called it "more positive news than negative news on Patrick" and described the issue as soreness that needs rehab.

Mahomes was at the facility Monday for treatment, and the staff plans to watch swelling and pain when he throws. Reid said he did not know whether his quarterback would take full reps this week, and that the team would see where Mahomes is at Wednesday. The feed listed him as questionable on its November 9 report. Mahomes was plainer about his own intentions: "If I'm able to play, I'm going to play."

Worthy's hamstring has no such clarity. He was hurt without contact in the fourth quarter, after catching all six of his targets for 48 yards, and was ruled out. Reid said only that Worthy "tweaked his hamstring," [per Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/nfl/chiefs/onsi/chiefs-get-important-xavier-worthy-injury-update-after-win-over-jets). The team listed him day-to-day, and an MRI was pending as of Sunday night. The comparison everyone in Kansas City made without being asked was Rashee Rice, who strained a hamstring in Week 4 and did not return until Week 8.

The front office answered the receiver question at the deadline. Kansas City acquired Josh Downs from Indianapolis for a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick, receiving Downs and a 2027 seventh-round pick in return, [as ESPN reported](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50445576/chiefs-trade-colts-wr-josh-downs). Downs had 33 catches for 354 yards and a touchdown on 56 targets for a 2-7 Colts team, a 57.9 percent catch rate that ESPN called a career low. He is a slot receiver, he had requested the trade, and his contract expires after this season. ESPN graded the deal a B+ and noted that his above-average 68 open score could rise with Mahomes throwing to him, while Rice's and Worthy's slot usage may decline.

It is not a rescue. It is an admission that the receiver room could not absorb another injury, and a hedge against Rice's free agency as much as against Worthy's hamstring.

Jaylon Moore, the right tackle, left before the final whistle with an ankle injury and is day-to-day. He is the latest entry in a line that has lost Josh Simmons to injured reserve and has changed at three positions since August.

Elsewhere, Peter Woods and Mansoor Delane were listed questionable on the November 10 report. L'Jarius Sneed has been on injured reserve since October 31, Brashard Smith since October 24, and Simmons since October 22.

Then there is the kicker. Butker missed a 51-yard attempt wide right before halftime and leads the NFL with seven missed field goals; since the start of last season he has missed 17 kicks, the most of any kicker in that span. He was 5-for-5 on extra points Sunday and was booed at Arrowhead for the first time in his career. "I feel confident, I'm excited for the kicks, and I've just got to do better," he said, [per ESPN](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50427823/reid-no-decision-harrison-butker-chiefs-future). "I mean, I'm booing myself sometimes."

Reid has not decided anything. "I'm not making any decisions right now," he said after the game, and on Monday he said he would evaluate the position through the week. Justin Tucker, signed to the practice squad on October 28, is the alternative. A team with a 3.5-point line and a playoff seed to protect cannot treat a 63 percent kicker as a detail.

## The next game

Kansas City visits Atlanta on Sunday, November 15, at 12:00 p.m. Central (CST), which is 1:00 p.m. Eastern at the venue. Mercedes-Benz Stadium, on CBS.

The Falcons are 3-6, last in the NFC South, and they have lost four of five: 30-23 in overtime to Baltimore, 31-23 to Chicago, 16-0 at Tampa Bay, and 34-17 to Cincinnati, with a 29-20 win over San Francisco in between. Their injury report is worse than their record. Cameron Williams, Brian Robinson Jr. and Samson Ebukam are on injured reserve, Zachariah Branch is doubtful, and Anterio Thompson is out.

DraftKings lists Kansas City at minus-3.5 with the total at 45.5. ESPN's Matchup Predictor gives the Chiefs 78.1 percent and the Falcons 21.7. Those are the feed's numbers, not this site's.

Three things to watch.

**The quarterback's week.** Wednesday's practice report will say more about the season than Sunday's score did. If Mahomes practices fully, the elbow is a story about pain tolerance. If he does not, the week becomes about the backup, and about an offense that already threw for 2.8 air yards a pass.

**The new receiver.** How many snaps Downs gets, and where, is the first read on whether the deadline trade changes the offense or merely deepens it. A slot receiver arriving on a Tuesday has three practices to learn a system.

**The kicker.** Reid's decision will be made this week, and Tucker is on the practice squad for a reason. A 3.5-point game can turn on one attempt from 50.

## What to watch beyond Sunday

Nine games remain, and the division is the difference between a home playoff game and a road one. Denver leads by a game. The Chiefs have the better point differential by 47 and the worse record, which is the wrong way around. The AFC is crowded: Buffalo is 7-1, Denver 7-2, and Jacksonville and Pittsburgh are 6-2 with Kansas City. The margin between the fifth seed and the first is not large in games. It is large in January.

The market has made its adjustment. Kalshi prices Kansas City at 7.5 percent to win the Super Bowl, 13.5 percent to win the AFC, 41.5 percent to win the AFC West and 92 percent to make the playoffs. Against the same book eight days ago, the Super Bowl price is unchanged, the conference price is down a point, the division price is down 3, and the playoff price is up 7. The playoff bid also moved up two cents in the last day.

Kalshi's win-total ladder has nine or more wins at 94 percent, ten at 85, eleven at 68, twelve at 44, thirteen at 28 and fourteen at 10. The fifty-percent rung sits between eleven and twelve, so the market's expected total is somewhere near 11.5 wins. With nine games left, twelve wins means 6-3 from here.

Polymarket reads it a little differently: 8 percent for the Super Bowl, 12 for the conference, 42 for the division, 92 to make the playoffs and 1 for Mahomes as MVP. The two venues differ by a point or two on most markets, which is what two books do.

The shape of the season is in the two division prices. The market has stopped doubting that Kansas City makes the playoffs and started doubting that it wins the AFC West. That is a different team than the one that opened the season, and it is the one that has to play the next nine games.
