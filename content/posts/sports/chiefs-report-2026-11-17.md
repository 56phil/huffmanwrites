**Question:** How much of the Chiefs' season was resting on an elbow nobody could see?

**Answer:** More than Kansas City wanted to admit, and less than the team feared. Patrick Mahomes threw five touchdown passes on a sprained ligament in Atlanta, and an offense that had spent two months throwing short found its deep ball again.

The medical detail arrived Sunday morning. ESPN's Adam Schefter reported that the Chiefs had sent Mahomes's scans to Dr. Vincent Key, the Royals' team physician, and then to Dr. Keith Meister, the Rangers' team physician, both of them specialists in the elbow injuries that end pitchers' seasons. The diagnosis was a muscle strain and, in the words of a source, "a very small sprain of his UCL." Both doctors believed he could play, and Key's assessment, as Schefter reported it, was that the Chiefs had gotten away with one.

That afternoon Mahomes threw for 348 yards and five touchdowns without an interception. The comparison in the same story is the one that lingers: Gardner Minshew tore his UCL on a similar play and was expected to need season-ending surgery.

## The game

Kansas City 35, Atlanta 13, and the game was decided well before the fourth quarter. The Chiefs led 14-3 at halftime, 21-3 after Cyrus Allen's touchdown in the third, and 28-3 early in the fourth. Atlanta did not convert a first down on any of its first four possessions, and its only points before the fourth quarter came on a 28-yard field goal.

The five touchdown passes measured 12, 18, 39, 6 and 43 yards, and the shape of that list is the afternoon's story. The first two were the offense Kansas City has been running since September: Emmett Johnson on a 12-yard catch on the opening drive, Josh Downs on an 18-yarder with 18 seconds left in the half. The last two were the offense it had not been able to run. Allen scored on a 39-yard stop-and-go for his first career touchdown, and Andrew Armstrong on a 43-yard play-action pass for his.

Mahomes finished 22 of 35 for 348 yards, five touchdowns and no interceptions, a 135.5 rating, sacked once. The yardage and the touchdowns were season highs. He completed three of six throws that traveled more than 20 air yards, for 132 yards and two scores, and it was his first game with multiple deep touchdown passes since 2021, [as ESPN's Nate Taylor reported](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50496736/patrick-mahomes-stars-chiefs-sync-josh-downs).

> "You don't know until you get out there and that adrenaline starts kicking in."

— Patrick Mahomes, [Patrick Mahomes stars for Chiefs, strikes early connection with Josh Downs](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50496736/patrick-mahomes-stars-chiefs-sync-josh-downs), ESPN, November 15, 2026

Emmett Johnson, starting because Kenneth Walker III's groin would not cooperate, carried 22 times for 101 yards and caught six passes for 70 more, with two touchdowns. His 171 yards from scrimmage led the team. A rookie who had spent the autumn behind Walker became, for an afternoon, both the leading rusher and the second-leading receiver.

Josh Downs played 10 snaps in his Chiefs debut, ran eight routes, drew five targets and caught four for 53 yards and a touchdown. His target rate of 62.5 percent was a career high, per Next Gen Stats. Ten snaps is not a large role, but the touchdown came with 18 seconds left in the first half, which is the situation that prompted the trade.

Travis Kelce caught two passes for 30 yards and left in the first half with a calf injury before returning in the third quarter.

The defense did what it has done most of the season. Michael Penix Jr. completed 23 of 43 for 286 yards with no touchdowns and one interception, a 35.4 QBR. Bijan Robinson, the league's leading rusher entering the day, carried 16 times for 71 yards. Atlanta reached the red zone five times and scored once, and Nohl Williams ended the last real threat with an interception in the end zone with 2:39 left.

Two numbers are worth keeping. Kansas City went 4 of 13 on third down, which is the offense's chronic problem and not a new one. And it took seven penalties for 61 yards, after eleven for 105 in Denver and three for 20 against the Jets, so the discipline is going the wrong way at the moment when a 22-point win hides it.

## The state of the team

At 7-2 the Chiefs are level with Denver atop the AFC West. The feed lists Kansas City first in the division and second in the conference, with the Broncos fifth. Las Vegas is 4-6 and the Chargers are 1-8, so the West is a two-team race with eight games left.

The season numbers describe a good offense and a defense that has been better than its sack count. Kansas City averages 26.3 points and 401 yards, 265 through the air and 136 on the ground at 5.0 a carry, and allows 17.3 points a game. The point differential is plus-81. Denver's is plus-12, and that 69-point gap is the reason a team with a 63 percent kicker and a 40 percent third-down rate is 7-2.

Third down is 44 of 109, 40.4 percent. The red zone is the opposite: 28 scores in 33 trips, 84.8 percent, with touchdowns on two-thirds of them. The turnover margin is plus-six, on 13 takeaways and seven giveaways. The pass rush has 17 sacks in nine games, 1.9 a game, three of them Sunday, and it remains the distance between the record and the roster.

The passing totals are strong: 2,385 yards, 19 touchdowns, four interceptions, a 106.7 rating and 66.8 percent completions. They were also compiled on short throws, which is why one afternoon in Atlanta says more about the offense than the totals do.

Then there is the kicker. Harrison Butker is 12 of 19 on field goals, 63.2 percent, and two of eight from 50 yards or longer.

## Injuries and personnel

The elbow is the season's central fact until it is not. Mahomes wore a Hyperice sleeve Sunday and threw 35 passes, and the immediate question is not whether he can play but how the joint responds to a short week: the Chiefs play Sunday and again the following Thursday at Buffalo, a four-day turnaround.

Walker was ruled out Friday after reporting tightness in his groin before the week's final practice. A source told ESPN the Chiefs "want to give him a week," which is what a team says about a minor injury with a short runway. He is listed as questionable for Arizona. He had 872 rushing yards entering Week 10, second in the league to Robinson's 880.

Kelce's calf is the week's quiet worry. He returned and finished the game, and the feed's Monday report does not list him, which is not the same as a clean bill of health for a player who has been managing an ankle for weeks.

Trey Smith left in the first quarter and was ruled out with a concussion. He must clear the league's protocol before he plays again. Jared Wiley fractured an ankle and is out. Jaylon Moore, Peter Woods and Jaydn Ott are all listed as questionable.

And the kicker, again. Reid committed to Butker on November 11, saying "I'm going to stick with Butker," [as ESPN reported](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50454081/chiefs-sticking-harrison-butker-kicker-vs-falcons), and special teams coordinator Dave Toub added that the staff is "not just going to pull the plug." Sunday offered no verdict, because Butker attempted no field goals. He made all five extra points and watched the game turn on other things. The reprieve is not a resolution.

## The next game

Arizona visits Arrowhead Stadium on Sunday, November 22, at 12:00 p.m. Central (CST), on CBS. The Cardinals are 3-7 overall and 3-3 on the road, and they have lost two straight. Kansas City is 4-0 at home.

The last five weeks describe a team going the other way. Arizona beat the Rams 18-16, lost to Denver 30-16, beat Dallas 33-30 in overtime, lost at Seattle 17-3 and lost to the Rams again, 34-6. That is nine points in two games. James Conner and Jordan Burch are on injured reserve, and three offensive linemen, Jayden Williams, Hjalte Froholdt and Jon Gaines II, are listed as questionable.

DraftKings lists Kansas City at minus-16.5 with the total at 41.5, and the money line at minus-1350 for the Chiefs and plus-800 for Arizona. The line opened at Kansas City minus-11.5 with a 44.5 total, so the number has moved five points in a week. ESPN Analytics gives the Chiefs 88.2 percent. Those are the feed's odds and the feed's projection, not this site's.

Three things to watch.

**The elbow on a normal week.** Sunday was the best-case version: a full week of practice, a cleared quarterback, and a defense that could not punish him. Buffalo on four days' rest is the other case. Watch whether the deep shots stay in the plan or whether Atlanta was a matchup.

**The backfield.** Walker is questionable and Ott is questionable, so Johnson may carry 22 times again. The rookie was excellent Sunday. Two weeks of it is a different question, and the answer arrives against the league's best teams.

**The kicker.** A 16.5-point line is not a test of a kicker, because the game may never need one. It may also be the last week the Chiefs can defer the question without cost.

## What to watch beyond Sunday

The schedule is about to get hard. After Arizona, the Chiefs are at Buffalo on November 26, at the Rams on December 3, at Cincinnati on December 13, home to New England on December 21, home to San Francisco on December 27, then at the Chargers and home to the Raiders. Four of the last eight are on the road, and the run includes Buffalo at 8-1, Cincinnati at 7-2 and San Francisco at 8-1.

The power rankings have noticed. ESPN moved Kansas City to fifth this week, up from sixth, behind San Francisco, Buffalo, Jacksonville and Chicago.

The market has made a narrower adjustment. Kalshi prices the Chiefs at 7.5 percent to win the Super Bowl, 14.5 percent to win the AFC, 45.5 percent to win the AFC West and 97.5 percent to make the playoffs. Against the same book eight days ago, the Super Bowl and conference prices are unchanged, the division price is up four points and the playoff price is up six.

That divergence is the interesting part. The market has grown more confident that Kansas City wins the division and reaches the postseason without growing any more confident that it wins a championship. The regular season is being priced as settled. January is not.
