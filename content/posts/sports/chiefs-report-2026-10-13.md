**Question:** What did the Chiefs do with a week off and two receivers in the training room?

**Answer:** They filled the hole with a name they already knew. Justin Watson is back on the practice squad, Tyquan Thornton is on injured reserve, and the reunion the league spent a week waiting for did not happen.

## The bye week

Kansas City did not play in Week 5. There is no score to weigh and no fourth quarter to take apart, which after the Raiders game is its own kind of relief. What the bye gave the Chiefs was time to answer the question that game left them: what does this offense do when both of the receivers around whom it was built go down in the same afternoon?

Andy Reid's answer came Monday, at his first media availability in a week. Thornton goes to injured reserve. Justin Watson, who played here from 2022 through 2024 and won championships doing it, returns on the practice squad. Cam Jones comes up from the practice squad to take Thornton's spot on the 53.

That last move carries the meaning. Kansas City had a vacancy at wide receiver and filled it with a linebacker. Pro Football Talk noted it dryly on Tuesday: the Chiefs "did not replace Tyquan Thornton on the active roster with another wide receiver."

So the answer is a receiver who knows the playbook better than he knows the depth chart, and a roster that says the team is not panicking. Both can be true in October. Whether they are still true in December is the rest of the season.

## The state of the team

Four games in, Kansas City is 4-0 and the only unbeaten team in the AFC. Only San Francisco and Minnesota, both at 5-0, have a better record in the league.

Within the division the Chiefs are 2-0, within the conference 4-0, and they hold the AFC's first seed with a point differential of plus-41, ahead of Baltimore, Jacksonville and Buffalo at 4-1. Denver and Las Vegas are each 3-2, and the Chargers are winless.

The offense has averaged 29.5 points and 421.5 yards a game, with 162.3 of those yards on the ground at 5.5 a carry. Patrick Mahomes is completing 64.1 percent of his passes for 1,037 yards, nine touchdowns and two interceptions, a 106.2 rating.

Those are the numbers of a team at full strength, and the Chiefs are not one. The passing game they describe had Thornton and Rashee Rice in it.

The pass rush is the older problem. Kansas City has four sacks in four games, and Sports Illustrated's Chiefs site reports that all four came in the opening win over Denver. ESPN's Week 6 power rankings hold the Chiefs at second and name "Not enough sacks" as the biggest issue on defense, noting that the unit generated a season-high 21 pressures against the Raiders and still never brought Kirk Cousins down. That is the shape of it: pressure without a finish, which is closer to a solution than last season's problem was.

Elsewhere the numbers are sound. Third down is 22 of 50, ordinary and improved. Penalties are 31 for 255 yards in four games, which is a lot of free field position. The turnover margin is plus-five, and the Chiefs have fumbled three times without losing one. Harrison Butker is 7 of 9 on field goals with a long of 46. Matt Araiza is averaging 52.3 yards a punt and has pinned nine of twelve inside the 20.

## Injuries and personnel

**Tyquan Thornton.** The Chiefs made it official Tuesday, moving him to injured reserve. He dislocated his left ankle in the fourth quarter against the Raiders, on the 55-yard catch that set up the go-ahead score, after the best afternoon of his career: five catches, 111 yards and two touchdowns. He will have surgery, and the estimates run from three to four months to the twelve to sixteen weeks ESPN's Ian Rapoport reported last week. Either way, most of the regular season is gone.

**Rashee Rice.** Listed as questionable since October 5 with the right hamstring he hurt in the first quarter at Las Vegas. Reid said Rice spent the bye week at the facility rehabbing, that he would return Tuesday, and that a clearer picture would come Wednesday. Day to day is the working description. Two weeks of rest is real treatment for a hamstring, and Rice's return is the largest single variable in what this offense becomes.

**Justin Watson.** Signed to the practice squad Monday after three seasons in Kansas City from 2022 through 2024. Reid pointed to his familiarity with the offense, which is the whole case for him. He can be elevated for a game at any point.

**Josh Simmons.** Out for the entire regular season so far with a bulging disc in his back, and also at the facility through the bye. Reid offered the same Wednesday timeline for him. If Simmons does return, the line moves, because the Chiefs have been starting a reshuffled group since August and his presence would push others back to their natural spots.

**Kingsley Suamataia.** Back in the lineup this week at left guard. He did not start against the Raiders, a decision Reid has declined to explain beyond calling it a matter between the two of them.

**Tyreek Hill.** The reunion did not happen. Hill remains a free agent, and a week that began with his agent predicting a signing ended with Kansas City adding a practice-squad receiver instead. Hill testified in Miami on October 8, the third day of the civil assault-and-battery case his estranged wife, Lakeeta Vaccaro, brought against him; he denied striking, scratching, shoving, spitting at or punching her. The NFL is reviewing his personal conduct under its own policy, on a schedule the jury does not control. Whatever comes of it, the Chiefs chose not to wait.

## The next game

Los Angeles at Kansas City, Sunday, October 18, 3:25 p.m. Central at Arrowhead, on CBS. It is the Chiefs' first home game since they beat the Colts on September 20.

The Chargers are 0-5, 0-2 in the division, 0-3 in the conference and fifteenth in the AFC. They have lost five straight and been outscored 129-84: 26-14 to Arizona, 26-14 to Las Vegas, 24-16 at Buffalo, 30-23 at Seattle, and 23-17 to Denver on Sunday.

Justin Herbert went 14 of 24 for 121 yards with a touchdown and an interception against the Broncos, was sacked five times and lost a fumble, and has now turned the ball over nine times in five games. Jim Harbaugh, at his own Monday press conference, said he remains determined to turn the season around and described the team as shoulder to shoulder in the fight together, which is what a coach says at 0-5 and occasionally what one means.

Los Angeles is hurt as well. The feed lists receivers Brenen Thompson and Quentin Johnston, cornerback Rodney Shelley and cornerback Donte Jackson as questionable, with receiver KeAndre Lambert-Smith on injured reserve.

DraftKings lists Kansas City at minus-8.5 with the total at 42.5, and the money line at minus-500 for the Chiefs and plus-380 for the Chargers. ESPN's Matchup Predictor gives Kansas City 74.1 percent and Los Angeles 25.7 percent. Those are ESPN's numbers, not this site's, and they are not a forecast this report is making.

Three things to watch.

**Whether Rice plays.** The bye was supposed to answer this and has not. Wednesday's practice report will be closer to the answer than anything written this week.

**Whether the line settles.** A healthy Simmons would let the Chiefs stop improvising on the side Mahomes cannot see.

**The pass rush, again.** Herbert was sacked five times on Sunday. If Kansas City cannot get home against a winless team at Arrowhead, the problem is not the opponent.

## What to watch beyond Sunday

Kansas City has thirteen games left and no more byes. The Chargers are the first of them.

The trade deadline is November 10. The Chiefs have now shown their hand on the receiver room, and it was not the expensive play. Whether a practice-squad receiver and a linebacker are enough to carry a thirteen-game run is a question the next month answers.

The markets spent the bye marking time. Kalshi prices Kansas City at 9.5 percent to win the Super Bowl, up a point over eight days, and 17.5 percent to win the AFC, also up a point. Its AFC West price is 65 percent, up two, and its playoff qualifier is 90.5 percent, up two. The No. 1 seed market is the exception: it sits at 34 percent, down four points against the venue's own previous print on 157 in 24-hour volume, which is a market saying the top seed is a race rather than a formality.
