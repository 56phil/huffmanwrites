**Question:** What does 5-0 look like on a day the quarterback throws for 272 yards and no touchdowns?

**Answer:** Like 22-6, a hundredth win, four sacks, and an offensive line that will decide what comes next.

Kansas City beat the Los Angeles Chargers 22-6 at Arrowhead Stadium on Sunday and moved to 5-0, the only unbeaten team in the AFC. Patrick Mahomes won his hundredth regular-season game in his 131st start, matching Tom Brady for the fewest starts needed to reach 100 wins in the NFL since 1950. Brady finished with 251 wins and seven championships. The comparison is about pace, not destiny.

ESPN's recap of the afternoon opened on a different kind of symmetry. Mahomes had not watched the tape of last December's game against these same Chargers, the one in which he tore ligaments in his left knee and Kansas City was eliminated from the postseason, until this week. Whatever that game cost him, this one asked for something else: 30 attempts, no touchdown passes, and a run game that carried the offense.

## The game

Kansas City led 9-3 after the first quarter and 19-3 at halftime, and the shape of the afternoon was already set. Harrison Butker opened with a 44-yard field goal. Kenneth Walker III went 33 yards for the game's first touchdown, and Butker missed the extra point. Cameron Dicker answered with a 23-yard field goal, Walker scored again from two yards out, and Butker closed the half with a 50-yard kick. Dicker's 30-yarder in the third quarter and Butker's 30-yarder in the fourth finished the scoring.

Walker's line was the game: 19 carries, 128 yards, two touchdowns, a long of 33. "The first guy never tackles him," Mahomes said afterward, in the [Chiefs 22-6 Chargers game recap](http://www.espn.com/nfl/recap?gameId=401873006). It is the best description of what Walker has given this offense while the receivers heal.

Mahomes finished 23 of 30 for 272 yards with no touchdowns and one interception, and was not sacked. Xavier Worthy caught seven passes for 73 yards, Travis Kelce four for 53, Noah Gray three for 53, and rookie Cyrus Allen two for 27. Cam Hart intercepted Mahomes, and Nikko Remigio lost a fumble.

The yardage says the offense moved: 422 total, 7.4 a play, 23 first downs, 150 on the ground at 5.6 a carry. The red zone says it stalled. Kansas City converted one of four trips inside the 20, went 3 of 10 on third down, and committed eight penalties for 89 yards. Those are the numbers that kept a 422-yard afternoon from becoming a rout, and they are the same numbers that have followed this team all season.

Butker made three of four field goals, including the 50-yarder, and one of two extra points. He is 10 of 13 on the season.

The defense did the rest. Los Angeles gained 259 yards, 4.1 a play, went 5 for 16 on third down, 0 for 3 in the red zone and 0 for 3 on fourth down, and punted on the possessions that mattered. Justin Herbert went 22 of 37 for 204 yards with an interception, was sacked four times, and finished with a 48.5 QBR. Nick Bolton made 11 tackles, Drue Tranquill seven, and Jack Cochrane intercepted Herbert and returned it 48 yards. George Karlaftis had six tackles, a sack, two tackles for loss, three quarterback hits and two passes defensed.

The sacks are the story inside the story. Kansas City had not brought a quarterback down since the opener. Ashton Gillotte ended that late in the third quarter, and the Chiefs finished with four, [as ESPN's Week 6 takeaways from Sunday's games noted](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50181469/week-6-sunday-games-takeaways-questions-2026). For a team whose defensive question was whether pressure could ever become sacks, four in one afternoon is an answer, if a late one.

The Chargers fell to 0-6, their worst start since 2000. They were the opponent on the day Kansas City made a little history, and they looked like it.

## The state of the team

At 5-0 the Chiefs hold the AFC's first seed, are 3-0 in the division and 5-0 in the conference, with a point differential of plus-57. San Francisco at 6-0 and Minnesota at 5-0 are the league's other unbeatens. Buffalo is 5-1 and second in the AFC, one loss behind with a game more played. Denver has won two straight and sits sixth at 4-2. The division is not a formality, and neither is the conference.

The offense averages 28.0 points and 421.6 yards a game, 261.8 through the air and 159.8 on the ground at 5.5 a carry. The running game is not a complement right now. It is the thing that makes the passing game work while Rashee Rice and Tyquan Thornton are unavailable.

The defense has eight sacks, five interceptions and a turnover margin of plus-five, having lost one fumble all season. Third down is 25 of 60. Penalties are 39 for 344 yards, nearly 69 a game. The red-zone numbers are the ones to watch: 19 scores in 23 trips, but only 13 touchdowns. This offense moves the ball and settles for field goals, which is survivable against a winless team and less so against a good one.

ESPN's Week 7 power rankings hold Kansas City at No. 2 and name "Offensive tackle health" as the biggest offensive issue. That is the correct diagnosis.

## Injuries and personnel

**Jaylon Moore.** The starting right tackle suffered a concussion against the Chargers and must be cleared by an independent neurologist. ESPN's injury report listed him as questionable on October 18.

**Josh Simmons.** The left tackle has not played this season because of a bulging disc in his back. He practiced on a limited basis all three days before the Chargers game, his first work since August 16, and is listed as questionable on October 20.

**Rashee Rice.** The hamstring he hurt in Week 4 against Las Vegas kept him out of every practice last week and out of Sunday's game. He is listed as questionable on October 20, and Reid sounded more hopeful about him than about Simmons, calling him "a pretty fast healer" [in ESPN's report on the injury](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50197599/chiefs-wr-rashee-rice-vs-chargers-hamstring-injury).

**The line.** Andy Reid was careful Monday, saying Simmons is "still probably going to be ramping up as we go," and would not commit to him playing in Seattle. If neither tackle can go, Kahlil Benson would start on the left and Joshua Ezeudu on the right. Ezeudu allowed one hurry on 17 pass-blocking snaps against the Chargers, and Reid said, "I thought he did a nice job there." Both comments are [in A to Z Sports' Week 7 injury update on Simmons, Moore and Rice](https://atozsports.com/nfl/kansas-city-chiefs-news/chiefs-latest-injury-update-josh-simmons-jaylon-moore-rashee-rice-week-7-seahawks/).

**Also.** Tyquan Thornton and Cooper McDonald remain on injured reserve. Travis Kelce tweaked an ankle Sunday and returned. Chris Roland-Wallace was ejected in the fourth quarter for punching Chargers tackle Alex Harkey.

## The next game

Kansas City plays at Seattle on Sunday at 7:20 p.m. Central, at Lumen Field, on NBC.

The Seahawks are 3-3 and second in the NFC West, and the record hides the shape of their season. They have scored 148 points and allowed 122. Their three losses came by two, three and three points, eight in total: 33-31 at Washington, 32-29 to San Francisco, and 17-14 in overtime at Denver. Their wins were 13-10 over New England, 31-7 at Arizona and 30-23 over the Chargers.

The problem is turnovers. Seattle has 12, third-most in the league. Sam Darnold has thrown seven interceptions in four games since returning from a glute injury, and the Seahawks have lost five fumbles. Ten of those turnovers came in the three losses, and opponents scored 44 points off them. A team that loses by a field goal and gives the ball away three times is a team that beats itself.

Seattle's pass rush is another matter. It ranks eighth in sacks, behind Minnesota, Cincinnati, Chicago, Houston, Denver, Cleveland and Detroit, [per A to Z Sports' Week 7 injury update](https://atozsports.com/nfl/kansas-city-chiefs-news/chiefs-latest-injury-update-josh-simmons-jaylon-moore-rashee-rice-week-7-seahawks/). Against a line starting Benson and Ezeudu, that is the matchup that decides the game.

DraftKings lists Seattle at minus-3 with the total at 42.5, and the money lines at plus-130 for Kansas City and minus-155 for Seattle. ESPN's Matchup Predictor gives Kansas City 52.8 percent and Seattle 46.8 percent. Those are the feed's numbers and not this site's, and it is worth noticing that the book and the model disagree about which team is better.

Three things to watch.

**The tackles.** Whether Moore clears the protocol and whether Simmons can play are two different questions with the same answer if both are no.

**Rice.** The receivers who carried Sunday are not the receivers around whom the offense was built.

**The ball.** Kansas City has lost one fumble all season and is plus-five in turnover margin. Seattle has given the ball away 12 times, third-most in the league, and that is the surest way for a 5-0 team to lose a game it should win.

## What to watch beyond Sunday

Twelve games remain, and no more byes. The next month is a referendum on the offensive line, the receiver room and the kicking game, which is an odd list for a 5-0 team and an honest one.

The AFC race is tighter than the record suggests. Buffalo is 5-1. Denver has won two straight. Kansas City's lead for the first seed is real but not comfortable, and the market reflects it.

Kalshi prices Kansas City at 10.5 percent to win the Super Bowl, 18 percent to win the AFC, 65.5 percent to win the AFC West and 91 percent to make the playoffs. Over the eight days since the last report, the Super Bowl price is up a point, the conference price up half a point, the division price down a point, and the playoff price unchanged.

The division price is the interesting one. It fell a point while Kansas City won, a market reading of Denver's two straight wins as much as anything the Chiefs did. The No. 1 seed market sits at 30 percent on 1,262 in 24-hour volume, down a cent against Kalshi's own previous print. The market thinks the West is close and the top seed is a race.

Polymarket reads it similarly: 11 percent for the Super Bowl, 18 percent for the conference, 66 percent for the division, 91 percent to make the playoffs, and 10 percent for Mahomes as MVP.

Kalshi's win-total ladder now has ten or more wins at 94 percent, eleven at 78, twelve at 64, thirteen at 47 and fourteen at 26. The fifty-percent rung still sits between twelve and thirteen, so the market's expected total is a little better than twelve. With twelve games left, twelve wins means 7-5 from here. That is the standard the rest of the season has to meet.
