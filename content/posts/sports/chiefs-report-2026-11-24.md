**Question:** Kansas City has won three straight games by at least 21 points, scored 34 or more in each of them, and watched Patrick Mahomes throw 13 touchdown passes without an interception since Week 8. Is this the Chiefs' best football arriving in time, or a schedule flattering them?

**Answer:** Some of both, and Thursday night in Buffalo will separate them. The offense has changed shape, the defense has been the league's stingiest outside Minnesota, and none of it has yet been tested by a team that can win the AFC. The fifth seed Kansas City holds is the price of a division Denver has not surrendered.

Sunday was the third of those wins, 34-7 over Arizona, and the third in a row by at least 21 points, which the club calls its longest such run since 1997.

## The game

The first quarter decided it. Kansas City ran 27 plays for 152 yards; Arizona ran four for 13. The Chiefs led 14-0 after one and 27-7 at halftime, then did not score in the third quarter because they did not need to. Xavier Worthy's 18-yard touchdown on the first play of the fourth ended a 97-yard drive and made it 34-7.

Mahomes finished 27 of 39 for 324 yards, three touchdowns, no interceptions, a 120.0 rating and a 64.9 QBR, sacked once, and he played just over three quarters. Arizona's only points came on a 2-yard pass from Josh Johnson to Simi Fehoko in the second quarter.

Kelce caught six passes for 74 yards and two of the touchdowns, and between them the two catches rearranged two record books. The first, an 8-yarder on the opening drive, moved him past Rob Gronkowski into third place all-time among tight ends for touchdowns including the playoffs, behind Antonio Gates (118) and Tony Gonzalez (115). The second, a 22-yarder before halftime, was his 90th regular-season touchdown catch, which moved him past Jimmy Graham for fourth among tight ends. He is 37, and he became the fourth player in league history to score twice in a half at that age or older, joining Jim Thorpe, Steve Young and Jerry Rice. Sunday was also his 100th regular-season game at Arrowhead Stadium, the fourth most in franchise history.

> "That's good company right there."

— Travis Kelce, [Kelce passes Gronkowski for most TDs by TE, including playoffs](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/50563590/kelce-passes-gronkowski-most-tds-te-including-playoffs), ESPN, November 22, 2026

Emmett Johnson carried 23 times for 105 yards and a touchdown, his second straight 100-yard game and his fourth consecutive game with a score, and added four catches; he finished with 143 yards from scrimmage. He is a rookie who began the season behind Kenneth Walker III.

The defense did what it has done most of the year. Arizona finished with 205 yards, 3.7 a play, and 72 of those came on its final possession, which ended on downs. Josh Johnson completed 18 of 31 for 155 yards with one touchdown and took three sacks. The Chiefs forced no turnovers and committed none.

Harrison Butker made both field goals, from 40 and 44 yards, and all four extra points. Kansas City took two penalties for 15 yards, went 7 of 13 on third down and scored three times in five red-zone trips, the afternoon's one soft spot.

## The state of the team

At 8-2 the Chiefs are level with Denver, and the feed seeds the Broncos second in the AFC and Kansas City fifth, behind two 7-3 division leaders in Jacksonville and Cincinnati. That is the season's central oddity: Kansas City's point differential is plus-108, Denver's plus-26, and the Broncos are still listed first in the West. Las Vegas is 4-7 and the Chargers 1-9, so the division is a two-team race with seven games left.

The offense averages 27.1 points and 405.1 yards a game, 270.9 through the air and 134.2 on the ground at 4.9 a carry. The rushing rank is the number that changed. Buffalo's defensive staff puts Kansas City sixth in total rushing yards and second in yards per rush, after the Chiefs finished last season 25th and 20th in those categories and 28th in explosive rushes. The defense allows 16.3 points a game, second in the league behind Minnesota's 16.1, and has held six opponents under 14. Third down is 41.8 percent; the red zone is 33 scores in 38 trips, with touchdowns on about two-thirds.

The turnover margin is plus-six, on 13 takeaways and seven giveaways. The pass rush has 20 sacks in 10 games, and George Karlaftis has seven in his last four, the most in the league over that span, after six all of last season; he had 2.5 Sunday.

The kicker remains the unresolved number. Butker is 14 of 21 on field goals, 66.7 percent, and two of eight from 50 yards or longer. The last three weeks have not tested him, which is not the same as answering the question.

Eight-and-two is also the sixth time in 14 seasons under Andy Reid that Kansas City has been 8-2 or better after 10 games. The club notes it did that six times in the 53 seasons before he arrived.

Mahomes was already past Sunday. Asked about the rest of the season, he said that ["it all begins Thursday night."](https://www.espn.com/nfl/recap?gameId=401873067)

## Injuries and personnel

The backfield is the week's live question. Walker practiced on a limited basis Tuesday, his first work since the groin injury, and Reid was careful about it: ["Let's see how he does today,"](https://sports.yahoo.com/nfl/articles/chiefs-bills-week-12-tuesday-214129890.html) he said, adding that Walker was "feeling pretty good." The Chiefs have little reason to push him. Johnson has 491 yards from scrimmage in his last four games, fourth in the league in that span, and consecutive 100-yard rushing games.

Trey Smith, back from the concussion protocol, was a full participant Monday and Tuesday, and Reid said he ["will probably be in."](https://atozsports.com/nfl/kansas-city-chiefs-news/chiefs-second-injury-report-week-12-bills-tuesday/) Jaylon Moore did not practice either day with a high ankle sprain, and Joshua Ezeudu has played well enough at right tackle that the Chiefs reportedly feel no urgency to rush Moore back. Ben VanSumeren went on injured reserve Tuesday with a knee sprain and must miss at least four games. Omarr Norman-Lott (knee) was limited; Noah Gray (rib) was full.

Mahomes was a full participant both days, with a knee and his right elbow listed. That is the most important line on the report and the quietest. Two weeks ago the elbow was the story of the season; this week it is a line on a practice report.

No game-status designations had been issued as of Tuesday evening. They were due Wednesday, and clubs can add to the report through kickoff.

For Buffalo, Dalton Kincaid (thumb and ankle) and Christian Benford (groin) were limited Tuesday. The feed lists both as questionable.

## The next game

Kansas City visits Buffalo on Thursday, November 26, at 7:20 p.m. Central (CST), at Highmark Stadium, on NBC. It is the Bills' first home Thanksgiving game, and it may be the best regular-season game left on the schedule.

Buffalo is 9-1, winners of six straight and the AFC's only one-loss team. The last five: 33-30 at Las Vegas, 36-10 over Baltimore, 23-6 at Minnesota, 31-12 at the Jets, 26-9 over Miami. DraftKings lists the Bills at minus-3 with the total at 47.5. ESPN's Matchup Predictor gives Buffalo 53.1 percent and Kansas City 46.6; those are the feed's numbers, not this site's.

What makes the game the week's real test is that Buffalo's defense has become good in a way nobody can yet measure. Ben Solak of ESPN traced it: entering its Week 7 bye, Buffalo ranked 31st in defensive EPA per drive, 30th in defensive success rate and worst in points allowed per drive. Jim Leonhard changed the coverage, and since Week 8 the Bills have not allowed a touchdown drive in three straight games, 43 consecutive drives, a franchise first. The quarterbacks in that stretch were Kyle McCord, Cade Klubnik, Carson Wentz, Joe Fagnano, Geno Smith, Max Brosmer, Tyler Huntley and, for a single dropback, Lamar Jackson. Solak's conclusion is that the unit could be 12th or 25th and that a full game from Mahomes is the measurement.

Three things to watch.

**Whether the new offense travels.** Solak's schematic note is the best thing written about the Chiefs this week. Through Week 7 Kansas City was fifth in pass rate over expectation at plus-1.5 percent; since Week 8 it is second at plus-6.2, behind only Cincinnati. Mahomes has averaged more than eight air yards an attempt in consecutive games for the first time since Weeks 2 and 3 of last season. On in-structure throws of four seconds or less he averaged 8.1 air yards an attempt over the last two weeks, against 5.1 over the previous nine, the lowest among starting quarterbacks.

Leonhard, meanwhile, described a running team. ["Kansas City is running the hell out of the football right now,"](https://www.buffalobills.com/news/how-special-buffalo-is-bills-anticipate-special-home-thanksgiving-game-detail-keys-to-facing-red-hot-chiefs) he said. "They're extremely physical." Both descriptions can be true: the season-long identity is the run, and the last two weeks are the pass. Thursday says which one the Chiefs believe.

**Allen's legs.** Josh Allen had no rushing attempts against Miami while playing the entire game, the first time in his career that has happened, and Steve Spagnuolo [called it "a setup."](https://www.buffalobills.com/news/how-special-buffalo-is-bills-anticipate-special-home-thanksgiving-game-detail-keys-to-facing-red-hot-chiefs) Since a 121-yard game against the Rams in Week 5, Allen has 51 rushing yards, and he has run for at least 30 in five of his last six regular-season meetings with Kansas City. The Chiefs' defensive plan starts there.

**The right side of the line.** Moore is out, Smith is back, and Buffalo's pass rush is the reason its defense turned. Greg Rousseau is the name Solak cites as a breakout; the protection in front of Mahomes is the matchup inside the matchup.

## What to watch beyond Sunday

The schedule is the argument. After Buffalo the Chiefs go to the Rams on December 3, to Cincinnati on December 13, home to New England on December 21 and San Francisco on December 27, then to the Chargers and home to the Raiders. Four of the last seven are on the road, and Solak names the three-week run of Buffalo, the Rams and Cincinnati as the test of whether the offensive change is real.

The seeding math is simple enough. A win Thursday pulls Kansas City level with Buffalo at 9-2 with the head-to-head in hand. A loss leaves the Chiefs 8-3 and still chasing a division they do not control.

The market reads the season the same way the standings do. Kalshi prices Kansas City at 8.5 percent to win the Super Bowl, 16.5 percent to win the AFC, 43.5 percent to win the AFC West, 98.5 percent to reach the playoffs, 14 percent for the AFC's top seed and 8 percent for Mahomes as MVP.

Over the last eight days the Super Bowl price is up a point, the conference price up two and the playoff price up two. The division price is down three in a week the Chiefs won by 27, because Denver won too. That is the market's summary: the wild card is nearly settled, the division is not, and the championship is still a long shot.
