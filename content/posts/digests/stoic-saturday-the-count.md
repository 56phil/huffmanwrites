"That which is not good for the swarm, neither is it good for the bee." — Marcus Aurelius, [*Meditations* 6.54](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_VI) (trans. George Long, 1862)

**The reflection**

Three counts moved this week, and each was a place where a country had agreed in advance to be bound by an arithmetic it had not yet seen. Brazil voted on Sunday, and Lula finished with 51.8 percent against 48.2, a lead of nearly four and a half million votes. By Monday morning Flávio Bolsonaro had refused to concede and asked for the result to be annulled. Israelis voted on Tuesday, and their count stopped short: with more than 99 percent of the vote in, the bloc around Benjamin Netanyahu held 53 seats and the bloc around Gadi Eisenkot 48, against the 61 a Knesset majority requires. On Wednesday evening in California, a federal judge refused the Justice Department's request to set aside the ballots of 20,000 people the department calls likely noncitizens. A runoff, a deadlock, and a courthouse: the same question in three shapes, which is what a count answers.

A count is not a poll with a deadline. It is a promise made in ignorance, and the ignorance is the whole of its virtue. Before the votes are in, the loser agrees that the number will settle the matter, having no idea yet whether the number favors him. That promise is why a count is the one political act in which a person can lose without being defeated: the outcome is taken out of the argument on purpose, by everyone, beforehand. Judge Carter's order says the same thing in the language of a court. "Once a vote is segregated, lasting harm has occurred," he wrote, and he was writing about ballot envelopes, about pulling a set of votes out of the pile and holding them apart until someone decides what they are worth. The harm is not only that ballots might be lost. It is the premise, which is that the vote of one person may be held separate from the count of everyone. Segregate a ballot and you have said that the whole is not the unit.

Marcus Aurelius denies that premise in a sentence short enough to be mistaken for a proverb: "That which is not good for the swarm, neither is it good for the bee." He is not asking the bee to sacrifice itself for the swarm. He is saying that the bee is not a separate thing that happens to live near other bees, and that there is no point of view from which a bee can calculate its own good in isolation and get an answer. Put a ballot in that sentence and the arithmetic becomes legible. A vote is not a private opinion that gets tallied; it is a share in a common thing, and its meaning is the count it joins. This is why the refusal in Brazil costs so little today and so much later. Bolsonaro's objection changed no ballot. It withdrew the agreement, and the agreement was the only thing that had made the number mean anything.

I do not want to be sentimental about institutions, which fail, and Israel's arithmetic this week is a clean example of failure by design. Two blocs that together hold 101 of 120 seats, neither able to reach 61, with the parties that hold the balance ruled out as partners by both sides. Nobody stole that result. It is what the voters produced, and the honest response to a deadlock is a deadlock, then whatever bargaining or second election follows. The Stoic claim is narrower than optimism, and it survives a week like this one. It is that the only part of the count which belongs to me is my own act: the vote I cast, and the agreement I gave in advance to accept what the count says. Four days from an election here, both are still available. Neither of them is a feeling.

**Saturday, the raids.** [SITREP: October 24](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-24/) records Israel sealing Hebron late on Friday and, by Saturday, raiding at least fifteen towns in the northern West Bank, leaving at least 200 families outside their homes and arresting dozens of Palestinians, three days before a national election.

**Sunday, Brazil votes.** [SITREP: October 25](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-25/) puts nearly 160 million citizens eligible in a runoff between Lula, 80, seeking a fourth non-consecutive term, and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, whose father is serving a 27-year sentence for attempting a coup after the last election. Voting was extended by two hours after buses were pulled from circulation in several cities.

**Monday, the refusal.** Lula took 51.8 percent to 48.2, and abstention fell from 21 percent to 19. Flávio Bolsonaro did not wait for the count to finish before rejecting it. ["It is impossible to recognise the results. I am going to request the annulment of these elections,"](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/26/brazils-lula-clinches-election-victory-as-bolsonaro-rejects-results) he said in Brasília, calling the extension a dirty trick. [SITREP: October 26](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-26/) records the same morning and the same refusal.

**The map, nine days out.** The [Senate Race Report of October 25](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/senate-race-report-2026-10-25/) finds Republicans spending $26 million to hold Kansas, suing to block certification in Alaska, and retreating from North Carolina, while four rating changes in seven days moved three of them toward the Democrats. Defense is not defeat, which is why control remains a coin flip.

**A wall made of arithmetic.** [The 2027 Wall](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/investing/the-2027-wall/) reads the AI buildout as a sum that has to close: Nvidia's own guidance implies roughly $650 billion of revenue in calendar 2027 against hyperscaler capital spending of $1.3 trillion, and the essay's base case puts the first crack in the second quarter of 2027. It is the week's other kind of count, the kind no one agrees to in advance.

**Tuesday, Israelis vote.** [SITREP: October 27](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-27/) reports the first general election since the October 7 attacks, 7.3 million people eligible, and the final surveys level between Netanyahu, 77, and the centrist challenger Gadi Eisenkot.

**Wednesday, three documents and no winner.** [SITREP: October 28](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-28/) carries three presidential documents into the Federal Register, on housing costs, on petroleum under the Defense Production Act, and on wind turbines under section 232, none of which imposes a tariff, each of which hands a directive to an agency. The same edition reads the Israeli count at 54 seats to 50. Al Jazeera's [running tally](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/28/netanyahu-or-eisenkot-can-you-form-israels-next-coalition-government), updated as the soldiers' and diplomats' ballots came in, has it at 53 to 48, with Likud the largest single party at 26 and official results due November 4.

**Wednesday evening, the ballots.** Judge David Carter, in a 26-page order, denied the Justice Department's emergency request to make California stop processing ballots belonging to 20,000 people the department calls likely noncitizens. "Never before in American history has the federal government, on the eve of an election, sought to coerce a state and local government to abandon their own Constitutional elections procedures and bend to the will of the Executive Branch," he wrote, and at the close: "Once a vote is segregated, lasting harm has occurred. If the Executive can discount votes and threaten an ongoing election due to unproven allegations, then our democracy is in danger. This Court declines to be a party to such process." The department [filed notice of appeal](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/28/nx-s1-6021478/doj-california-ballots-denied) the same night and said it would seek emergency relief from the Ninth Circuit.

**Thursday, five rules and a crash.** [SITREP: October 29](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-29/) reports five deregulatory actions from four agencies crossing the Federal Register in a single morning, the largest of them a final rule removing requirements from child support enforcement. It also reports the crash near Urda in Castilla-La Mancha, where a Renfe high-speed train struck a standing Iryo train and 68 people died, Spain's second rail disaster this year.

**The sheet that is not the paper.** [The Unstated Sheet](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-unstated-sheet/) takes the label on an American ream and finds two numbers that describe neither the paper nor its use, which is a small lesson in what a stated figure can be made to hide.

**The ledger.** [The Credibility Ledger](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-credibility-ledger/) reads Judge Reyes's findings in the East Potomac golf case, where the government's account of the course could not be credited, and where the sharpest finding is arithmetic rather than temper: a declaration signed at 5:15 p.m. and the tree plan omitted twenty-four minutes later. The government was due back in her courtroom at 10:00 this morning.

**Two Senecas.** [Two Things Called Seneca](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/two-things-called-seneca/) separates a Roman Stoic dead for nineteen centuries from a federally registered super PAC raising money for the midterms. They share a word and nothing else, and the piece is about what a name is worth once it can be bought.

**The apology.** [An Apology to the Seventy Million](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/weekly-satire-2026-10-26/), the Weekly Satire of October 26, confesses to having doubted the children's investment accounts, and then lets the record keep talking.

**Friday, the duty and the corridor.** [SITREP: October 30](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-30/) records that at 12:01 a.m. Eastern the list of Canadian goods paying a 50 percent duty changed, by proclamation rather than by legislation. Overnight, under a ceasefire in Kherson that has held since Tuesday, [hundreds of residents left Russian-occupied Oleshky](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c60me298k1yeo) in a convoy of 25 buses with a four-hour window; several hundred stayed. The truce runs to the end of Sunday, and the Kremlin has said its success should not be read as a willingness to extend it anywhere else.

**The weekly reports**

The **Senate Race Report** published its October 25 edition on Sunday, nine days before Election Day, and its subject is a party playing defense without conceding: $26 million committed to Kansas, a lawsuit seeking to block certification in Alaska if the Republican incumbent loses to a similar name on the ballot, a retreat in North Carolina, and four rating changes in seven days with three of them toward the Democrats. Sabato's Crystal Ball and Silver Bulletin still disagree about which five states decide the majority. The final edition of the season comes at 07:00 Sunday.

The **Docket Report** published its October 24 edition on Saturday, on a comparison withdrawn. The Justice Department conceded that three sentences in its motion to lift the Kennedy Center injunction "mistakenly describe" a JLL plan, that the comparison of a two-year closure against a four-year phased renovation came from a version of the report the board never received, and that the report the board had was 160 pages rather than 180. Beatty's reply accused the government of withholding that report until October 19. The next edition publishes at 08:00 this morning.

The **Chiefs Report** published its October 27 edition on Tuesday, on the first loss of the season. Kansas City lost 20-3 at Seattle, ran 50 plays for 213 yards, and never reached the red zone for the first time in any start of Patrick Mahomes' career; Josh Simmons' move to injured reserve leaves the line unsettled until Week 11. The next edition comes Tuesday.

The **Bond Market's Next Ninety Days** and **The S&P 500's Next Ninety Days** last published on October 1, both closing the same window at December 30. The next installments come tomorrow, at the start of November.

The **Global SITREP** publishes every morning, and the editions of October 24 through 30 are linked above.

The **Repair Plan** is a quarterly working document for a future administration rather than a letter to you.

**The Practice**

Before Tuesday, write down the outcome you least want, in one sentence, and then write what you will do the morning after if it happens: the call you will make, the work you will take up, the neighbor you will help. Keep both where you will see them. Then do one thing this week that does not depend on the result at all, because the point of the exercise is to find out how much of your week was already hostage to a number. When the count comes in, read it before you describe it. Not the exit polls, not the percentage with three percent reporting, not the clip of a man saying the fix was in: the count, from the office that counts. Marcus Aurelius governed through a plague, a frontier war, and a rebellion, and wrote nothing in twelve books about how the provinces ought to have voted. He wrote about what a man owes a swarm from which he cannot separate himself. The count is where that debt gets a number, and the agreement to accept the number is the only part of it you can perform in advance.

See you next Saturday. — Phil
