"Nothing for me is too early nor too late, which is in due time for thee." — Marcus Aurelius, [*Meditations* 4.23](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_IV) (trans. George Long, 1862)

**The reflection**

At 3:03 on the morning of November 4, the last election-night update arrived from Pike County, Mississippi, and Scott Colom led Cindy Hyde-Smith by forty-three votes. On Friday evening the same race stood at 2,574 votes apart, out of 953,314 counted, and had not moved in two days. On Wednesday afternoon, when Jackson County reported 2,173 more votes, the Associated Press called it at 4,669 ahead of 962,237 counted, half a percentage point, and the senator conceded that evening. Three numbers describing one election. Only the last one is the count, and the distance between them is not error. It is envelopes nobody had opened yet.

The whole week had that shape. A federal judge set a hearing for ten o'clock this morning, two days before the park it concerns is scheduled to close. A circuit clerk set an appeal to be briefed from December 15 to February 25, so a question argued in September will be answered by a panel sometime next year. Alaska will not run the ranked tabulation that decides its Senate seat until November 18, and will not certify until the 25th. Two days of talks in Abu Dhabi ended with a prisoner exchange and no ceasefire. Every one of those is an interval, and in almost every case the interval is where the actual news is, which is why it goes unreported.

Marcus wrote the line above to a universe he did not schedule. It is worth being exact about what it does not say. It does not say outcomes are indifferent, and it does not make waiting a virtue, because waiting is not a virtue; a man can wait out a wrong as patiently as he waits out a right. What the sentence claims is narrower and harder: the timing is not his to set, so the only question left is what he does while it runs. Read that way, the vices of this week sort themselves. The campaign that calls a count corrupt because it is losing it, the official who treats a filing deadline as a ruling, the commentator who calls a race at forty-three votes: each is the same mistake in different dress, which is a verdict asserted before the process delivered one. Not impatience as a mood. Prematurity as a claim.

The discipline the interval asks for is not folded hands. In a county clerk's office it looks like affidavit ballots verified one at a time, twelve thousand of them, with a rule about identification that no one will ever be thanked for applying. In a courtroom it looks like a plaintiff asking for fourteen days instead of a verdict, and a judge who grants a hearing rather than an opinion. In Alaska it looks like a method the voters were asked to repeal and kept, doing unglamorous arithmetic on a schedule published in advance. This is the part of Stoicism that reads least like consolation and most like procedure: the work is the part within your control, and almost always that part is smaller and duller than the thing that worries you.

There is one hour this week that was fixed in advance, and the country still keeps it. The armistice took effect at eleven o'clock on the morning of November 11, 1918, and the date was moved to a Monday in 1971 and moved back by statute in 1975, because a long weekend is a convenience and a date is a claim. Almost nothing else in a life ends at a stated hour. The endings that matter arrive unannounced, in a county update at 3:03 in the morning, in a physician's sentence, in a letter that does not come. The eleventh hour is precious precisely because it is the exception: it is what it looks like when the waiting has a term.

So this is not an argument for delay, and it is not a counsel of doubt. Peltola leads the Alaska first choices 49.0 percent to 47.2, the tabulation is nine days away, and the seat will in fact be decided; no honest person calls that unknowable. The distinction the week keeps teaching is between *not yet* and *never*, which are different words doing different work. A count that has not finished is not a count in dispute, an appeal that has not been heard is not a conviction reversed, a hearing not yet held is not a ruling. Most of the disorder of public life now comes from collapsing that distinction, usually in whichever direction flatters the person speaking.

The Stoic use of a week like this one is to practice the smaller interval at home. You have things that are unfinished, and you have been calling them early or calling them late, when the honest word is *not yet*. Name one. Give it a date. Then keep the appointment and decline to grade the result before the date arrives. That is the whole discipline, and it is not passive; it is the only way anything gets counted.

**Saturday, the mandate.** Israel's president gave the task of forming a government to the opposition leader, Gadi Eisenkot, though Likud remains the largest party with 27 seats and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc finished on 53 of the Knesset's 120. The consultations decided it: 67 members backed Eisenkot against 46 for Netanyahu, and neither bloc was near the 61 a majority requires. The tasking is an instruction to try, not a government, and the coalition that follows is the open question.

**Monday, the stay.** The Supreme Court stayed a district judge's injunction against the Big Bend border barrier in *Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church*, No. 26A560, writing per curiam that the government was "exceedingly likely to prevail" because the plaintiffs' major-questions argument was statutory rather than constitutional, and the immigration statute's review bar therefore left the district court without jurisdiction. The Court's point was that a constitutional major-questions claim does not exist, whatever a litigant calls it.

**Wednesday, the call.** Nine days after the polls closed, the Associated Press called Mississippi for Scott Colom. He is 43, a local prosecutor, and the first Black United States senator from that state since Reconstruction. The call gives Democrats 55 Senate seats, a net gain of eight, their largest midterm gain in that chamber since 1986; they last held 55 in 2014. Fewer than 12,000 ballots remained for counties to review before Friday's certification deadline, and Mississippi has no recount law, so a challenge would have needed a court order rather than a request.

**Today, the hearing.** The plaintiffs in the East Potomac case asked Judge Ana Reyes for a fourteen-day pause on the closure of the park, and she hears the motion at ten o'clock this morning in Courtroom 12, two days before the November 15 closure date. The week's filings are narrower than the questions that produced them: the government says it consulted the White House Counsel and reasonably believes no contract exists with Tom Fazio, Gavin Fazio or Fazio Design; it says the date on which the plan entered the planning commission's submission is privileged; and its reply argues that the decision became final "well before October 15" while the submission describing it was not final until October 30. The [Docket Report of November 7](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/docket-report-2026-11-07/) sets out all forty-four entries and the two amicus briefs.

**Wednesday, the tabulation to come.** Alaska is the last Senate race uncalled, and its ranked-choice tabulation begins November 18. The method is the one the voters kept on November 3, rejecting a repeal 52.8 to 47.2 with 84 percent counted, a wider margin than the 743 votes by which they kept it in 2024. [What Missouri Locked, What Alaska Kept](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/what-missouri-locked-what-alaska-kept/) reads the two verdicts together: Missouri rejected its legislature's map 61.8 to 38.2 and, on the same ballot, entrenched the veto that killed it at 59.9 percent, with an eighty percent wall behind it.

**The talks.** The first trilateral round on the Russia-Ukraine war since February met in Abu Dhabi and ended without a ceasefire. American envoys then flew to Moscow, where the Kremlin said it had not yet agreed to include European mediators. Overnight, roughly 1,200 drones crossed in both directions, a hospital in central Kyiv burned after a strike at about three in the morning, four people were killed in an industrial zone in Volgograd, and at least two died in Odesa. A prisoner exchange is a real thing and it is not a peace, and the difference between the two is the interval the winter will be spent inside.

**The eleventh.** The [Veterans Day reflection](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-eleventh-hour/) records what the date actually commemorates: the hour the firing stopped, not the victory that followed a year later. Congress moved the observance to a Monday in 1971 and gave it back in 1975, and the reason is worth keeping. A date carries a fact a long weekend cannot. The poem [Where Valor Sleeps](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/where-valor-sleeps/) asks the same debt in verse, and ends where the essay does: honor them not with tears, but with the work of all our years.

**The board.** Equities closed lower on Thursday, with the S&P 500 at 7,592.40, down 0.35 percent, and the ten-year Treasury at 5.137 percent, up 3.4 basis points. The VIX sat at 18.23 this morning, well below the 26.78 it touched on the Thursday after the election, which suggests a market that has stopped pricing the result and started pricing something else. The morning prints put gold at $3,899.10 an ounce and front-month crude at $88.58 a barrel. These are Thursday's closes and Friday's quotes, not verdicts.

**The weekly reports**

The **Docket Report** published its November 7 edition with a calendar in place for all five dockets. The golf case got a hearing two days before the closure, the Kennedy Center got November 19 at 2:00 p.m. on the motion to dissolve its injunction, and the Epstein appeal got a briefing schedule running to final briefs on February 25, 2027. Its finding is that the government answered each question narrowly, and that each narrow answer bought time.

The **Chiefs Report** published November 10 on a 35-12 rout of the Jets that cost more than the score suggested. Patrick Mahomes left the field holding his right arm after a strip-sack, an MRI showed no significant damage, and the team called him day-to-day; Xavier Worthy pulled a hamstring; and by the deadline the front office sent a fourth- and a fifth-round pick to Indianapolis for the slot receiver Josh Downs. Kansas City is 6-2, second in the AFC West behind a Denver team on a five-game run, with nine games left and a kicker who has missed seven field goals.

The **Bond Market's Next Ninety Days** published November 1 and led with its own error, which is the rare thing in a forecast: its range for the ten-year by December 30 was 5.2 to 5.8 percent, and the note closed October at 4.94. The window now runs to January 30, and the ten-year has since climbed to 5.137, which is the range arriving late rather than not at all.

The **S&P 500's Next Ninety Days** published the same morning on the seam in the keystone: a twenty-eighth record on October 6 at 7,818.93, all of it surrendered by October 30, and a trillion dollars of technology value gone in three weeks while earnings grew. The index stands at 7,592.40.

The **Senate Race Report** ended with its November 1 edition, on the Sunday before the vote, and does not continue. The series it spent a season measuring is now the thing it records, and the count it anticipated finished this week.

**The Practice**

Take one unfinished thing in your life that you have been describing as too late or too early. Write it in a single sentence, with no adjectives. Then write the next action that is actually within your control, which will be smaller than the thing itself: the call, the form, the appointment, the envelope. Set a date within the next seven days and put it where you will see it. When the date arrives, do the action and record what happened, without grading the outcome, because the outcome is not due yet. Do this once a week for a month, and you will notice the thing that changes is not the result but the tone of your own voice when you talk about it. That is the interval doing its work, and the work is the only part that was ever yours.

See you next Saturday. — Phil
