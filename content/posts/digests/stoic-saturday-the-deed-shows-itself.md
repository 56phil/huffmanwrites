"There is no occasion to give this notice. It will soon show itself by acts." — Marcus Aurelius, [*Meditations* 11.15](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_XI) (trans. George Long, 1862)

**The answer, and the shells.** On Saturday the president rejected Iran's seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. "I'm rejecting their deal," he said, and called it unacceptable ([BBC News, 2026](#sources)). That looked like an ending. It was not one. Within days Qatar had carried an official American response to Tehran, and the foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, brought it into a cabinet meeting and laid it before the president of Iran ([ABC News, 2026](#sources)). Officials briefed on the exchange describe the two sides as agreeing on the components and differing only on their order ([CGTN, 2026](#sources)). All of that was diplomacy. In the same waterway, the Revolutionary Guard confirmed for the first time that it strikes shipping in the strait every twenty-four hours, and a Kuwait-flagged tanker was set alight on Thursday ([The New York Times, 2026](#sources); [Seatrade Maritime, 2026](#sources)). Washington answered the proposal and sent a third carrier group, with roughly nine thousand sailors and Marines ([Fortune, 2026](#sources)).

**The rate that did not arrive.** On Wednesday the ten-year Treasury yield broke its 2007 peak, touching 5.304 percent and closing at 5.297, its highest close since 2002 ([24/7 Wall St., 2026](#sources)). Fixed income had spent the week pricing two more increases, in October and December. Then the September jobs report did what no forecaster had: payrolls rose by 29,000 against an expected 84,000, the unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2 percent, and the two prior months were revised down by a combined 60,000 ([CNBC, 2026](#sources)). By Friday the market's implied odds of holding rates steady at the October meeting had jumped to 82.8 percent. A bond market that had broken a nineteen-year record on Wednesday had by Friday postponed the thing it had promised itself.

**The grid, hit hardest since spring.** Overnight on Tuesday Russia attacked Ukraine's power system in eleven regions, the largest combined strike since the end of the last heating season. The Trypilska station, which supplies more electricity to Kyiv than any other, was damaged; seven people were killed; and the grid operator imposed emergency cuts in the capital and three surrounding regions for the first time since spring ([BBC News, 2026](#sources); [The Guardian, 2026](#sources)). On Thursday Ukraine answered with a new weapon, using its FP-7 ballistic missile in combat for the first time ([Defense News, 2026](#sources)). The hard winter had been promised for months. The strikes are the part that arrived.

**A hearing about whether to act.** On Wednesday the parliament of Thuringia held the first hearing in any German legislature on a procedure to ban the Alternative for Germany, on an application by the Left. A comedian, Hape Kerkeling, told the members, "I feel that this democracy is in danger" ([tagesschau, 2026](#sources)). On Friday the Greens demanded that one of the three constitutional organs actually file for such a ban ([Mitteldeutsche Zeitung, 2026](#sources)). The party leads the national polls, and the chancellor, Friedrich Merz, used a prize ceremony on Thursday to warn that if Germany failed to hold the line against Moscow, "the Russian war machine will, sooner or later, turn against us and against NATO" ([POLITICO Europe, 2026](#sources)). A debate about whether to begin is not a beginning.

**The release that begins immediately.** Diesel set a record across the European Union this week at €2.24 a litre, against about €1.59 before the war, with twelve member states at all-time highs ([Euronews, 2026](#sources)). In the United States the price fell 14.7 cents, to $6.382 a gallon, down from a record $6.529 the week before ([U.S. Energy Information Administration, 2026](#sources)). On Thursday the Group of Seven agreed to release up to 100 million barrels from emergency reserves, and its statement reads, in its own words, "to begin immediately over four months" ([Euronews, 2026](#sources)). The announcement was the deed that was available. The barrel is the one deferred.

Here is the honest reading of the week. A plan was refused and the conversation continued, which is what serious talks often look like from the outside. A bond market broke a record nineteen years old and then deferred the thing it had priced, because a single number arrived on a Friday. A power grid took its heaviest hit in months, and a missile flew for the first time. A German legislature spent an afternoon deciding whether to decide. And seven governments promised a barrel of oil that will move over a season. Every one of these is a notice. Marcus's line is the one that sorts them: the man who says he has determined to deal fairly has not yet dealt fairly, and the thing itself will soon show by acts.

The thread is not that promises are lies. Some of these will be kept. The oil will move, the case will be argued, and the talks may yet succeed. The thread is the distance between the announcement and the act, and how much of a life is spent on the near side of it. It is easier to describe the good man than to be one, and easier still to describe the plan than to execute it. The strain shows up closest to home, in the thing you have told someone you will do, which waits on a date you set yourself.

**The Practice**

This week, name one thing you have put on a date. Not the smallest thing, and not the largest either. Then ask a single question about it: is the date a deadline the world imposed, or one you chose to keep from deciding? For anything in the second group, take the first twenty minutes of it today, without waiting for the date and without telling anyone that you have. The notice is the part that costs nothing, which is why it so often stands in for the work.

See you next Saturday. — Phil
