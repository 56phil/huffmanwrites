"No longer talk at all about the kind of man that a good man ought to be, but be such." — Marcus Aurelius, [*Meditations* 10.16](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_X) (trans. George Long, 1862)

**The reflection**

The week's public business was almost entirely announcement, and the announcements were unusually good at being announcements. A sanctions statute that Congress passed and the President signed in September, aimed at Russian oil and the banks that move it, was set aside by executive order on a Friday night, two days before it was to take effect. On Wednesday of this week the machinery appeared again, in an order that raised duties on Russian goods by five hundred percent and then waived most of what it had just authorized, leaving the threat standing and the trade flowing. In between, a diesel waiver was announced that would put more than three hundred thousand tonnes of Russian product into world markets, while Ukrainian drones struck the oil terminals that pay for the war next door. None of this is incoherent. Read as a single instrument, it is a government that has learned it can keep the threat and spend nothing, and that a waiver is easier to defend than a policy.

The trouble with a government of announcements is not that it lies. It is that it trains everyone reading, including its friends, to treat a stated act as a gesture with a shelf life. Senator Cornyn called the suspension reckless, Senator Tillis called it beyond irresponsible, and the sanctions law remained on the books the whole time, intact and unexecuted. Both facts can be true, and the combination is what corrodes: a statute that says one thing and an order that says another teach the country that the sentence in the statute was never the operative sentence. The one place this week where a sentence changed something was a courthouse. A divided D.C. Circuit held that twenty-seven Christian and Jewish organizations are likely to have standing to challenge the withdrawal of the policy that kept immigration enforcement away from houses of worship, reversed the district judge, and sent the case back. It moved because a filing is a filing, and a panel's judgment does not come with a waiver attached.

Marcus Aurelius has the line for this, and it is harsher than it looks: "No longer talk at all about the kind of man that a good man ought to be, but be such." He is not asking anyone to stop reasoning about virtue. The *Meditations* are nothing but reasoning about virtue, twelve books of it, worked and reworked. What he refuses is the substitution, the moment when describing the good act takes the place of performing it, because the description is easier, has an audience, and can be revised. That substitution is the week's real subject. An announcement is a claim on the future that costs nothing today, and a country can run on those for a while, until the day it needs something to have actually happened.

Here is the fact that sorts the week for me, and I spent Wednesday morning with it. The notebook everyone now calls the *Meditations* was written in Greek by a man addressing himself, in the second person, in the middle of a plague and a frontier war, and it was not written for us. Nobody announced it. It was not a policy, a communiqué, or an order with waivers attached, and for that reason there is nothing in it to walk back. Nineteen centuries later, the edicts of that reign survive as entries in other men's histories, and the private book is the thing still being read on a Wednesday morning by a man in Kansas City. The deed unannounced is the only kind that cannot be undone by the next press release.

Ten days from an election, this is not an abstraction. The week offers two ways to get it wrong, and they are mirror images. The first is to mistake speech for action: to read the polling, the rating changes, the lawsuit, the order, and count the reading as participation. The second is to mistake one's own action for speech: to post the opinion, register the outrage, and leave the ballot in the envelope, which is where a great many opinions have spent their whole lives. A vote is the one act in the sequence that cannot be waived, reinterpreted, or announced in place of performed. It is served, or it is not. I published a piece on that on Wednesday, and the line in it I keep returning to is that a vote is not an opinion at all; it is an opinion that has been served, and the rules of the serve decide whether it becomes a point.

So the question this week leaves is not what to say about the country. It is what to do that no one will hear about, and whether you will do it anyway. The practice below is that question made small enough to answer.


**Saturday's docket.** The [Docket Report of October 17](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/docket-report-2026-10-17/) covers a week that looked procedural and was not. A White House lawyer invoked the presidential communications privilege over Representative Beatty's discovery "at the direction of the President," which put a name and a signature on an assertion her motion to compel had called blank, and Judge Cooper moved her opposition to October 30 while staying summary-judgment briefing until he rules on the motion to lift the injunction. Discovery closes October 23.

**Sunday, the statute set aside.** [SITREP: October 18](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-18/) records the Friday-night order that suspended, for sixty days, sanctions Congress had passed and the President signed in September, two days before they were due to take effect, and the objections that followed from senators of his own party. Overnight, Ukrainian drones reached the oil terminal at St Petersburg.

**The map, sixteen days out.** The [Senate Race Report of October 18](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/senate-race-report-2026-10-18/) finds Democratic Senate candidates raising four times what Republicans did, nine of eleven rating changes moving their way, and Sabato's Crystal Ball with Democrats needing two of five toss-ups while Republicans need four.

**Monday, the grid.** [SITREP: October 19](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-19/) reports Russia striking Kyiv's power system in waves, damaging more than ten energy facilities and leaving roughly 550,000 families without electricity by morning; Zelenskyy said the attack came with "no early warning from our partners," and Secretary Rubio answered that there had been no pause in intelligence sharing.

**The fee.** [A Fee of Seventy Thousand Dollars](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/weekly-satire-2026-10-19/), the Weekly Satire of October 19, is a notice from a university international office itemizing what it now costs a college to let one student take one job. The mirror is empty and the frame was paid for in full.

**Tuesday, the negotiator.** [SITREP: October 20](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-20/) carries the President's remark that it might be time for Ukraine to find someone else to negotiate, made hours before the Oslo talks, and his defence of a waiver that would send more than 300,000 tonnes of Russian diesel into world markets; crude stood at $110.00 a barrel that morning, and a Reuters/Ipsos survey put his approval at 31 percent, the lowest of his two terms.

**Five and oh.** The [Chiefs Report of October 20](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sports/chiefs-report-2026-10-20/) has Kansas City at 5-0 and Patrick Mahomes at 100 wins, with both offensive tackles uncertain for Sunday in Seattle.

**Two books, one argument.** The library gained summaries of both Kishimi and Koga volumes. [The Courage to Be Disliked](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/summaries/the-courage-to-be-disliked-summary/) works through the five-night dialogue on trauma, tasks, and the freedom of being disliked; [The Courage to Be Happy](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/summaries/the-courage-to-be-happy-summary/) takes the same psychology into teaching, self-reliance, and love. Read together they argue about whether a person is a fixed verdict or a moving project.

**Feeling behind.** [Feeling Behind](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/feeling-behind/) puts Adler in plain language: why the inferiority feeling is an engine rather than a defect, what a style of life is, and where the theory breaks down.

**Wednesday's court.** [SITREP: October 21](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-21/) reports a divided D.C. Circuit holding that twenty-seven Christian and Jewish organizations are likely to have standing to challenge the withdrawal of the buffer around houses of worship, and remanding for a fresh look at an injunction; the Democratic campaign committees and the two Democratic leaders sued that same day over pressure on state election officials; and crude fell back below $100 a barrel.

**The man in the notebook.** [The Life and Times of Marcus Aurelius](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-life-and-times-of-marcus-aurelius/) is the essay behind the epigraph above: what he actually did, what the *Meditations* actually are, and why the practice still works on a modern morning.

**The serve.** [The Ball in the Hand](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-ball-in-the-hand/), Wednesday's last piece, argues that a vote is not an opinion but an opinion that has been served, and that the rules of the serve decide whether it ever becomes a point.

**Thursday, the release and the strike.** [SITREP: October 22](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-22/) reports Russia releasing a jailed American schoolteacher and telling the president's envoy that it was willing in principle to restart talks on Ukraine, and then striking Kyiv with missiles that killed at least one person and injured twenty-two, four of them children. Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin at Constantine Palace in St Petersburg on Wednesday, praised him as a humanitarian for the release, and told him Washington was grateful for a separate arrangement under which Russia would sell diesel to the United States. The President said the war "may be as close as it's ever been to ending." Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, answered that "appeasement, servility, and flattery will not stop this war."

**The lobby stops announcing.** The same day, AIPAC told its members that ["Endorsements, direct PAC contributions, and earmarking will no longer be part of our approach."](https://www.jta.org/2026/10/21/politics/aipac-to-halt-direct-campaign-contributions-and-candidate-endorsements) Its super PAC remains active, its spokesperson said the mission and strategy are unchanged, and the money is to move through its members rather than through the PAC. The announcement is what changed, and the giving continues.

**The man in the second notebook.** [The Life and Times of Seneca](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-life-and-times-of-seneca/), published Thursday morning, is the companion to last week's Marcus Aurelius: a Spanish-born rhetorician's son who could not reliably breathe, who governed Rome as tutor to the emperor who later ordered his death, and who wrote 124 letters on how to spend a day. The letters still read as though they were posted last week, and the reason is the one this letter keeps returning to: they were written in the last three years of his life, by a man who had stopped waiting for a better hour in which to begin.

**Friday, three kinds of act.** [SITREP: October 23](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/sitrep/sitrep-2026-10-23/) reports the President telling a rally in Sioux City that he has the right to seek a third term, a claim the two-term limit does not allow and one no market prices. It reports the other kind of act the same morning: the Comptroller of the Currency's final rule implementing the GENIUS Act, effective January 18, 2027, which begins on a date because a signed document says so. And it reports a notice from the Justice and Education departments withdrawing reliance on seven joint guidance documents, which ends an obligation by declaration.

**The lamp and the shackle.** [The Life and Times of Epictetus](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-life-and-times-of-epictetus/), published Friday morning, is the third of these lives and the one for which the least evidence survives, because he wrote nothing at all. Everything we have of him comes through Arrian, a student at Nicopolis who took notes during the lectures and said he set them down "in the very same words," and what those notes record is a slave of Nero's household, lame for life, expelled from Rome with the other philosophers, insisting that "he is free who lives as he wills." That sentence costs nothing to say in a lecture hall and everything to say while limping, which is the part of the piece that stays with me.

**The lecture that was cancelled.** [The Life and Times of Ryan Holiday](https://www.huffmanwrites.org/posts/essays/the-life-and-times-of-ryan-holiday/), published an hour later, is the fourth and the only one still in progress. It opens at the Naval Academy in April 2025, where he was asked to cut one passage from a talk on courage because the passage mentioned 381 books the academy had pulled from its library shelves; he declined, the lecture was cancelled, and he delivered it to a camera instead. The rest of the essay is the part that answers for the refusal: seven years transcribing interviews for Robert Greene, a marketing career built on tricks he then described in print, and a book that sold three thousand copies in its first week before a locker room picked it up.

**The weekly reports**

The **Senate Race Report** published its October 18 edition on Sunday, and its central number is money. The third-quarter filings showed Democratic candidates raising $253 million to the Republicans' $56 million across the fifteen races NBC News rates competitive, and winning that race in fourteen of them; Sabato's Crystal Ball now has 49 seats at least leaning Democratic, 46 at least leaning Republican, and five toss-ups in Kansas, Iowa, Maine, Ohio and Texas, where Republicans need four and Democrats need two. The next edition comes at 07:00 Sunday, the second-to-last of the season.

The **Docket Report** published its October 17 edition on Saturday, on a privilege invoked at last and a binder delivered at last. In the Epstein files case the government spent the week arguing that no administrative record was needed and then hand-delivered the redacted interview notes the court had asked for twice, while in the third docket the D.C. Circuit has let the stay motion sit eighteen days past the last brief. The next edition publishes at 08:00 this morning.

The **Chiefs Report** published its October 20 edition on Tuesday, on a 22-6 win over the Chargers carried by Kenneth Walker III's 128 yards and a defense that sacked the quarterback four times. Kansas City is 5-0 and the only unbeaten team in the AFC, Mahomes reached 100 regular-season wins in his 131st start, and the offensive line decides what happens next in Seattle on Sunday.

The **Bond Market's Next Ninety Days** and **The S&P 500's Next Ninety Days** last published on October 1, both closing the same window at December 30; the next installments come at the start of November.

The **Global SITREP** publishes every morning, and the editions of October 18 through 23 are linked above.

The **Repair Plan** is a quarterly working document for a future administration rather than a letter to you.

**The Practice**

Pick one thing this week that needs doing, and do it where nobody can see. Verify your registration, which takes four minutes and earns no one's approval. Write the letter, make the call, put the check in the envelope, and say nothing about it afterwards, not in a post, not in a conversation, not to yourself as evidence of good character. Then watch what the mind does when a deed has no audience. It will offer a reason to skip it, and the reason will sound like realism. It will offer to describe the deed instead, which costs nothing and feels like progress. Marcus Aurelius wrote twelve books of instruction to himself and published none of them. This week's practice is that shape at small scale: an act complete enough that announcing it would only subtract.

See you next Saturday. — Phil
