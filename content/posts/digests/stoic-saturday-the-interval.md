"Take away thy opinion, and then there is taken away the complaint, 'I have been harmed.' Take away the complaint, 'I have been harmed,' and the harm is taken away." — Marcus Aurelius, [*Meditations* 4.7](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Thoughts_of_the_Emperor_Marcus_Aurelius_Antoninus/Book_IV) (trans. George Long, 1862)

**The reflection**

On Friday the S&P 500 closed at 6,910.39, down 3.70 percent, its seventh consecutive lower close and 11.6 percent below the record it set in October. On Monday it closed at 7,237.17, up 4.73 percent, the best session since April 2025. Between those two closes no earnings estimate changed. Nvidia had already reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue, up 103 percent, and guided the next quarter to $130 billion. What changed was who would run the company. Huang had stepped down earlier in November amid reporting on his personal conduct, and on Monday the board reinstated him, and the stock rose 31 percent and added roughly a trillion dollars of value in a session. The president said he had spoken with the board and "convinced" them. A trillion dollars of opinion moved on the strength of a phone call, and not one dollar of it moved on a fact about the business.

Marcus Aurelius is usually read at this point as a consoler, the philosopher who tells you the loss does not hurt. Read the sentence as mechanics instead and it says something harder and more useful. Between an event and a judgement there is a seam, the judgement is made in the seam, and the judgement is made by somebody. "Take away thy opinion" is not advice to have none. It is a description of where the harm lives, and a warning about how quickly the location can be changed by a hand other than your own. An opinion a board can be made to hold, or unmade, is not a person's opinion. It is a rented one, and this week the rent came due on a Monday.

The administration spent the same week shortening other seams. Executive Order 14439, signed November 20 and published Wednesday at 91 FR 76627, requires federal contractors to notify the White House of "any pending or future congressional request respecting the contractors' communications or work with the executive branch," and to "abide by any instructions received from the White House and contracting agency" when the executive branch claims privilege. A presidential memorandum published this morning at 91 FR 76953 directs the attorney general to pursue death sentences in three new categories of murder and to eliminate "unnecessary administrative delays," calling prolonged delay "a grave offense to the justice system." The SEC printed immediately effective rule changes from the Texas Stock Exchange and Nasdaq. The Texas change amends its rule on resuming trading after a market-wide halt, made "in connection with the expansion of trading hours to 23 hours per day, 5 days per week," and the Nasdaq change incorporates overnight price bands under the plan that governs extraordinary volatility. A committee's question, a sentence of death, a closing bell: three intervals, and each document proposes to shorten one.

The courts spent the week holding a seam open on purpose. Judge Christopher Cooper ordered the government to produce the Kennedy Center documents it had withheld under the presidential-communications privilege so that he could read them for himself, and the government lodged them with him on November 20. Judge Ana Reyes took thirty pages to set the Park Service's stated reasons for closing East Potomac Park beside its own chronology, found those reasons pretextual, and wrote that "in its haste to meet an arbitrary deadline, the Government has likely violated at least the National Environmental Policy Act and the Administrative Procedure Act." Neither judge discovered anything. Each one read a document against a claim, which is the only operation in this letter that has ever changed my mind about anything, and it is the operation the week's other documents were written to prevent.

This is the week I am grateful for that interval. Not as a virtue in the abstract. As a thing with a cost, paid by people who could have finished faster and did not.

**Friday's close.** The S&P 500 fell 265.44 points to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq Composite 5.28 percent to 22,617.73, and the Dow 1.93 percent to 48,147.33. The volatility index closed at 35.89, up 26.20 percent. Gold fell 3.98 percent to $3,539.00 an ounce, so the haven that did not bid on Thursday did not bid on Friday either. Money went into government paper and out of nearly everything else, and that is what liquidation looks like rather than a rotation between sectors.

**The keystone.** Nvidia's quarter was the best in its history and the stock fell 14 percent on Friday anyway, and 23 percent over the week, on reporting that regulators were examining its accounting. On Monday the board put its chief executive back, the stock rose 31 percent, and the index had its best day since April 2025. [The account of the reversal](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/22/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) is worth reading twice, because it is a political event wearing a ticker.

**The order.** Executive Order 14439 runs to subcontractors. It directs agencies to coordinate with the White House on their responses to Congress, "including the application or assertion of any relevant privileges," and it lists among the consequences of refusal "potential termination or cancellation of contracts and the potential debarment or suspension of contractors." A contractor that receives a committee's request now holds a written instruction about whom to ask first.

**The memorandum.** The new categories are first-degree murder of a minor; murder committed during rape, sexual abuse, sexual exploitation of a child, or sex trafficking; and murder committed during kidnapping or human trafficking. The direction to hasten applies "when appropriate and consistent with applicable law," and it is bounded by appropriations and by a statement that it creates no enforceable right. The part that will be tested in court is the instruction to prioritize the prompt resolution of appeals and habeas petitions and to oppose "dilatory litigation tactics."

**The twenty-three-hour day.** The Texas Stock Exchange's rule change covers how trading resumes after a market-wide halt, and Nasdaq's incorporates overnight price bands under the plan that governs extraordinary volatility. Both were effective on filing. A market that no longer closes is a market in which the interval between a price and the news that moves it is measured in seconds, which is the interval least likely to hold a judgement.

**The courthouse.** The Docket Report of November 21 counts forty-two entries across five dockets, thirty of them in the golf case. Judge Reyes's restraining order keeps East Potomac Park open through November 28, the government appealed it within four days, and the appeal is D.C. Circuit No. 26-5390. In the Kennedy Center case the government lodged the withheld documents ex parte at ECF 121. In the Epstein files case the Justice Department filed the certified list of the administrative record and appealed another order, D.C. Circuit No. 26-5389.

**The satellite.** German broadcasters and the Süddeutsche Zeitung reported that Russia is preparing to launch a satellite armed with a nuclear warhead, which they said could disrupt or destroy other satellites. The report is unconfirmed. NATO's secretary-general, in Berlin, said the alliance would make assessments of its deterrence "and make updates where necessary." Russia signed the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, which bans weapons of mass destruction in orbit, and the European Space Agency's director called nuclear weapons in space "the ultimate nightmare scenario."

**The edges.** Three Russian cruise missiles came down in southeastern Poland about thirty miles from the Ukrainian border, during a European exercise, and Warsaw called it an intentional provocation. A sanctioned Russian cargo ship burned off Finland after a drone attack that Finland's military attributed to Ukraine. Ukrainian naval drones sank the corvette Merkury at its berth in Benghazi, 2,400 kilometres from the front. A drone crossed briefly into eastern Latvia and NATO fighters scrambled. Four countries in a single week, and the same campaign widening at the edges.

**The party secretary.** China's discipline commission is investigating Chen Jining, the party secretary of Shanghai and a member of the Politburo, for suspected violations of party discipline, and gave no detail beyond that phrase. Shanghai's Standing Committee endorsed the investigation of its own party secretary before any finding was published.

**The Gulf.** The State Department's virtual embassy for Iran told American citizens to leave the country immediately, in language that left nothing to interpret: "Do not travel to Iran for any reason. United States citizens should leave Iran immediately." The alert followed a week of Iranian strikes on Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait, which an Associated Press analysis of imagery found had been struck at least thirty times, and repeated attacks on sites in Bahrain.

**The weekly reports**

The **Docket Report** published its November 21 edition on what changes when a court stops accepting assurances and asks for the documents, and its answer is that the government produces them or it appeals, and that this week it did both. Its forty-two entries include the restraining order that keeps East Potomac Park open through November 28, the government's appeal of it four days later, Judge Cooper's order that the withheld Kennedy Center documents be produced for his own reading, and the Epstein files record filed and appealed on the same day. The next edition publishes this morning at 08:00.

The **Chiefs Report** published its November 24 edition on a team that has won three straight by at least 21 points and a schedule that has not yet tested it. Kansas City is 8-2, level with Denver, seeded fifth in the AFC despite a point differential of plus-108, and Patrick Mahomes has thrown thirteen touchdown passes without an interception since Week 8. Thursday at 9-1 Buffalo is the measurement the report asks for and does not yet have.

The **Bond Market's Next Ninety Days** opened a new window to February 22 with a finding its previous installment did not anticipate: Brent settled Friday at $92.57 against $117.64 on October 30, with the Strait of Hormuz still closed. Supply adapted faster than the strait could shut, the ten-year took a thirty-one basis point round trip and ended two basis points lower at 4.92 percent, and the market prices a quarter-point hike on December 8 and 9 at roughly 98 percent.

The **S&P 500's Next Ninety Days** opened the same window with the week's most uncomfortable sentence: the keystone was not the earnings. Nvidia reported its best quarter and lost a fifth of its value on a governance story, then regained a trillion dollars when the board reversed itself. The report's arithmetic is that the index trades at 17.4 times forward earnings with an earnings yield about 83 basis points above the ten-year, and its question is whether that gap survives a year in which the rules can change for reasons that appear in no model.

The **Global SITREP** publishes every morning, and the editions of November 21 through 27 are linked above. This morning's carries the capital-sentencing memorandum and the drone over Latvia.

The **Senate Race Report** ended with its November 1 edition. The count it anticipated closed at 56 seats to 44, and nothing since has changed it.

**The Practice**

Pick one judgement you have made about this week and write down, in a single sentence, what would change it: a named document, a figure, a finding. Do this before you go looking, because a test chosen after the evidence arrives is not a test. Marcus Aurelius does not promise that the demonstration will never come. He promises his own answer to it in advance, and a promise made before the showing is the only kind that binds.

Then keep one interval open this week. Take the claim you are most inclined to repeat, and let a day pass before you repeat it, long enough to open the page behind it. Most weeks the page is there and the claim holds. Some weeks it is not there, and the honest sentence is that nobody has said. Either way the opinion is yours afterward, and an opinion you have checked is the only kind that cannot be moved by a phone call.

See you next Saturday. — Phil
