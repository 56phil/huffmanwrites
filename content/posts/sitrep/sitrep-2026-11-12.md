The Associated Press called Mississippi's Senate race for Scott Colom shortly before 6 p.m. eastern time on Wednesday, after Jackson County reported 2,173 more votes and put him 4,669 ahead of Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith out of 962,237 counted, a margin of half a percentage point. Colom, 43, a local prosecutor, becomes Mississippi's first Black United States senator since Reconstruction, and the call gives Democrats 55 Senate seats, a net gain of eight and their largest midterm Senate gain since 1986. Hyde-Smith conceded the same evening. In Moscow, American envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived Thursday morning for talks with President Vladimir Putin, the day after two days of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi ended without a ceasefire. And in the Federal Register, the President declared the Oval Office a national monument.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register published 124 documents in the window: one presidential document, 17 rules, 10 proposed rules and 96 notices.

The presidential document is Proclamation 11080, [Establishment of the Oval Office National Monument](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/12/2026-23113/establishment-of-the-oval-office-national-monument). It declares the Oval Office a national monument under section 320301 of title 54, the Antiquities Act, which the proclamation recites allows the President to declare historic landmarks and structures on federally owned or controlled land. Signed November 5 and published November 12, it covers the Oval Office itself, Room 110, together with the Outer Oval Office, Rooms 106 and 108, and the Cabinet Room, Room 105. The National Park Service, under the Secretary of the Interior, is to manage the monument; the proclamation requires a management plan, documentation and review before any action that would substantially alter or demolish it, and coordination with the Committee for the Preservation of the White House. It also provides that nothing in it restricts the President's authority to redecorate the rooms or to make ordinary operational and security changes that do not substantially impair the structure.

The Housing Department published a final rule, [Housing and Community Development Act of 1980: Verification of Eligible Status](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/12/2026-23003/housing-and-community-development-act-of-1980-verification-of-eligible-status), amending the Section 214 regulations to require verification of citizenship or eligible immigration status for every applicant and recipient in covered programs regardless of age, and making prorated assistance temporary pending that verification. It takes effect December 14.

The Justice Department published [Attorney General Memorandum on Implementing English as the Official Language; Request for Information](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/12/2026-22966/attorney-general-memorandum-on-implementing-english-as-the-official-language-request-for-information), announcing a memorandum titled "Guidance on Implementing English as the Official Language," issued under Executive Order 14224, and inviting public comment on it.

The Federal Communications Commission repealed the [National Television Multiple Ownership Rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/12/2026-23040/national-television-multiple-ownership-rule), the national cap that barred a commercial television licensee from holding a cognizable interest in stations whose aggregate national audience reach exceeded 39 percent. The commission cited "seismic changes in the modern media marketplace" and said it will review previously barred transactions case by case for localism, competition and viewpoint diversity. The repeal takes effect January 11, 2027.

The Labor Department's Mine Safety and Health Administration published five final rules under the heading "Improving and Eliminating Regulations," all effective December 14. They remove requirements for the final mining of pillars, which MSHA said is no longer practiced because of safety concerns; drop a mandate for certain electrical equipment in coal seams above the water table, since every coal mine is now classified as gassy; and eliminate requirements for foam generator systems in underground coal mines, outdated diesel particulate matter personal-exposure provisions, and redundant electric cap-lamp requirements.

Among the notices, 24 were Interior Department notices of inventory completion or intended repatriation under the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act. The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control published a sanctions action and a correction to an earlier notice; the abstracts name no targets, and this report did not open the underlying notices. Treasury also gave notice, in [Federal Scholarship Tax Credit; Hearing](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/12/2026-23032/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-hearing), that the December 15 hearing on the proposed credit, which would be non-refundable and would apply to contributions to organizations that grant elementary and secondary school scholarships, will be telephonic only.

## Markets

The morning prints, collected around 06:00 CT on Thursday: the VIX at 18.36, up 0.87 or 4.97 percent; gold at $3,952.40 an ounce, down $48.10 or 1.20 percent; front-month WTI crude at $85.94 a barrel, down $0.59; and the dollar index at 102.879, down 0.029. The quote service had the ten-year Treasury yield at 5.00 percent, up 0.011 of a point. Those are pre-market quotes, taken before the cash market opened.

Treasury's daily curve still ends at November 10, at 4.73 percent for two years, 4.98 for ten and 5.35 for thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.25 against +0.32 on November 9. Total public debt outstanding was $40,559,796,589,243 on November 9, up $7,045,570,151 from November 6; $32,763,773,414,012 was held by the public and $7,796,023,175,231 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics series is unchanged from yesterday's edition, and the three readings are the October ones it reported. The all-items consumer price index was 337.511, a monthly rise of 0.17 percent against 0.59 percent from August to September. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September, and total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September and 225 thousand above August. Taken together, the three say what they said yesterday: prices decelerated in October while the labor market did not loosen. One month is not a trend, and this report has nothing to add until the series moves.

## Elections and the midterms

The Associated Press [called the Mississippi Senate race for Scott Colom](https://wtop.com/us-politics/2026/11/why-ap-called-the-mississippi-senate-race-for-scott-colom/) shortly before 6 p.m. eastern time on Wednesday, after Jackson County reported 2,173 more votes and put him 4,669 ahead of Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith out of 962,237 counted, a margin of 0.5 of a percentage point. After the final Election Night update, which came from Pike County at 3:03 a.m. eastern time on November 4, Colom led by 43 votes; he won all twelve county updates released Wednesday, and fewer than 12,000 ballots remained for county officials to review before Friday's certification deadline. [Mississippi elects Democrat Scott Colom as state's first Black US senator since Reconstruction](https://wtop.com/national/2026/11/mississippi-elects-democrat-scott-colom-as-states-first-black-us-senator-since-reconstruction/) reported that Hyde-Smith conceded Wednesday evening: "While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters." Mississippi has no recount law, so a challenge would need a court order.

The call gives Democrats 55 Senate seats, a net gain of eight, their largest midterm Senate gain since 1986; they last held 55 seats in 2014.

Alaska is the last Senate race uncalled. AP's count, as [Votelect](https://votelect.com/) carries it, had Mary Peltola at 48.6 percent and Senator Dan Sullivan at 47.5. The Division of Elections plans a ranked-choice tabulation on November 18; [Peltola and JKT strengthen leads in latest Alaska election results](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-11-10/peltola-and-jkt-strengthen-leads-in-latest-alaska-election-results) put AP's estimate of the counted vote at 84 percent on November 10 and said more results were due Friday.

The House ledger on the same tracker is stamped November 10 and marked "retained; source error," so this report does not restate a fresh count. The last figure this report used was AP's 253 Democrats to 166 Republicans with 16 races uncalled, as of that stamp.

Polymarket priced the remainder. Peltola stood at 99.4 percent on $193,093 of twenty-four-hour volume while the count had her below 50 percent and the race uncalled; Chuck Schumer stood at 85.5 percent to be Senate majority leader against 12.7 percent for Chris Van Hollen, and Hakeem Jeffries at 97.6 percent to be Speaker. The venue put House turnout between 120 and 125 million at 71.5 percent. Those are prices, not results.

## The world

Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived in Moscow on Thursday morning for talks with President Vladimir Putin. [US envoys arrive in Moscow for Russia-Ukraine talks with Putin](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/12/us-envoys-arrive-in-moscow-for-russia-ukraine-talks-with-putin) reported that Kirill Dmitriev, Putin's special envoy, met them at the airport, and that the delegation reportedly brought an updated peace-plan proposal and hoped to persuade Putin to include Europeans in negotiations. Moscow is adamant about talks without the Europeans. The United States said differences remained on major issues, including territory, and reported some progress on post-war security guarantees. Kyiv has strongly rejected Putin's demand that it surrender all of its eastern region of Donbas.

The visit follows two days of United States-mediated trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi, which ended Wednesday with an agreement on a large prisoner exchange and no ceasefire, as yesterday's edition reported. Whether the exchange survives the Moscow round is the next thing to watch.
