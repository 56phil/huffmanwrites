A presidential memorandum published in this morning's Federal Register directs the attorney general to pursue federal jurisdiction and seek death sentences in three categories of murder, and to ensure that federal death sentences are carried out as expeditiously as possible. It is titled "Expeditious Enforcement of Capital Sentences and the Pursuit of Justice for Victims of Heinous Crimes." It was signed on November 23, filed on November 25, and printed this morning at 91 FR 76953.

The categories it adds are first-degree murder of a minor; murder committed during rape, sexual abuse, sexual exploitation of a child, or sex trafficking; and murder committed during kidnapping or human trafficking. The direction applies "when appropriate and consistent with applicable law," and it operates in addition to the crimes named in section 3(b) of Executive Order 14164 of January 20, 2025.

The enforcement half carries the weight. The attorney general is told to take "all lawful and appropriate measures" toward carrying out federal death sentences, among them prioritizing the prompt resolution of direct appeals, habeas petitions and other post-conviction proceedings; opposing "dilatory litigation tactics"; seeking expedited judicial consideration; and eliminating "unnecessary administrative delays." The memorandum's background section calls prolonged delay in carrying out a death sentence "a grave offense to the justice system," and says capital sentences are too often frustrated by "years, or even decades, of litigation."

Four general provisions bound the directive. It must be carried out consistently with applicable law and "subject to the availability of appropriations." It does not impair the authority of an executive department or agency head, or the budget functions of the Office of Management and Budget. It creates no enforceable right or benefit, substantive or procedural. The Justice Department pays the cost of publication.

## The administration and the rule of law

The register's two-day window holds 137 documents: one presidential document, ten rules, seventeen proposed rules and 109 notices. Most are routine at this volume: airworthiness directives, air-plan approvals, fisheries notices, a long run of information-collection requests, and a Postal Service proposal on exceptional addressing changes.

Two clusters stand out. The first is trade and enforcement. Homeland Security extended the comment period on ["Heightened Import Disclosures for Supply Chain Visibility; Extension of Comment Period"](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/27/2026-24149/heightened-import-disclosures-for-supply-chain-visibility-extension-of-comment-period), and the Agriculture Department reopened the comment period on ["Agricultural Foreign Investment Disclosure Act of 1978"](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/27/2026-24143/agricultural-foreign-investment-disclosure-act-of-1978), its proposed revisions to the regulations under that statute after stakeholders asked for more time.

The second is market structure, where the SEC published a run of immediately effective exchange changes tied to longer trading days. [The Texas Stock Exchange's notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/27/2026-24073/self-regulatory-organizations-texas-stock-exchange-llc-notice-of-filing-and-immediate-effectiveness) amends TXSE Rule 11.021 on resuming trading after a Level 3 market-wide circuit-breaker halt, "in connection with the expansion of trading hours to 23 hours per day, 5 days per week." [Nasdaq's notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/27/2026-24065/self-regulatory-organizations-the-nasdaq-stock-market-llc-notice-of-filing-and-immediate) incorporates overnight price bands established under the Regulation NMS plan to address extraordinary market volatility, and FINRA and Nasdaq Texas filed companion changes. Several of the morning's exchange notices pointed the same way.

## The courts

Nothing was filed in any of the courts or dockets this report watches. Neither the Supreme Court nor the D.C. Circuit issued an opinion in the two-day window; the district court in Washington recorded no new complaints; and none of the five watched dockets, from Beatty v. Trump to the consolidated Epstein-files appeal, shows a filing.

A quiet window is a fact, and this report states it without reading anything into it. The new memorandum on capital sentencing will reach the courts soon enough, and the appeals and habeas petitions it orders prioritized are where its effect will first be visible.

## The economy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' newest readings are October's, unchanged from yesterday's pack. The consumer price index was 337.511, against 336.940 in September, a monthly rise of 0.17 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September's 4.2 and level with August. Nonfarm payrolls reached 159,293 thousand, a gain of 165,000 on the month after September's 60,000.

Prices cooled, hiring strengthened, and the jobless rate held just above four percent. The three readings describe an economy that is not contracting, and they are a month old.

## The world

Fighter jets on NATO's Baltic air-policing mission were scrambled early Friday after an unidentified drone crossed briefly into eastern Latvia, near the Russian border. Latvia's National Armed Forces said the drone was detected in Ludza municipality and "briefly flew over Latvian territory." An air threat was declared in Ludza at 2:30 a.m. local time, with residents receiving an orange alert, and lifted at 2:55 a.m.; a yellow warning for neighbouring Balvi municipality ended around 3:30 a.m., according to the public broadcaster LSM. The details are from [NATO jets scrambled after drone briefly enters Latvian airspace](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/27/nato-jets-scrambled-after-drone-briefly-enters-latvian-airspace), Euronews's report of November 27.

The armed forces said: "Fighter jets from NATO's air-policing mission in the Baltic States were scrambled," adding that an interceptor drone of the national armed forces was deployed and later returned to its launch site. The army did not identify the drone's origin. It said it had sent additional air-defence units to the eastern border and warned that "foreign drones could again enter Latvian airspace while Russia's war in Ukraine continues."

Drones have been shot down over Latvia twice this year: once by French jets in June, and once by allied fighters over Balvi in August. Friday's incursion was brief and ended without a shoot-down, and it belongs to a pattern the Baltic states have been living with for months rather than to a single night's alarm.
