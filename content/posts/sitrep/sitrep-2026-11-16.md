A third American aircraft carrier reached the Middle East over the weekend while the United States and Iran exchanged fire around the Strait of Hormuz for a second day, and the morning's two accounts of it disagree about how close the two countries are to full war. Euronews reported that American forces struck missile launchers in southern Iran, at Bandar Lengeh and Sirik, after Iran attacked a US base in Bahrain and targeted tankers in the strait, and that the Revolutionary Guard claimed six tankers hit since Sunday. Al Jazeera reported that the USS Theodore Roosevelt had joined the George Washington and the George H.W. Bush, putting three carriers in the region at once for the first time since the Iraq war, though it said the Pentagon and Central Command have not formally confirmed the arrival. The same explainer quotes the president as calling further strikes "quite unlikely" and the military phase "effectively over."

The federal government's own record of the day was quieter and more mundane, and it will outlast the headlines. The Federal Register published 104 documents, among them four credit-union rules issued together, a final rule removing wage and benefit requirements from the Head Start performance standards, a score of trade determinations, and two designations of one organization, Los Lorenzanas, as a terrorist group. CourtListener logged one new lawsuit in Washington. And the arithmetic that governs what the government borrows has not improved: the ten-year Treasury yielded 5.13 percent this morning, and total public debt stood at $40.61 trillion.

## The administration and the rule of law

Two presidential documents headed the file: a [proclamation of Veterans Day, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23348/veterans-day-2026), and a [presidential determination concerning wildfire suppression activities in the United States](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23349/presidential-determination-concerning-wildfire-suppression-activities-in-the-united-states).

The National Credit Union Administration accounted for four of the eleven rules, all published the same morning. [Accuracy of Advertising and Notice of Insured Status](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23288/accuracy-of-advertising-and-notice-of-insured-status) eliminates the "official advertising statement" that federally insured credit unions have long been required to include in advertising, while leaving the requirement to display the official sign in place. [Nondiscrimination Requirements](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23281/nondiscrimination-requirements) removes an entire lending-discrimination regulation, which the agency describes as duplicative: the rule summarized Fair Housing Act limits on discrimination in real-estate lending, appraisals and advertising, and the agency notes that the act is administered primarily by the Department of Housing and Urban Development and still applies to federal credit unions. The regulation had last been amended substantively in 2001. Two further rules, on consumer financial privacy and on the registration of mortgage loan originators, make technical corrections.

Health and Human Services finalized a rule titled [Restoring Flexibility To Support Head Start Program Access](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23279/restoring-flexibility-to-support-head-start-program-access), which removes requirements on wages and benefits from the Head Start Program Performance Standards. The Administration for Children and Families, which wrote it, says the provisions are costly and overly prescriptive, that the rescissions affect "the costliest parts" of the 2024 rule on the Head Start workforce, and that the changes will produce "over $2 billion in future cost savings." The rule takes effect January 15, 2027.

Commerce supplied the largest bloc. More than twenty of the notices report results in antidumping and countervailing-duty proceedings, on steel flanges and threaded rod from India, aluminum foil from Türkiye, wooden cabinets and metal lockers from China, steel propane cylinders from Thailand and frozen fish fillets from Vietnam among others. Two further notices open countervailing-duty and less-than-fair-value investigations into [railway tank cars and subassemblies from Mexico](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23291/certain-railway-tank-cars-and-subassemblies-thereof-from-mexico-initiation-of-countervailing-duty), and one continues the orders on diamond sawblades from China. The State Department gave notice of two designations of Los Lorenzanas, as a [foreign terrorist organization](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23246/foreign-terrorist-organization-designation-of-los-lorenzanas) and as a specially designated global terrorist, and the Treasury gave [notice of OFAC sanctions actions](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23208/notice-of-ofac-sanctions-actions). Among the eight proposed rules, Interior would set hunting rules for Castle Mountains and Craters of the Moon national monuments.

## The courts

Neither the Supreme Court nor the D.C. Circuit filed an opinion in the window, which ran from Sunday into this morning. The only new district-court entry was a complaint: [Burlew v. Trump](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75120499/burlew-v-trump/), 1:26-cv-04030, filed November 15. CourtListener carries captions and docket numbers, not complaints, so this report can say who sued whom and not what the suit alleges.

All five watched dockets were silent. The Kennedy Center case, [Beatty v. Trump](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72069932/beatty-v-trump/), 1:25-cv-04480, recorded nothing; neither did the two Epstein-files cases, [Phang v. Department of Justice](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70999320/msw-media-inc-v-department-of-justice/), nor their consolidated appeal at the D.C. Circuit, [Katie Phang v. Todd Blanche, No. 26-5299](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74696600/katie-phang-v-todd-blanche/). The East Potomac golf course case, [D.C. Preservation League v. Department of the Interior](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72278895/dc-preservation-league-v-department-of-interior/), was quiet as well, two days after Judge Ana Reyes blocked the closure and the docket recorded entries 158 and 159.

## Markets

Cash equities have not traded since Friday's close, so the index prints are three days old: the S&P 500 at 7,561.67, down 0.40 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 25,136.273, down 0.91; the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 52,347.55, up 0.10. Several quotes carry this morning's stamp instead. The VIX printed 20.11 at 6:59 a.m. eastern, up 0.81, or 4.20 percent. The ten-year yield was 5.133 percent at 7:00 a.m. eastern, against Treasury's constant-maturity reading of 5.15 for November 13. Gold was $3,878.40 an ounce, down 0.20 percent; WTI crude $87.82 a barrel, down 0.84; the dollar index 103.506, up 0.09.

Treasury's curve for November 13 ran at 4.88 percent for two years, 5.15 for ten and 5.47 for thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.27, against +0.24 on November 12. The ten-year has held above five percent at every print in this pack. Total public debt outstanding was $40,608,506,856,903 on November 12, up $24,376,776,630 from November 10, the prior business day. Of that, $32,824,386,143,425 was held by the public and $7,784,120,713,478 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

No new release has landed since the October figures. The all-items consumer price index was 337.511 for October, up from 336.940 in September, a monthly rise of about 0.17 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September. Prices rose slowly, the labor market held, and one month is not a trend.

## Elections and the midterms

The Alaska Senate race remains uncalled by the Associated Press, and the next step in it comes on Wednesday. The Division of Elections tallied another 4,290 ballots on Saturday, and Alaska Public Media, reading the AP's count, put Mary Peltola at 49.9 percent to Senator Dan Sullivan's 46.0, with 97 percent of the vote counted. The state tabulates ranked choices on November 18 in any race where no candidate won a majority of first-place votes, and certifies the result on November 25.

Mississippi's counties reported their final numbers on Friday. The secretary of state's count, as WLBT published it, put Scott Colom at 479,404 votes, or 49.3 percent, to Cindy Hyde-Smith's 471,817, or 48.6, a margin of 7,587, with 21,344 affidavit ballots processed and about 73 percent of them accepted.

Polymarket priced Peltola at 99.8 percent on $74,127 of twenty-four-hour volume, and Sullivan at 0.1 percent. The same venue had Chuck Schumer at 91.5 percent to be the next Senate majority leader and Chris Van Hollen at 8.2. Those are prices, not calls.

## The world

The war that a two-week ceasefire in April and a June memorandum of understanding failed to end widened over the weekend and again this morning on both sides of the Strait of Hormuz, according to Euronews. American forces struck missile launchers around Bandar Lengeh and Sirik in southern Iran after Iran attacked a US base in Bahrain; Iran flew a drone at a radar at Bahrain's Al Riffa Air Base on Sunday; and the Revolutionary Guard said two more tankers exploded in the strait on Monday, six since Sunday. The Guard said two tankers crossing the southern route under American guidance struck naval mines, and Iran's navy said a tanker it called a violator caught fire after hitting a mine east of the strait. The British maritime authority, UKMTO, confirmed one Monday incident: a tanker hit by "an unidentified projectile" while leaving the strait, starting an engine-room fire, with no casualties and the crew safe. Euronews noted that the Guard's and the navy's claims were not independently verified.

The naval picture is the part that moved most. Euronews put the Theodore Roosevelt alongside the George Washington and the George H.W. Bush, three carriers in the region for the first time since the Iraq war and about nineteen warships. Al Jazeera, citing an unnamed American official, said the Roosevelt arrived on November 15 after leaving San Diego in September, and that the Makin Island amphibious group, with more than 2,000 Marines, had followed. It said the Pentagon and Central Command have not formally confirmed either movement.

Official Washington described the moment more calmly than the exchanges did. Al Jazeera's explainer quotes the president calling further strikes "quite unlikely," the military phase "effectively over," and American forces "locked and loaded"; it also quotes him saying, "We don't need a deal with Iran." Secretary of State Marco Rubio, in the Euronews account, described "the Shia clerics of Iran and their expansionist mentality" as "an extraordinary historic danger and one that cannot be ignored," and told Al Jazeera that "the economic pressure on Iran is going to get even worse."

Tehran's terms have not moved. Iran's deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, told Al Jazeera that Tehran "will not wait for the US to attack first" and that "Iran is not Cuba that you can blockade, nor is it Venezuela that you can force to surrender." The parliamentary speaker, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said Iran had sent seven conditions through Qatar, among them lifting the naval blockade and sanctions, releasing frozen funds and formally declaring the war over, and was waiting for an American response. Pakistan and Qatar have mediated since July. Ali Vaez of the International Crisis Group warned that the two sides' efforts to deter war "could inadvertently ignite one that neither can win, contain or easily end."

What to watch is whether Washington confirms the carrier count, whether Tehran answers the seven conditions, and whether the strait's shipping lanes reopen. A third carrier is a signal; the damaged tankers are the fact.
