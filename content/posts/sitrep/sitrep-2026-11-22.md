American equities fell hard on Friday, and the scale of the fall is the day's story. The S&P 500 ended Friday at 6,910.39, down 265.44 points, or 3.70 percent. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1,261.47 points, 5.28 percent, to 22,617.73. The Dow lost 949.73 points, 1.93 percent, to 48,147.33. The CBOE volatility index rose 26.20 percent to 35.89, a reading that describes disorder rather than nerves.

What lifts the day above a bad afternoon for stocks is where the money went instead. The ten-year Treasury yield fell to 4.92 percent from 5.04 the day before, meaning prices rose as buyers took cover. Gold, the asset that often rises when equities fall, fell 3.98 percent to 3,539.00 dollars an ounce. Money moved into government paper and out of nearly everything else, gold included, which is the look of liquidation rather than a rotation between sectors. Oil was the exception: WTI rose 1.43 percent to 83.62 dollars a barrel.

Below the markets, the country's institutions were quiet. The Federal Register published nothing across the two-day window, the courts filed nothing, and the five dockets this report watches all sat still. The other settled fact is political: the Senate count is final, and it is a Democratic majority of 56 seats to 44. The number to carry into Monday is the one from Friday's close.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register carried nothing for 2026-11-21 or 2026-11-22: zero presidential documents, zero final rules, zero proposed rules and zero notices. A weekend is lean by habit, and a late-November weekend leans further, but zero is still a fact to state plainly rather than dress up. No agency rule took effect in the window, no proposal opened for comment, and no presidential document entered the record. Read the emptiness as the register's own account of itself, not as proof that the government stopped; the register records what is published, and this weekend nothing was.

## The courts

The courts were dark for the weekend. No Supreme Court opinion and no D.C. Circuit opinion was filed in the window, and the district courts recorded no new complaint.

Each of the five dockets this report follows was read, and each recorded no filing in the window:

- Beatty v. Trump, 1:25-cv-04480, the Kennedy Center matter.
- Phang v. Blanche, 1:26-cv-01417, one of the two Epstein-files cases.
- MSW Media, Inc. v. Department of Justice, 1:25-cv-02518, the other.
- D.C. Preservation League v. Department of Interior, 1:26-cv-00477, over the East Potomac golf course.
- Phang v. Blanche, D.C. Cir. No. 26-5299, consolidated with 26-5334, the appeal.

A quiet docket is not a closed one. None of these matters is finished, and the report claims only that Saturday and Sunday produced no movement in any of them.

## The economy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' newest readings are all for October 2026, and they do not describe an economy in retreat. The consumer price index stood at 337.511, up 0.17 percent on September's 336.940, a slower monthly rise than September's own 0.59 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September's 4.2. Nonfarm payrolls reached 159,293 thousand, a gain of 165,000 on the month after a 60,000 gain in September.

Read together, the three say prices cooled on the month while hiring strengthened and the jobless rate edged down. That is not the profile of a contracting economy, and it sits uneasily beside an equity market that spent Friday selling as if one were coming. Both can be true at once: markets price expectations, and the labor data describing October is already a month old by the time it prints.

## Elections and the midterms

The count is final. Democrats will hold 56 Senate seats to the Republicans' 44, a net gain of nine, after Mary Peltola's win in Alaska, the last race called, late on Tuesday, as reported by ["Final 2026 midterm election results: Democrats clinch 56-seat Senate majority"](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/article/final-2026-midterm-election-results-democrats-clinch-56-seat-senate-majority-with-peltolas-alaska-win-what-they-can--and-cant--do-next-184618858.html). Peltola took just over 50 percent against the incumbent, Dan Sullivan, enough to clear the threshold that avoids a ranked-choice tabulation.

The prediction markets have stopped counting seats and started pricing the shape of the result. On Polymarket, Chuck Schumer carries a 93.5 percent chance to be the next Senate majority leader on $67,371 of twenty-four-hour volume; Chris Van Hollen sits at 6.3 percent and Brian Schatz at 0.7. The House turnout market puts 115 to 120 million votes at 70.3 percent. These are prices, not results, and the report prints them as prices: what traders will pay is not what has been counted.

## The world

One story outside the country moved on Saturday and was still moving this morning. A Russian-flagged cargo ship, the Sparta II, was attacked by drones in the Gulf of Finland west of Gogland island while sailing from St Petersburg to Kaliningrad. The 121-metre vessel crossed into Finnish waters with a fire aboard, anchored there, and burned through the night while Finnish coastguard vessels used water cannon on it. All sixteen crew members were evacuated to another Russian ship, and no deaths or injuries were reported, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/22/russian-cargo-ship-still-on-fire-off-finland-after-drone-attack).

Finland's military said the attack was likely carried out by Ukraine. Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the war is "spilling over into our immediate vicinity" and that "Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor." The ship is owned by Oboronlogistika, a logistics firm that serves Russia's Defence Ministry and is under U.S. sanctions. Estonia's navy commander, Ivo Vark, said further strikes in the gulf could not be ruled out.

The Gulf of Finland is the water between Finland, Estonia and Russia, and it carries Russia's traffic to and from St Petersburg. A drone attack there is a strike in the approaches to a Russian home port, on a sanctioned hull doing military logistics, acknowledged and defended by the NATO state whose waters the burning ship entered. That combination makes Saturday's attack more than another maritime incident, and it is the foreign story to watch into the week.
