President Trump said, in remarks reported overnight, that it might be time for Ukraine to find someone else to negotiate, and he defended a sanctions waiver that will send more than 300,000 metric tonnes of Russian diesel into world markets. Ukrainian and American officials were due to meet in Oslo the same day. The morning's crude print is the other half of the story: front-month WTI at $110.00 a barrel, up $6.34, or 6.12 percent, as of 6:47 a.m. ET, on supply risk from the war with Iran rather than from Russia. The ten-year Treasury stood at 5.119 percent, and September's consumer prices were 0.9 percent higher than July's. Energy is the hinge on which the rest of the day turns.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register's two-day window holds 228 documents: three presidential documents, 26 rules, 17 proposed rules and 182 notices. The presidential documents are Monday's renewals of the national emergencies covering the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Sudan and significant narcotics traffickers centered in Colombia. Each continuation keeps a sanctions authority in force for another year.

Today's substantive rulemaking is concentrated in health care and energy. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services proposed [Medicare Program; Implementation of Sections 6219, 6220, and 6224 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21420/medicare-program-implementation-of-sections-6219-6220-and-6224-of-the-consolidated-appropriations), carrying adjustments to Part D cost-sharing reductions for low-income beneficiaries, the provider-directory requirements of the REAL Health Providers Act, and new pharmacy benefit manager accountability measures. The Energy Department and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission jointly proposed [Authorizations for Certain Activities at Liquefied Natural Gas Plants](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21443/authorizations-for-certain-activities-at-liquefied-natural-gas-plants), which would create procedures for approving certain activities at LNG plants without a project-specific authorization order under sections 3 or 7 of the Natural Gas Act. In a single proposal, that would move a class of routine LNG decisions out of case-by-case review. The Agriculture Department moved the other way on one import rule, proposing [Minimum Maturation Temperature for Beef and Ovine Meat From Regions Affected With Foot-and-Mouth Disease](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21433/minimum-maturation-temperature-for-beef-and-ovine-meat-from-regions-affected-with-foot-and-mouth), a cut from 4°C to 2°C that aligns with World Organization for Animal Health standards while leaving the other FMD safeguards in place.

The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control published [Notice of OFAC Sanctions Action](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21445/notice-of-ofac-sanctions-action), one of three sanctions notices in the window. It identifies individuals added to the specially designated nationals list and blocks their property under United States jurisdiction. The State Department published its [Determination and Certification With Respect to the Child Soldiers Prevention Act (CPSA) of 2008](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21394/determination-and-certification-with-respect-to-the-child-soldiers-prevention-act-cpsa-of-2008). The International Trade Commission issued determinations on [Small Vertical Shaft Engines From China; Determinations](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21459/small-vertical-shaft-engines-from-china-determinations) and [Van-Type Trailers and Subassemblies From China; Determinations](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21382/van-type-trailers-and-subassemblies-from-china-determinations), and the Small Business Administration carried [Presidential Declaration of a Major Disaster for Public Assistance Only for the State of Utah](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/20/2026-21469/presidential-declaration-of-a-major-disaster-for-public-assistance-only-for-the-state-of-utah) and a parallel declaration for New Hampshire.

## Markets

Monday's close was mixed. The S&P 500 finished at 7,690.73, up 0.41 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,430.886, up 1.11 percent. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.07 percent to 51,966.03.

This morning's board is where the pressure shows. The VIX was 18.72, up 1.52 percent, as of 6:59 a.m. ET. Gold was $4,140.30 an ounce, down 0.48 percent, as of 6:50. The dollar index stood at 101.892, down 0.09 percent, as of 7:00:21. Front-month WTI crude was $110.00, up 6.12 percent, as of 6:47, and Brent remained at a five-month high around $110 a barrel, according to [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/20/oil-prices-top-110-as-traders-weigh-middle-east-risks). The ten-year Treasury was quoted at 5.119 percent as of 7:00:05, after closing Monday at 5.15.

The Treasury's Monday curve ran 4.77 percent on the two-year, 5.15 on the ten-year and 5.48 on the thirty-year. The 2s10s spread was +0.38 percentage points, three basis points steeper than Friday's +0.35. A steeper curve with oil rising is not the shape of a market pricing easier money.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,387,508,795,232 on October 16, down $808,879,840 from the prior business day. Debt held by the public was $32,532,409,708,643, and $7,855,099,086,588 of the total was intragovernmental.

## The economy

September's three readings describe an economy in which prices are accelerating and hiring has nearly stopped. The consumer price index was 336.940 in September, 334.980 in August and 333.918 in July. That is a 0.6 percent monthly rise in September and 0.9 percent over two months. Held for a year, the two-month pace would run near 5.5 percent. Unemployment was 4.2 percent, up from 4.1 in August. Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 to 159.044 million, after 133,000 in August.

The combination matters because the ten-year is trading just above 5.1 percent. On that arithmetic, a lender is not being paid much of a real return to hold government debt for a decade. The choice the readings present, holding rates against inflation or raising them against a slowing labor market, is the one every policy meeting this autumn inherits.

## Elections and the midterms

The midterms are November 3. All 435 House seats and 33 Senate seats are up, with Republicans holding the House 218–214 and the Senate 53–47.

A Reuters/Ipsos survey published Monday put approval of Trump's job performance at 31 percent, the lowest of his two terms and down from 32 percent two weeks earlier, and the generic ballot at 47 percent Democratic to 35 percent Republican, a twelve-point lead widened from seven. The sample was 1,679 adults. The figures come from [Trump's approval ratings plummet: what that means for the US midterms](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/20/trumps-approval-ratings-plummet-what-that-means-for-the-us-midterms).

On Polymarket, the venue prices match the drift. The House control market gave Democrats 91.5 percent and Republicans 8.5 percent. The Senate control market gave Democrats 64.5 percent. The balance-of-power market put a Democratic sweep at 62.5 percent, a Republican Senate with a Democratic House at 27.5 percent, and a Republican sweep at 8.5 percent. Texas gave Democrat James Talarico 62.5 percent against Republican Ken Paxton at 38.5 percent; among the state races the pack lists, Texas carried the most volume.

Two of the most-traded contracts tie the Senate to energy. In the market on who wins the Senate pending a Hormuz agreement by October 23, the no-agreement outcome carried 93.2 percent on $235,238 of twenty-four-hour volume, with a Republican Senate contingent on an agreement at 3.6 percent and a Democratic one at 2.9. These are the venue's prices, not this publication's view, and a thin book is not a forecast.

## The world

The alliance between Washington and Kyiv is coming apart in public, and the timing is the story. Trump's suggestion that Ukraine find another negotiator, and his remark that Zelenskyy "should have had settled this war a long time ago," came hours before Ukrainian and American officials were due to meet in Oslo, [Zelenskyy to hold talks on Russia-Ukraine war with Witkoff, Europeans](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/20/zelenskyy-to-hold-peace-talks-in-oslo-with-witkoff-kushner-attending). Special envoy Steve Witkoff was to attend, with Jared Kushner joining remotely because of illness; the prime ministers of Norway and Finland, the E4 group of Britain, France, Germany and Poland, the European Union and NATO were also expected. Zelenskyy had asked for both American representatives.

Before the meeting, Zelenskyy said he had held a "substantive conversation" with Secretary of State Marco Rubio about Patriot interceptors, intelligence sharing and Starlink. He called it "constructive" and did not confirm any American commitments. That is the pattern of the week: conversation without delivery. Zelenskyy said arms shipments had been delayed and intelligence sharing restricted, that the Pentagon was holding a missile package paid for through the PURL program pending Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's signature, and that European governments were ready to send Patriot interceptors from their own stocks but needed American approval. Ukraine went without advance warning of Russian attacks three times in the past week, he said.

The sanctions waiver runs the other way. Trump defended it as leverage to keep Moscow at the table, and announced that Russia would supply "more than 300,000 metric tonnes of diesel" to American and global markets. Zelenskyy said the decision "will hurt Ukraine," and told American senators that administration decisions "are hard to explain between friends."

The military picture has not paused for the diplomacy. Russian strikes continued, and Ukrainian drones struck Russian oil infrastructure. Oil at $110 links the two wars in the market's arithmetic: Russian supply is being admitted through the waiver while Iranian supply is under attack in the Gulf.
