Russia struck Kyiv's power grid overnight with ballistic missiles and drones, damaged more than ten energy facilities, and left roughly 550,000 families without electricity by morning, according to Ukrainian officials. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack came with "no early warning from our partners." Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking to reporters in Uzbekistan, answered that there had been no pause in intelligence sharing. The exchange puts the two governments' accounts of the war at odds in public, on a morning when the ten-year Treasury yields 5.08 percent, oil trades above $100 a barrel, and Friday's equity close was down across all three indexes.

## The administration and the rule of law

Three presidential documents renewed national emergencies, all published today: one for the Democratic Republic of the Congo, one for Sudan, and one for significant narcotics traffickers centered in Colombia. A continuation keeps an emergency, and the sanctions authorities that rest on it, in force for another year. Taken together they are the day's clearest signal that the administration is not letting any of those files close.

The substantive rulemaking was concentrated in two agencies. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission finalized [Further Definition of "Swap" To Exclude Casino-Style Gambling Products](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/19/2026-21376/further-definition-of-swap-to-exclude-casino-style-gambling-products), with a companion proposal on swap inclusions published the same morning. The pair draws the line between derivatives the agency regulates and gambling products it does not. The Office of Personnel Management issued three rules touching the federal workforce in a single day: [Elimination of Time-in-Grade](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/19/2026-21319/elimination-of-time-in-grade), [Managing Senior Professional Performance](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/19/2026-21316/managing-senior-professional-performance), and [Administrative Leave for Workforce Realignment and Other Purposes](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/19/2026-21320/administrative-leave-for-workforce-realignment-and-other-purposes). Administrative leave is the mechanism agencies use to idle an employee while a personnel action proceeds, and the title names workforce realignment as the purpose.

The Defense Department published ten separate arms sales notifications, each a notice to Congress of a proposed transfer. Commerce carried most of the enforcement docket: final antidumping and countervailing determinations covering Canadian softwood lumber, truck bed covers from China, and forged steel fluid end blocks from Italy, among others, plus a postponement of the final determination on large-diameter graphite electrodes from China with an extension of provisional measures in that case.

In all, the Register ran 146 documents: three presidential documents, 23 rules, 13 proposed rules, and 107 notices. Most of the notices are routine collection and meeting paperwork. The weight of the day sits in the trade docket and the personnel rules, not in the presidential documents, which renew what already exists.

The captions are the record so far; the dockets will say more in the coming days.

## Markets

The equity prints are Friday's close, and they were down across the board: the S&P 500 at 7,659.44, off 1.09 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 26,140.034, off 2.14 percent; the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 52,002.41, off 0.34 percent. This morning's pre-market readings show volatility lower. The VIX was 18.49, down 7.50 percent, as of 6:59 a.m. ET. The dollar index stood at 101.867 as of 7:00 a.m. ET. Gold was $4,186.60 an ounce and WTI crude $101.00 a barrel as of 6:50 a.m. ET, the crude down 0.40 percent.

The Treasury curve closed Friday at 4.74 percent on the two-year, 5.09 percent on the ten-year, and 5.43 percent on the thirty-year. The 2s10s spread was +0.35 percentage points, two basis points steeper than Thursday's +0.33. The ten-year was quoted at 5.081 percent this morning.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,388,317,675,072 on October 15, up $59.1 billion from the prior business day. Debt held by the public was $32,538,236,547,452, and $7,850,081,127,620 of the total was intragovernmental debt.

## The economy

September's readings describe an economy pulling in two directions. The consumer price index rose to 336.940 in September from 334.980 in August, a 0.6 percent monthly increase after 0.3 percent the month before. Unemployment ticked up to 4.2 percent from 4.1 percent. Nonfarm payrolls rose by 29,000, to 159.044 million, after a gain of 133,000 in August.

Read together, prices accelerated while hiring nearly stopped. That is the combination that leaves a central bank with no comfortable move, and it is the backdrop against which the ten-year is trading above 5 percent.

## Elections and the midterms

On Polymarket, in contracts with real volume, the House is priced as all but decided and the Senate as leaning Democratic. The House control market gave Democrats 90.5 percent, with $38,113 of twenty-four-hour volume on that contract and $118,249 on the Republican contract at 9.5 percent. The Senate control market gave Democrats 64.5 percent and Republicans 36.5 percent.

The balance-of-power market put a Democratic sweep at 62.5 percent, a Republican Senate with a Democratic House at 27.5 percent, and a Democratic Senate with a Republican House at 0.9 percent. Among the state races with the most volume, Texas gave Ken Paxton 37.5 percent in the Senate race, Nebraska gave Pete Ricketts 70.5 percent against an independent at 29.5 percent, and Michigan gave Mike Rogers 30.5 percent.

Two of the day's most-traded contracts tie the Senate outcome to energy prices: a Republican Senate with oil below $85 before the midterms was priced at 3.0 percent, and the same outcome with oil below $80 at 2.2 percent. A separate market put no Hormuz agreement before the midterms at 88.7 percent. These are the venue's prices, not this publication's view, and a thin book is not a forecast.

## The world

Russia attacked Kyiv's power system overnight in waves, beginning around 11 p.m. Sunday and returning around 3 a.m., with at least 16 ballistic missiles fired at the capital region and about 200 drones across the country, including jet-powered Shaheds, according to Ukraine's Defense Ministry. More than ten energy facilities were damaged. A clinic, a school, a metro station, and homes were hit. The Defense Ministry said there were casualties but gave no figure.

Moscow said it struck sites linked to the defense industry, including facilities storing cruise missiles and drones and a data center in the Kyiv region. The attack followed a Ukrainian drone campaign over the weekend that Russia said its air defenses met by downing 1,015 drones near Moscow and in western regions.

The political damage may outlast the physical. Zelenskyy said there was "no early warning from our partners" before the first wave, and Ukrainian Air Force spokesman Yuri Ihnat said the same: no warning before 11 p.m., though one did arrive before the 3 a.m. strike. Zelenskyy described the combination of blocked interceptor supplies and Russian sanctions relief or delays as "a denial of reality on the part of our partners."

Washington's answer has been to deny a break. Rubio said, "There has been no pause," and added that Zelenskyy should focus on "bringing an end to this war." He said he did not know the details of the latest strike but cited "a software problem involving their early warning system," and said European Command was reviewing it. Zelenskyy had told NBC that intelligence sharing was interrupted and later "deblocked," and that it now works.

Behind the dispute is a sanctions waiver. On Friday, President Trump waived a sanctions package named for Senator Lindsey Graham, telling Congress the pause was in the national interest. On Truth Social he said he was "in the process of negotiating peace" and did not want to "drive, foolishly, Russia away from the bargaining table." He also said Zelenskyy "doesn't care" and "doesn't want to end this war."
