A deregulatory package crossed the Federal Register in a single morning. The Department of Health and Human Services issued a final rule removing outdated requirements from child support enforcement and a companion proposal for block grants. The Personnel Management Office rewrote the rules for federal human capital management. The Environmental Protection Agency proposed to rescind the Super Emitter Program, the mechanism by which it catches oil and gas operators releasing large volumes of methane. The Department of Agriculture opened its guaranteed rural loan program to homes with income-producing accessory dwelling units. Three are final rules, effective November 30 and December 28. Two are proposals, with comment periods closing November 30 and December 16. The Register's two-day window carried 206 documents in all.

Money was not quiet while it happened. Treasury's curve closed October 28 with the thirty-year at 5.37 percent and the two-year at 4.67, a spread of 38 basis points, nine wider than the day before. The ten-year traded at 5.001 percent at seven o'clock Eastern this morning, and total public debt outstanding stood at $40.58 trillion on October 27 after a one-day increase of $53.9 billion. Outside the country, the day belonged to Spain: a state-owned Renfe high-speed train struck the rear of a stationary Iryo train near Urda on Wednesday, killing 68 people and injuring more than 100, the second deadly high-speed rail crash in Spain this year and one that arrived in the middle of a general election campaign.

## The administration and the rule of law

The window carried 206 documents for October 28 and 29: four presidential documents, 17 rules, 11 proposed rules and 174 notices. All four presidential documents are dated October 28 and were the subject of yesterday's edition, so nothing new arrived at that level today. The work was done by agencies.

The child support rule is the plainest case. [Reducing Bureaucracy and Burden for Child Support Enforcement Programs](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/29/2026-22116/reducing-bureaucracy-and-burden-for-child-support-enforcement-programs) removes what the department calls outdated or unneeded regulations across state plan, program operations, funding, performance, systems, tribal and self-assessment provisions, effective November 30. Child support enforcement runs on federal systems, performance measures and audit requirements, and a rule that removes a category of those obligations changes what a state must prove it did.

The block grant proposal applies the same method to the same department's Administration for Children and Families. [Reducing Bureaucracy and Burden for Block Grants](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/29/2026-22123/reducing-bureaucracy-and-burden-for-block-grants) would amend block grant regulations to modernize requirements and reduce administrative burden on states and tribal grantees, on the stated ground that current provisions no longer reflect statutory authority or agency practice. Comments close November 30.

The personnel rule has the widest reach. [Personnel Management in Agencies: Strategic Human Capital Management](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/29/2026-22093/personnel-management-in-agencies-strategic-human-capital-management) streams planning and reporting, strengthens the authority of each agency's chief human capital officer, updates employee surveys and aligns the workforce with executive branch priorities, effective November 30. A rule that moves authority toward the chief human capital officer is a rule about who decides whom to hire, and it lands as the same administration is reducing the size of the federal workforce.

The EPA proposal is the one most likely to be litigated. [Additional Reconsideration Standards of Performance for New, Reconstructed, and Modified Sources and Emissions Guidelines for Existing Sources: Oil and Natural Gas Sector Climate Review](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/29/2026-22082/additional-reconsideration-standards-of-performance-for-new-reconstructed-and-modified-sources-and) would rescind the Super Emitter Program and amend the standards of performance and emission guidelines for oil and gas across well-site subcategories, liquids unloading, controllers, pumps, storage vessels, leaks, associated gas and control devices. Comments close December 16.

The program deserves a paragraph before the rescission is judged. Under it, certified third parties detect methane releases of 100 kilograms an hour or more by satellite, aircraft or mobile monitor, report them to the agency, and the agency notifies the operator, which must begin an investigation within five days and report findings within fifteen ([Methane Super Emitter Program](https://www.epa.gov/compliance/methane-super-emitter-program)). That is enforcement by remote sensing rather than by inspection. It is also not running. The agency's own page says implementation is extended until January 22, 2027. The proposal would therefore retire a program that has not yet begun, which makes the practical change small and the legal question sharp: an agency withdrawing a rule it has already postponed has to explain why the record that supported the rule no longer supports it.

The housing rule is the smallest and the most concrete. [Single Family Housing Guaranteed Loan Program-Income Producing Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) Provisions](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/29/2026-22038/single-family-housing-guaranteed-loan-program-income-producing-accessory-dwelling-unit-adu) amends the Section 502 guaranteed loan program to permit financing of single family homes with income-producing accessory dwelling units, and clarifies eligibility for properties with non-commercial features used for home-based work. It takes effect December 28, the morning after the housing executive order published in the same window. The order was addressed to the mortgage agencies; this rule reaches a rural borrower who wants to finance a property carrying a second, rentable unit.

Two further proposals from the same department would revise the Mandatory Guidelines for Federal Workplace Drug Testing Programs, one for urine specimens and one for oral fluid. The remainder of the 174 notices is the ordinary machinery of a Thursday: information collections, advisory committees, airspace corrections and marine mammal authorizations.

## Markets

Wednesday closed higher across all three indexes. The S&P 500 finished at 7,743.98, up 21.38 points or 0.28 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 26,549.851, up 72.04 or 0.27 percent; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 52,475.10, up 97.53 or 0.19 percent, all as of October 28. Three indexes moving within a tenth of a percentage point of one another is what a quiet tape looks like.

This morning's prints, stamped between 6:50 and 7:00 a.m. Eastern, put the VIX at 16.99, up 0.08; the ten-year yield at 5.001 percent, down 0.044; gold at $4,039.30 an ounce, down $12.30; front-month WTI crude at $101.48 a barrel, down $1.30 or 1.26 percent; and the dollar index at 102.163, down 0.031. Crude eased while the dollar held flat, so the move in oil is a supply story rather than a currency one.

Treasury's official curve for October 28 ran 4.67 percent at two years, 5.05 at ten and 5.37 at thirty, with the 2s10s spread at +0.38. Against October 27's 4.70, 4.99 and 5.30, the front end fell three basis points while the ten- and thirty-year rose six and seven, moving the spread from +0.29 to +0.38. The steepening came from the long end, and it leaves the thirty-year 32 basis points above the ten.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,582,274,254,844 on October 27, up $53,915,315,428 from the prior business day. Debt held by the public was $32,677,379,585,771 and the intragovernmental share was $7,904,894,669,073. A one-day move of that size is a cash-management event as much as a borrowing one.

The CNBC ten-year print and Treasury's official close differ by five basis points because they measure different things: 5.001 percent is a live quote at seven o'clock this morning, and 5.05 percent is the par yield curve's close for October 28.

## The economy

The three monthly series carry no release in this window, so the readings are the ones yesterday's edition reported and they still describe the same tension. September's consumer price index stands at 336.940 on the 1982-84 base, against 334.980 in August and 333.918 in July, a one-month move of about 0.59 percent. Unemployment is 4.2 percent, up from 4.1 in August and July. Nonfarm payrolls are 159,044 thousand, a gain of 29,000 over August's 159,015 thousand. Prices are accelerating while hiring slows. That is the combination the long end priced on Wednesday, at 5.37 percent.

The next test is the October release, which is not in this window.

## Elections and the midterms

Polymarket's midterm contracts firmed toward the Democrats overnight, and the move is uniform enough to read as one shift rather than several. The House contract stands at 92.5 percent Democratic on $1,067,937 of twenty-four-hour volume, up a point from 91.5 on $347,155 the day before; the Republican side is at 7.5 percent on $1,080,740, down a point from 8.5. The Senate moved the same way and less: 67.5 percent Democratic on $157,899 against 66.5 on $457,888, with the Republican contract at 32.5 percent on $271,806.

The balance-of-power event prices the combinations separately, and it moved with the chambers. A Democratic sweep stands at 66.5 percent on $481,447, up from 65.5; a Republican sweep at 6.5 percent on $461,000, down from 7.5; a Republican Senate with a Democratic House at 27.5 percent on $139,959, up from 26.5; and a Democratic Senate with a Republican House at 0.8 percent on $196,000, down from 0.9.

In the states, Texas moved first. Democrat James Talarico stands at 68.5 percent on $135,343 against Republican Ken Paxton at 31.5 percent on $260,059, a point better for Talarico than the day before. Republican Mike Rogers holds 28.5 percent in Michigan on $121,814; Senator Dan Sullivan holds 29.5 percent in Alaska on $101,363; Democrat Xavier Becerra stands at 97.4 percent in the California governor's race on $97,875; and Democrat Richard Ojeda is at 32.5 percent in NC-09 on $88,912.

These are the venues' prices, not this publication's view, and volume is not conviction. The Republican House contract drew slightly more money than the Democratic one at a twelfth of the price, which is a cheap tail trading heavily, not a forecast that it arrives.

## The world

The crash near Urda is the day's largest event outside the United States. A state-owned Renfe high-speed train struck the rear of an Iryo train that was standing on the track, in Castilla-La Mancha south of Madrid. Sixty-eight people died and more than 100 were injured. Spain's Military Emergency Unit worked through the night among derailed carriages, heavy cranes lifted wreckage, and the authorities set up a temporary morgue at Toledo's Institute of Forensic Medicine to identify the dead ([Spain: cranes clear twisted train wreckage as rescuers work through the night](https://www.euronews.com/video/2026/10/29/spain-cranes-clear-twisted-train-wreckage-as-rescuers-work-through-the-night)).

It is the second deadly high-speed rail crash in Spain this year, and it landed in the middle of a general election campaign in which Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez is under pressure ([Europe Today: Second high-speed rail disaster in nine months leaves Spain in grief](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/29/europe-today-second-high-speed-rail-disaster-in-nine-months-leaves-spain-in-grief)). Sánchez promised a tough line on possible safety failures, which is the promise every government makes in the first day of a disaster and the one that is hardest to keep once an inquiry begins.

The inquiry will have to answer a narrow question with a wide reach: how a moving train came to strike a stationary one, and whether the systems built to prevent exactly that failed or were absent. The operators complicate the answer. Renfe is state-owned and Iryo is private, so an inquiry has two companies to examine, each with its own staff, records and maintenance logs. Which of them is answerable for what is a question the campaign will not wait to resolve.
