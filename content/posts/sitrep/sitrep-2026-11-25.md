The United States told its citizens to leave Iran immediately, in a security alert that warned "hostilities could escalate rapidly" and said Iran, the Houthis and aligned groups "may target US interests and citizens in the region, including companies and institutions." The alert, issued through the U.S. virtual embassy for Iran, put the instruction without qualification: "Do not travel to Iran for any reason. United States citizens should leave Iran immediately."

It came at the end of a week in which the war's Gulf front widened. Iranian forces struck Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait early on November 18 and hit sites in Bahrain, home of the Navy's Fifth Fleet, repeatedly; a Houthi strike on a tower in Riyadh's financial district wounded eleven people. The market read the week as risk: the volatility index stood at 30.52 before this morning's open, up 15.78 percent.

At home the Federal Register carried an executive order that states, in the open, where this administration believes the line between Congress and the executive branch falls.

## The administration and the rule of law

The register carried two presidential documents, 26 final rules, 15 proposed rules and 151 notices for the two-day window.

[Executive Order 14439, "Protecting the Executive's Privileges and Enhancing Efficiencies in Federal Contracting"](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/25/2026-24114/protecting-the-executives-privileges-and-enhancing-efficiencies-in-federal-contracting), signed November 20 and published this morning at 91 FR 76627, is the document to read. It requires federal contractors to notify the White House and the contracting agency of "any pending or future congressional request respecting the contractors' communications or work with the executive branch," and to "abide by any instructions received from the White House and contracting agency" when the executive branch determines that material is protected by executive privilege. The duty runs down to subcontractors. Agencies must coordinate with the White House and the contractors on their responses, "including the application or assertion of any relevant privileges," and the Office of Management and Budget is to write guidance on how agencies should confirm compliance.

The stated purpose is "preventing and prohibiting uncoordinated disclosures of information pursuant to congressional requests." The order argues that executive privilege is "inextricably rooted in the separation of powers," that Congress may not obtain privileged material before the executive has had a chance to assert the privilege, and that the protection extends to information shared between federal employees and contractors. For enforcement it directs agencies to consider "all appropriate actions, consistent with applicable law," among them "potential termination or cancellation of contracts and the potential debarment or suspension of contractors."

A contractor that receives a congressional request therefore holds a written instruction to take its orders from the White House, and a written list of what refusal may cost. The order does not say how a committee's compulsory process is to be answered; it says the contractor must follow the White House's instructions.

Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security [added seven companies to the Entity List](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/25/2026-23999/additions-to-the-entity-list): three in Malaysia, three in Singapore and one in Thailand. The rule cites a "significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities," imposes a license requirement on all items subject to the Export Administration Regulations, and sets a presumption of denial. Three of the seven carry the Megaspeed name, one in each country. The rule took effect November 23; it is printed at 91 FR 76148.

The Department of Health and Human Services [proposed the contract-year 2028 rule for Medicare Advantage, Part D and PACE](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/25/2026-23960/medicare-program-contract-year-2028-policy-and-technical-changes-to-the-medicare-advantage-program), at 91 FR 76314. It would create ADVaNCE MA, "the first MA mandatory model under section 1115A of the Act"; establish a 15-measure Star Ratings set, remove the reward factor and raise the bar for quality bonus payments; require any food or beverage supplemental benefit to meet a "healthy food" standard drawn from the 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines; and replace the agent and broker compensation rules that a court vacated with a market-based definition. Comments close January 19, 2027.

The rest of the list was the ordinary machinery: airworthiness directives and airspace amendments, Bering Sea fisheries actions, information collections, advisory-committee meetings, sanctions notices from Treasury and State, a rule renaming narcotic treatment programs as opioid treatment programs, and a final rule on the control of communicable diseases and foreign quarantine. The Federal Communications Commission set the procedures for Auction 115 of upper C-band licenses, with bidding to begin April 27, 2027, and the President issued a proclamation for National Adoption Month, 2026.

Monday's 4.73 percent rebound gave a fraction of itself back on Tuesday. The S&P 500 closed at 7,201.09, down 36.08 points or 0.50 percent; the Nasdaq Composite fell 356.023 points, or 1.48 percent, to 23,733.535; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 423.39 points, or 0.85 percent, to 50,018.70. A falling Nasdaq against a rising Dow is the shape of a rotation out of growth stocks rather than a broad selloff, and the morning's volatility reading does not contradict it. The rest of the board moved in smaller steps. Gold fell $49.20 to $3,512.70 and crude slipped 45 cents to $81.60, both moves that sit oddly beside a Gulf war and are worth watching for that reason. The dollar index rose 0.18 percent to 104.006.

## The economy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' newest readings are unchanged from yesterday's pack. The consumer price index for October was 337.511, against 336.940 in September and 334.980 in August, a monthly rise of 0.17 percent after September's 0.59. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September and level with August. Nonfarm payrolls reached 159,293 thousand, a gain of 165,000 on the month after September's 60,000.

Taken together, prices cooled, hiring strengthened and the jobless rate held just above four percent. That is a picture of an economy that is not contracting, and it is a month old.

## The world

The alert from the virtual embassy was issued after Iranian strikes on American assets in Bahrain and Kuwait and after the Houthi strike on a tower in Riyadh's financial district that injured eleven people. It told Americans to watch for flight cancellations and airspace closures, to expect sudden changes to land-border rules, to avoid protests and heavily policed areas, and to register with the State Department for updates.

The week's attacks, as The Associated Press reported them, were heavier than a single exchange. Iran struck Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait early on November 18, firing on air-defense systems and on drone and missile launch platforms, according to Iranian state media. Kuwait's military said its air defenses had intercepted drone attacks and did not immediately report damage, and its Foreign Ministry condemned "the repeated Iranian attacks" as criminal. AP's analysis of imagery found the base had been "struck at least 30 times by Iran," damaging or destroying at least 15 aircraft hangars and 10 buildings near a residential area. In Bahrain, the Interior Ministry issued two shelter alerts before an all clear; the military said it intercepted a number of attacks and gave no damage or casualty figures. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told a Senate committee he lacked a full damage assessment because "it's a nonpermissive area for us to go," having said earlier that Iran "blew the hell out of Bahrain."

The cycle was reciprocal. The November 18 attacks followed a U.S. fighter jet's strike the day before on a Revolutionary Guard missile site on Qeshm Island, itself retaliation for an Iranian attack on a U.S. radar installation in Bahrain. Separately, ADNOC said one of its tanker crew members was missing and eleven others were injured after an attack in the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump has said new strikes are unlikely while deploying a third carrier and more troops, and analysts quoted by the AP warned of the risk of miscalculation, saying that a limited response may encourage Iran to push further. The alert is the State Department's judgment about where that leaves American civilians: not in Iran, and ready to leave the neighborhood.
