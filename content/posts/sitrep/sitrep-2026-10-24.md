Israel sealed Hebron late Friday and by Saturday had raided at least fifteen towns in the northern West Bank, left at least 200 families outside their homes, and arrested dozens of Palestinians, days before a national election. The operation followed an attack at a checkpoint near Hebron in which two Israeli soldiers and two suspected gunmen were killed. In Washington the day was quiet: every one of the 82 Federal Register documents in the weekend window carries Friday's date, the Supreme Court filed nothing, and Friday's close was the week's last word on the markets. The S&P 500 fell 0.52 percent and the Dow 1.23 percent, the ten-year Treasury yield closed at 5.00 percent, and total public debt passed $40.44 trillion.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register published 82 documents in the October 23 to 24 window: no presidential documents, six rules, seven proposed rules and 69 notices. Saturday added none. A weekend window with no presidential document is a finding about the window, and it is reported here rather than padded.

The substantive items in Friday's issue were already reported in Friday's edition: the comptroller of the currency's final stablecoin rule, Treasury's proposal on large institutional purchases of single-family homes, the Justice and Education departments' notice withdrawing seven joint guidance documents, the SNAP electronic-benefit proposal, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission's petition on irradiated fuel reprocessing, and the SEC's annual regulatory review list. What was not reported is a notice buried among the 69: the [Federal Trade Commission's analysis of a proposed consent order in the Publicis and LiveRamp matter](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21659/publicis-and-liveramp-analysis-of-proposed-agreement-containing-consent-order-to-aid-public-comment), which opened for public comment.

The rest is routine: Gulf of Alaska fisheries actions, airworthiness directives for Boeing and MD Helicopters, an extension of the comment period on the Postal Service's environmental review procedures, a cadmium action level for baby food, and a long list of information-collection notices.

## Markets

Friday's close reversed Thursday's pattern. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 652.82 points, 1.23 percent, to 52,215.16; the S&P 500 fell 39.75 points, 0.52 percent, to 7,623.68; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 53.518 points, 0.21 percent, to 25,955.342. The VIX rose 0.42 to 19.45, up 2.21 percent, and the dollar index was 101.752, up 0.145 or 0.14 percent. Thursday's selling was concentrated in long-duration growth stocks. Friday's was concentrated in the Dow, with the Nasdaq falling least, which is the reverse of the earlier session.

Treasury's official Friday curve ran 4.69 percent at two years, 5.00 at ten and 5.33 at thirty, four basis points higher at every point than Thursday and a 2s10s spread of +0.31, unchanged. A parallel move of that size repriced the whole curve without changing its shape. The live ten-year quote was 4.999 percent, up 0.046, as of 5:05 p.m. ET Friday.

The pack's commodity prints are gold at $4,010.00 an ounce, down $50.40 or 1.24 percent, and front-month WTI crude at $103.44 a barrel, up $3.97 or 3.99 percent. Both carry a Saturday timestamp from the quote feed that corresponds to no trading session, so they are recorded as the feed's last available prints and not as a move that happened today.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,444,342,709,389 on October 22, up $30,703,697,083 from October 21. Debt held by the public was $32,597,664,070,880, and $7,846,678,638,509 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

September remains the newest reference month, and the three readings are the same ones Friday's edition reported. The consumer price index was 336.940, against 334.980 in August and 333.918 in July, a monthly rise of 0.59 percent, or roughly 7.2 percent at an annual rate. Unemployment was 4.2 percent, up from 4.1. Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 to 159.044 million.

Taken together, the readings describe prices accelerating while hiring stalls. A ten-year yield at 5.00 percent is consistent with that combination; it is not the yield of a market pricing near-term cuts.

## Elections and the midterms

Polymarket's chamber markets put the House at 91.5 percent Democratic on $308,753 of twenty-four-hour volume and the Senate at 67.5 percent Democratic on $108,255. The balance-of-power event gave a Democratic sweep 66.5 percent on $255,610, a Republican sweep 7.5 percent on $201,391, and a Republican Senate with a Democratic House 24.5 percent on $102,282.

The state races moved at the margin. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico traded at 63.5 percent against Republican Ken Paxton at 36.5, a three-point shift toward Paxton since Friday's edition. Michigan gave Democrat Abdul El-Sayed 70.5 percent against Republican Mike Rogers at 29.0, a one-point decline for El-Sayed.

These are the venues' prices, not this publication's view. A thin book is not a forecast.

## The world

Israel announced late Friday a major operation it described as counterterrorism, centered on Hebron. By Saturday raids had reached at least fifteen towns in the northern West Bank, including Nablus, Jenin, Tubas and Qalqilya. Hebron was sealed off and seven district entrances closed. Up to half of one targeted neighborhood was displaced, with at least 200 families outside their homes; bulldozers destroyed a three-story building, and families in Ein Deir Bahha were ordered to leave. Dozens of Palestinians were arrested.

The operation followed a Friday attack at a checkpoint near Hebron in which two Israeli soldiers were killed; the two suspected gunmen were shot dead. According to [Al Jazeera's report on Israel's new West Bank operation](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/24/why-is-israel-launching-a-new-military-operation-in-the-occupied-west-bank), Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered a full West Bank lockdown and said troops would go house to house. "We will act to destroy this place, to change it completely," he said. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich said the offensive should proceed "exactly like in Gaza," and National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called for the Palestinian Authority to be dismantled.

Mustafa Barghouti described the lockdown as turning cities and villages into a small prison. The UN human rights office called it "yet another episode in a never-ending stream of suffocating measures making life more and more unbearable for Palestinians," and B'Tselem warned of "collective punishment and acts that could constitute war crimes." The Palestinian Authority says more than 40,000 people have been displaced in the West Bank since early 2025.

The operation comes days before Israel's election on Tuesday. Polymarket gave Netanyahu 26.5 percent to be prime minister after the next election on $241,982 of volume.
