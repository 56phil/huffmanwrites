On Friday the President signed [an executive order waiving the new Russia sanctions package](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/17/trump-signs-executive-order-waiving-new-russia-sanctions-package), setting aside the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, a statute he had signed into law himself in September, and with it the sanctions on Russian government and military officials, on major banks, and on vessels in the shadow fleet, together with duties on Russian goods and on goods from nations that still buy Russian oil and gas. In return, Russia is to supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel to the American and global market immediately, 500,000 tons in November, and larger volumes after that. The tonnages were announced by the President himself, on Truth Social. Moscow's envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the pause "a significant and truly historic moment" and said the package it suspends could have "brought economic cooperation between Russia and the US to a complete standstill." Volodymyr Zelenskyy, in Brussels, said Ukraine "can't accept diesel deals, because gas deals will come next," and warned that relief would spread to banking, to individuals, and to chemicals. Within the same twenty-four hours a supertanker burned in the Strait of Hormuz, and front-month crude closed at $101.84, up 3.95 percent.

## The administration and the rule of law

The window carries one Register issue and no more, because the Register publishes on business days and Saturday is not one. Friday's issue runs to 129 documents: no presidential documents, twelve rules, four proposed rules, and 113 notices. The executive order above has not reached the Register yet, which is why the count of presidential documents is zero rather than one.

The rules are routine by their own titles. Treasury finalized the [non-applicability of section 357(c) to consolidated groups](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/16/2026-21253/non-applicability-of-section-357c-to-consolidated-groups); three Commerce rules reallocated Alaskan fishery quotas; Transportation established an area navigation route and restricted areas; Homeland Security closed out a set of local marine-event regulations. The four proposed rules carry more weight, and this report described the two that matter in yesterday's edition: the Environmental Protection Agency's [reallocation of additional 2025 small refinery exemptions](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/16/2026-21155/renewable-fuel-standard-rfs-program-reallocation-of-additional-2025-small-refinery-exemptions) under the Renewable Fuel Standard, and the Labor Department's [proposal to require powered air purifying respirators in underground mines](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/16/2026-21154/powered-air-purifying-respirators-paprs-in-underground-mines).

Three things in the notices are worth naming. Interior published sixteen notices of inventory completion or intended repatriation, the filings by which museums and universities account for holdings that a repatriation claim has reached. Harvard, Yale, the University of Pennsylvania, Michigan State, Indiana, and UCLA are among the institutions named. Treasury carried a [notice of an OFAC sanctions action](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/16/2026-21212/notice-of-ofac-sanctions-action), which sits oddly beside the waiver above, and which the Register does not describe beyond its title. And a cluster of exchange filings at the Securities and Exchange Commission rewrites halts, speed bumps, kill switches, and clearly erroneous execution rules for [twenty-three-hour, five-day trading](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/16/2026-21137/self-regulatory-organizations-the-nasdaq-stock-market-llc-notice-of-filing-and-immediate), from Nasdaq, the Long-Term Stock Exchange, and the options exchanges. The market is being rebuilt to run almost continuously, in the same week that a war moved the price of oil four percent in a session.

## Markets

Every figure below is the last print the pack carries from Friday's session, because the equity and Treasury markets were closed for the weekend when this edition was written. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 52,002.41, down 175.28 points or 0.34 percent. The S&P 500 closed at 7,659.44, down 84.62 or 1.09 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,140.034, down 572.992 or 2.15 percent. The CBOE volatility index stood at 19.99, up 3.53 or 21.45 percent. Gold was $4,186.70 an ounce, up $16.60 or 0.40 percent; front-month crude $101.84 a barrel, up $3.87 or 3.95 percent; the dollar index 101.779, down 0.276 or 0.27 percent. The ten-year note yielded 5.081 percent.

The decline was not general. A Nasdaq that falls twice as far as the S&P and six times as far as the Dow is a fall concentrated in long-duration equities, the names most exposed to a ten-year note above five percent, and that is the arithmetic of a discount rate rather than a growth scare. Oil was rising for a different reason, in a different market, on news that arrived after the closing bell.

The Treasury's curve added its October 16 row: 4.74 percent at two years, 5.09 at ten, and 5.43 at thirty, against 4.77, 5.10, and 5.43 the day before. The two-year fell three basis points while the long end held, which widened the two-to-ten spread from 33 basis points to 35.

The federal debt stood at $40,388,317,675,072 on October 15, an increase of $59.09 billion from the prior business day. Debt held by the public was $32.54 trillion of that total, and $7.85 trillion was intragovernmental. A single day's borrowing of $59 billion against a ten-year yield of 5.081 percent is the arithmetic of this section that does not move with the news.

## The world

Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said Saturday that a supertanker caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz after striking a sea mine. Through Iranian state media, the IRGC navy said the vessel carried more than two million barrels of oil and struck the mine while "attempting to pass through an unauthorised route," producing a "massive explosion" and a "fierce fire." It said the tanker had shut down its positioning and radar systems to avoid detection, and the Fars news agency published video that appeared to show the ship burning. The Iranian account did not give a location, and no casualty figure was reported.

The claim of jurisdiction is the part that matters. The IRGC navy declared the strait closed and warned of "burning in the fire of trust in the terrorist US army." On Friday the commander of CENTCOM, Admiral Brad Cooper, had put it the other way: "Let me be clear, the Strait of Hormuz is controlled by the United States and our regional partners, not Iran," he said, adding, "It remains open for business." The strait normally carries about a quarter of the world's seaborne oil trade. The United States has blockaded Iranian ports since Iran began obstructing passage after the American and Israeli strikes of late February. A tanker burning in the channel is a direct test of which account is true, and the two governments now have to live with whichever one the next hull settles.
