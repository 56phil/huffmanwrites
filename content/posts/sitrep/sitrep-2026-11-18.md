The Senate count is finished. [NBC News called Alaska's seat for Mary Peltola](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/mary-peltola-democrat-wins-alaska-senate-dan-sullivan-gop-rcna603307) on Tuesday night, and the [official ballot count reported Wednesday](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261118-alaska-democrat-flips-us-senate-seat-in-tight-race-official-count) put her at 144,302 votes to Dan Sullivan's 131,844, a margin of 12,458. Because she cleared 50 percent on the first ballot, the state's ranked-choice tabulation never ran, and the Senate will hold a 56-44 Democratic majority. Two weeks of counting ended in a result that settles the chamber and, with it, the shape of the next Congress.

The day's other movements were smaller but not quiet. Equities fell for a second session, the ten-year yield held above five percent, and Donald Trump arrived in China for a trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin at the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register published 211 documents for November 17 and 18: no presidential documents, twenty rules, fifteen proposed rules and 176 notices. The empty presidential column is a finding about the window, not a gap. No proclamation, order or determination reached the register in these two days.

Treasury and the Internal Revenue Service finalized the [automatic-enrollment requirements under Section 414A](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23530/automatic-enrollment-requirements-under-section-414a), providing guidance on the rule that the SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022 added for certain cash-or-deferred arrangements and salary-reduction agreements. The regulations, TD 10061, take effect January 19, 2027. The Federal Reserve, in the same register, [indexed Regulation D](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23453/reserve-requirements-of-depository-institutions) for 2027: the reserve requirement exemption stays at $39.2 million, and the low reserve tranche rises to $688.5 million from $674.1 million, effective December 18. No institution's requirement changes, because the Board set every reserve ratio to zero in March 2020.

Three other actions deserve a line. The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23435/safety-standard-for-button-cell-or-coin-batteries-and-consumer-products-containing-such-batteries) updated the mandatory safety standard for button cell and coin batteries to ANSI/UL 4200A-2026, effective February 13, 2027. The [Copyright Office](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23415/copyright-office-fees) raised its fees for registration, recordation, search, certification and licensing services, the first adjustment since 2020, effective January 5, 2027. The [Nuclear Regulatory Commission](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23395/in-the-matter-of-triso-x-llc-confirmatory-order-modifying-license) issued a confirmatory order to TRISO-X, LLC, making binding the corrective actions the company agreed to in mediation over management measures for structural stability of the TX-1 process building's main force resisting system during construction; the order is effective on issuance.

Among the proposals, the IRS [added a hearing date](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23533/racial-nondiscrimination-in-private-schools-hearing) for its September rule that would deny tax-exempt status to private schools discriminating on the basis of race, color, or national or ethnic origin. Labor proposed a [QPAM re-eligibility exemption](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23465/proposed-qpam-re-eligibility-options-after-disqualification-qroad-class-prohibited-transaction) that would let certain qualified professional asset managers continue relying on PTE 84-14 after an event that would otherwise disqualify them, subject to protective conditions. And the EPA released its [draft policy assessment for the lead air-quality standards](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23463/release-of-draft-policy-assessment-in-the-review-of-the-lead-national-ambient-air-quality-standards) for public review.

Two notices carry more than housekeeping. Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control [published the designation](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23456/notice-of-ofac-sanctions-action) of Los Huistas Drug Trafficking Organization, based in Huehuetenango, Guatemala, as a blocked entity linked to the Sinaloa Cartel and Cartel de Jalisco Nueva Generacion; the action was issued November 13 under Executive Orders 14059 and 13224. And the State Department [delegated its functions relating to the National Endowment for Democracy](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/18/2026-23462/functions-of-the-department-of-state-relating-to-the-national-endowment-for-democracy), including audits, Freedom of Information Act compliance and grant administration, to the Under Secretary for Public Diplomacy.

## The courts

The Supreme Court filed nothing in the window. The D.C. Circuit filed one opinion, [Sean Nealy v. John Phelan](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/11063174/sean-nealy-v-john-phelan/), No. 25-5175, on November 17. This run could not read the opinion text, so what it holds is not stated here.

## Markets

Tuesday's close was lower for a second session. The S&P 500 fell 76.06 points, or 1.03 percent, to 7,340.53; the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 848.75, or 1.64 percent, to 50,759.42; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 181.375, or 0.74 percent, to 24,328.383.

This morning the VIX printed 24.19 at 6:59 eastern, down 4.99 percent. The ten-year yielded 5.105 percent at 7:00. Gold was $3,763.80 an ounce at 6:50, down 2.60 percent; WTI crude $85.11 a barrel at 6:49, down 1.05 percent; and the dollar index 103.84 at 7:00, up 0.34 percent.

Treasury's curve for November 17 ran 4.80 percent at two years, 5.10 at ten and 5.46 at thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.30, against +0.29 the day before. Total public debt outstanding was $40,662,417,395,617 on November 16, up $45,738,568,525 from November 13. Of that, $32,852,881,286,593 was held by the public and $7,809,536,109,025 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

No new release has landed since the October figures. The all-items consumer price index was 337.511 for October, up from 336.940 in September, a monthly rise of about 0.17 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September and level with August. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September. Prices rose slowly, hiring held, and one month is not a trend.

## Elections and the midterms

The count is final. Mississippi certified its result last week, and Alaska's official tally on Wednesday closed the Senate at 56-44. What remains is the market's read, and it has moved on to the leadership questions.

Polymarket's most-traded midterm question was the next Senate majority leader, with Chuck Schumer at 90.5 percent on $32,817 of twenty-four-hour volume and Chris Van Hollen at 8.1. The same venue priced Hakeem Jeffries at 99.3 percent to be speaker after the midterms, House turnout of 115 to 120 million at 79.5, and a Democratic House popular-vote margin of 14 to 16 points at 93.6. Those are prices, not calls.

## The world

Donald Trump arrived in China on Wednesday, a day before a trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, and [Xi greeted him on arrival](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/11/18/trump-arrives-in-china-ahead-of-trilateral-summit-with-xi-putin). The summit's economic agenda turns on the trade truce between Washington and Beijing and whether it will be extended beyond January, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/18/president-xi-says-russia-and-china-should-lead-push-to-multipolar-world).

Xi met Putin on the sidelines the same day and said the two countries should "lead and advance the trend towards a multipolar world." Putin described the West as "former hegemons" and said Europe was in "humiliating decline," arguing that sanctions and export restrictions "are incapable of halting growth – let alone crushing the economies of competitors." The analyst Robert Kelly, quoted by Al Jazeera, expected little agreement: the leaders arrive with "fairly different expectations and desires," he said, and an arms-control deal "just won't happen."

The meeting follows the Ukrainian strike that brought down the road span of the Kerch bridge and Russia's retaliation against Kyiv, reported in Tuesday's edition. Whether the three can turn a shared stage into a settlement is the question the week will answer.
