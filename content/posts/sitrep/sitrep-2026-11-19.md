The three leaders met. Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin sat together at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on Thursday for the first trilateral meeting of the three, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, with Ukraine, Iran, nuclear weapons and trade on the agenda. No agreement was announced. It was the meeting toward which the week had been building, and it closed a stretch in which the Senate count ended, the ten-year yield held above five percent, and the Federal Register filled with rules that reach a household's insurance premiums and its medical privacy.

The day's sharpest domestic movement was in the equity market, and it cut two ways. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 561.25 points, or 1.11 percent, to 50,198.17, while the Nasdaq Composite rose 101.502 points, or 0.42 percent, to 24,429.885. The S&P 500 slipped 16.90 points, or 0.23 percent, to 7,323.63.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register published 210 documents for November 18 and 19: twenty-two rules, sixteen proposed rules and 172 notices, with no presidential document in the window. The empty presidential column is a finding about these two days, not a gap in the record.

The most consequential rule came from Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security. An interim final rule, [Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23529/addressing-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors), took effect on publication. It restricts exports, reexports and in-country transfers involving overseas data centres that remotely provide substantial compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau, requires enhanced customer due diligence, and adds licence requirements for advanced semiconductors shipped to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. It also formally rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule of the previous administration, which the document notes was never enforced.

HHS finalized its [HIPAA Privacy Rule changes](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23429/hipaa-privacy-rule-changes-to-support-coordinated-care-and-individual-engagement-and-reduce), effective January 19, 2027, to support value-based care and care coordination, to encourage individual engagement, and to reduce regulatory burdens. The SEC proposed an [Executive Compensation Disclosure Reform](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23555/executive-compensation-disclosure-reform), a principles-based rewrite that simplifies the summary tables and rationalizes the compensation discussion and analysis, the pay ratio, the pay-versus-performance and perquisite disclosures. FinCEN proposed [customer due diligence requirements for covered financial institutions](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23614/customer-due-diligence-requirements-for-covered-financial-institutions), directed by Executive Order 14406, which would require institutions to collect enough information to verify the beneficial owners of legal entity customers and, where relevant to risk, additional information including immigration status and employment authorization. Comments on both proposals close January 19, 2027.

Two CMS notices set next year's Medicare figures, effective January 1, 2027. The [Part B standard monthly premium](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23601/medicare-program-medicare-part-b-monthly-actuarial-rates-premium-rates-and-annual-deductible) rises to $209.40 and the annual deductible to $292.00. The [Part A premium](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23604/medicare-program-cy-2027-part-a-premiums-for-the-uninsured-aged-and-for-certain-disabled-individuals) for uninsured people aged 65 and over who are not otherwise eligible is $597 a month, with $328 for certain other individuals. CMS also proposed [Comprehensive Regulations To Uncover Suspicious Healthcare (CRUSH)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23461/medicare-medicaid-and-marketplace-programs-comprehensive-regulations-to-uncover-suspicious), which would change enrollment, medical review and investigation requirements across Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP and the Marketplace to strengthen fraud prevention.

One cluster in the notices is worth a line. The Small Business Administration issued nine administrative disaster declarations on Thursday, covering California, Texas, Georgia, Tennessee, Ohio and Minnesota, with California and Ohio each receiving more than one.

## The courts

The Supreme Court and the D.C. Circuit each filed nothing in the window, so the appellate week was quiet.

## Markets

The S&P 500 closed lower for a third consecutive session, down 16.90 points, or 0.23 percent, to 7,323.63. The Dow fell 561.25, or 1.11 percent, to 50,198.17, and the Nasdaq rose 101.502, or 0.42 percent, to 24,429.885.

This morning the VIX printed 22.08 at 6:59 eastern, down 10.10 percent. The ten-year yielded 5.089 percent at 7:00. Gold was $3,727.50 an ounce at 6:50, down 0.38 percent; WTI crude $82.95 a barrel at 6:49, down 0.37 percent; and the dollar index 103.706 at 7:00, down 0.15 percent.

Treasury's curve for November 18 ran 4.80 percent at two years, 5.07 at ten and 5.41 at thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.27, down from +0.30 the day before. Total public debt outstanding was $40,714,946,316,573 on November 17, up $52,528,920,956 from the prior business day. Of that, $32,887,006,494,500 was held by the public and $7,827,939,822,074 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

No new release has landed since the October figures. The all-items consumer price index was 337.511 for October, up from 336.940 in September, a monthly rise of about 0.17 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September and level with August. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September. Prices rose slowly, hiring held, and one month is not a trend.

## Elections and the midterms

The count is final. Mississippi certified its result last week, and Alaska's tally closed the Senate at 56-44, as Wednesday's edition reported. What remains is the market's read.

Polymarket's most-traded midterm question was the next Senate majority leader, with Chuck Schumer at 94.5 percent on $68,605 of twenty-four-hour volume and Chris Van Hollen at 4.8. The same venue priced Hakeem Jeffries at 99.0 percent to be speaker after the midterms, a Democratic House popular-vote margin of 14 to 16 points at 91.3, House turnout of 115 to 120 million at 77.0, and Mississippi as the closest Senate race at 98.8. Those are prices, not calls.

## The world

Trump and Putin met first at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on Thursday, for forty minutes, and then joined Xi for the trilateral session. [Euronews reported](https://www.euronews.com/video/2026/11/19/china-trump-calls-putin-his-friend-as-leaders-meet-amid-in-shenzhen) that Trump, approaching Putin beside the Russian flag, joked "Who is he?" and then called him "my friend," and said on camera that the two had shared a great relationship for many years and that he hoped a lot of wonderful things would come from the session. Putin, in his opening remarks, said the three countries "can strengthen global stability if they cooperate constructively," and that he had discussed Ukraine with Trump during their closed-door meeting, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/11/19/putin-says-us-china-russia-can-strengthen-global-stability).

No agreement was announced. The [agenda as the venue reported it](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/19/watch-trump-putin-and-xi-sit-down-for-historic-three-way-talks) covered Ukraine, the Iran conflict, nuclear weapons and trade. Euronews noted that the two leaders' previous in-person meeting, in Alaska in 2025, did not yield a breakthrough, and that Trump's warmth toward Putin comes while Russia's war in Ukraine continues.

The war did not pause for the meeting. Three Russian strikes hit Odesa's historic centre on Wednesday morning, killing four people and wounding at least 13, [the city's military administration said](https://www.euronews.com/video/2026/11/19/ukraine-four-killed-and-at-least-13-injured-in-russian-strike-on-odesa), with the toll still being tallied.
