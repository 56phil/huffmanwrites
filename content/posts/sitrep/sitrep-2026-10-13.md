President Trump announced on Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an "energy ceasefire," effective immediately, and by Tuesday the announcement had bought less than two days of quiet. Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelensky, said Russia struck six Ukrainian energy facilities in the hours after the truce was declared, injuring 33 people, and he told Fox News the timing was "Putin's answer to President Trump." On Monday evening a drone hit a 25-story residential building in Kyiv's Darnytskyi district, killing one person and injuring 11, the capital's mayor, Vitali Klitschko, said. Russia's foreign minister, Sergei Lavrov, said Ukraine does not deserve the "respite" a ceasefire would bring. The same day, drones struck two merchant ships off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast, one of them sinking with 11 crew members missing, and Bulgaria, a NATO member, summoned Russia's ambassador. Oil fell on the news: West Texas Intermediate traded at $91.83 a barrel, down 2.04 percent, as of 6:50 a.m. Eastern.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Register's Tuesday issue carries 103 documents, every one of them dated today, which is what the window looks like when a federal holiday sits inside it. Two are presidential. [Proclamation 11072, "National Energy Dominance Month, 2026,"](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20969/national-energy-dominance-month-2026) and [Proclamation 11073, "Columbus Day, 2026,"](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20972/columbus-day-2026) were both signed October 7 and appear today at 91 FR 65073 and 91 FR 65075.

The energy proclamation is the one to read beside the day's other news. It declares that the administration has made the United States "the most dominant energy superpower in the history of the world," counts more than 6,000 drilling permits approved, the February rescission of the Obama-era Endangerment Finding, and "the largest oil deal in all of human history," which it says was secured last month. It lands in a week when Washington has been pressing Kyiv to stop striking Russian oil infrastructure so that a Russian diesel shipment can reach the American market, an arrangement Ukraine's president has called a "gift" to Vladimir Putin.

The rest of the issue is heavy with housekeeping and light on action, and three items are worth naming. The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control published two determinations it had issued earlier, which is the Register's habit rather than new policy: a [sector determination extending Executive Order 13902 to Iran's automotive and rail sectors](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20839/publication-of-an-iran-related-determination), effective October 1 and signed by the office's director, Bradley T. Smith, and [Venezuela sanctions General License 60](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20831/publication-of-venezuela-sanctions-regulations-web-general-license-60), which authorizes transactions for earthquake relief in Venezuela through October 23. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission opened an [advance notice of proposed rulemaking on crypto asset transactions and markets](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20888/regulation-crypto-asset-transactions-and-regulation-crypto-asset-markets), seeking comment on the rules it calls "fit-for-purpose" under section 2(c)(2)(D) of the Commodity Exchange Act. The Environmental Protection Agency finalized [a delay of the compliance date for Clean Water Act hazardous-substance facility response plans](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20849/clean-water-act-hazardous-substance-facility-response-plans-compliance-date-delay-and-changes-to), recasting the rule to reflect administration policy, and separately [approved Colorado's application for primary enforcement authority over Class VI injection wells](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20846/colorado-underground-injection-control-uic-program-class-vi-primacy), the wells used for geologic carbon storage. The Marine Minerals Administration, which now carries the former Bureau of Ocean Energy Management's authorities, opened comment on a [proposed notice of sale for Cook Inlet Lease Sale 2](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/13/2026-20892/notice-of-availability-of-the-proposed-notice-of-sale-for-the-cook-inlet-outer-continental-shelf-oil) under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act; comments are due December 14 and the bid opening is set for March 3, 2027.

The remaining notices are the ordinary traffic of a Tuesday: a dozen exchange rule filings from the Cboe and Nasdaq venues, fisheries actions from Commerce, information-collection renewals, airworthiness directives, and a proposed rule from Homeland Security on advance traveler information for rail.

## The courts

The courts were quiet at the top and steady at the bottom. Neither the Supreme Court nor the D.C. Circuit filed an opinion in the window. ## Markets

Monday's stock session closed mixed: the S&P 500 at 7,807.24, down 4.30 points or 0.06 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 27,245.902, down 0.44 percent; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 51,850.55, up 195.60 points or 0.38 percent, each as of October 12. The CBOE volatility index stood at 15.53, up 2.10 percent, and the dollar index at 102.30, down 0.28 percent, both as of 7:00 a.m. Eastern today. Gold was $4,204.30 an ounce, up 0.33 percent, as of 6:50 a.m. Eastern.

Oil was the day's mover. West Texas Intermediate traded at $91.83 a barrel, down 2.04 percent, as of 6:50 a.m. Eastern, on a morning whose news was an escalation rather than a de-escalation. The 10-year Treasury note yielded 5.189 percent, down 5.5 basis points, as of 7:00 a.m. Eastern.

The Treasury's own daily curve is current through October 9, the last business day before the holiday; Monday is not in it. It reads 4.80 percent at two years, 5.24 percent at ten, and 5.60 percent at thirty, a two-to-ten spread of 44 basis points against 47 the day before.

The federal debt stood at $40,305,316,210,830 on October 8, up $21.28 billion from the prior business day. Debt held by the public was $32.45 trillion, and $7.85 trillion of the total was intragovernmental.

## The economy

The three series carry the reference months they have carried all week, so this window produced no new reading. The consumer price index for all urban consumers was 334.980 in August, against 333.918 in July and 333.952 in June. The unemployment rate was 4.2 percent in September, a tenth of a point above the 4.1 percent readings of July and August. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,044 thousand in September, against 159,015 thousand in August and 158,882 thousand in July, which puts the September gain near 29,000 against about 133,000 in August.

Read together, the three still describe a labor market that is cooling rather than breaking. September's payroll gain is the smallest of the three months shown, unemployment is at its highest, and consumer prices were roughly flat across the summer. One month is not a trend, and a week of the same numbers is not a longer one.

## The world

Bulgaria's Black Sea coast became the war's newest front on Tuesday. Drones struck two merchant ships, and the Bulgarian prime minister, Rumen Radev, summoned Russia's ambassador to account for it.

One vessel sank 17 nautical miles, or 31 kilometers, east of the village of Krapets, with 11 crew members missing and one sailor rescued, local officials said. The other, a Ukrainian-owned ship, was hit 18 nautical miles from the town of Byala, and all 15 of its crew were rescued. Radev identified that vessel as the Maltese-flagged Chelsea 3, registered in Ukraine's Odesa, and said five of its crew were being treated for minor injuries. He convened an emergency meeting and said that though no conclusive evidence pointed to either side, "the logic suggests that the outcome benefits the Russian Federation." The ambassador, Eleonora Mitrofanova, was quoted by Bulgarian media as telling him she had no information from Russia's defense ministry about the attacks, and that a third party could have staged a provocation.

The attack is the second in a week. Two commercial ships were struck by sea and aerial drones in Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone on October 6; one, the Alfa Watan, sank, and all ten of its crew, nine of them Syrian, are presumed dead. Radev had asked NATO and the European Union for technical help investigating that one.

The alliance answered within the day. NATO's secretary general, Mark Rutte, said after speaking with Radev that the alliance "will continue to work closely with our allies in the Black Sea" and had "already strengthened our posture along the eastern flank." The European Commission's president, Ursula von der Leyen, called the Black Sea "another front of escalation of the Russian war in Ukraine. This is unacceptable." Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, said the attack showed "Moscow's deliberate disregard for civilian lives and maritime security in the Black Sea," and that "Russia is attacking it to weaponise hunger and hold the world hostage." Turkey has asked both countries to stop attacking shipping in the sea. Moscow says it targets vessels carrying weapons and dual-use cargo, and has not commented publicly on Tuesday's strikes.

The venues price the wider war at arm's length. A [ceasefire between the United States and Iran continuing through October 31](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events?slug=us-iran-ceasefire-continues-throughptptpt) stands at 80.5 percent on $180,746, a [ceasefire with the Houthis through October 15](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events?slug=us-yemen-ceasefire-continues-throughptptpt) at 98.2 percent on $544,135, and a market on [Vladimir Putin leaving the Russian presidency by December 31, 2026](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/events?slug=putin-out-before-2027) at 4.2 percent on $259,314. Where the venues can see a truce, they price it as durable. The one announced for this war lasted two days.
