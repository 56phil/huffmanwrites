A divided panel of the D.C. Circuit held on Tuesday that twenty-seven Christian and Jewish organizations are likely to have standing to challenge the rescission of the policy that kept immigration enforcement away from houses of worship. The court reversed the district judge, 2–1, and sent the case back for a fresh look at a preliminary injunction. That same day, the Democratic campaign committees and the two Democratic congressional leaders filed a new complaint against the administration over what it describes as a pattern of intimidation aimed at state election officials. The financial backdrop moved the other way from Monday's fear: front-month WTI crude was $99.92 a barrel at 6:50 a.m. ET, after trading at $110.00 yesterday morning, and the ten-year Treasury held at 5.125 percent.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register's two-day window holds 145 documents: one presidential document, six rules, ten proposed rules and 128 notices.

The presidential document is the day's substantial one. [Executive Order 14436](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/21/2026-21599/implementing-the-lindsey-o-graham-sanctioning-russia-and-iran-act-of-2026), signed October 16 and published this morning, implements the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, which became law on September 18. The order raises duties on goods imported from Russia by 500 percent ad valorem. It raises them by 100 percent on goods from the five largest importers of Russian crude oil and natural gas if those countries knowingly make new purchases on or after October 18, and by another 100 percent on goods from the top five facilitators of Russian oil sanctions evasion. The increases apply on top of duties under the Tariff Act of 1930 and sections 122, 201 and 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 and section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962.

Then the order waives most of what it has just authorized. It waives duties under sections 112 and 113 of the Act and under the order itself, sanctions and restrictions under section 102(d) while preserving the measures under the prior Russia national-emergency order continued in March, and the measures in sections 102(a), 102(e) and 103 through 111, along with section 117. The waivers continue until the President or the Secretary of State determines that further action is warranted. Set beside yesterday's report on the diesel waiver, the pattern is consistent: the statutory machinery is built and then suspended, which leaves the threat of it as the instrument.

The rest of the window is thinner. Treasury proposed a partial withdrawal of guidance under section 355 concerning device and active trade or business. The National Credit Union Administration issued three proposals, on notification to members and shareholders, on a nonconforming investment special reserve, and on conversion to a state-chartered credit union. The SEC's interval fund modernization proposal, published Tuesday, would extend multiple share classes to registered closed-end management investment companies and business development companies. The final rules were routine: Interior's annual civil penalty inflation adjustments, an FAA airspace incorporation-by-reference amendment, and security and safety zones at Puget Sound and Honolulu.

Treasury also published three OFAC sanctions notices across the window. Homeland Security gave notice of an order excluding products developed by Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence and its affiliates from federal civilian procurement and requiring their removal from civilian agency systems, including third-party products built on DeepSeek, docketed as DHS-2026-0628. It is a procurement action, not a rule.

## The courts

The Supreme Court filed nothing in the window. The D.C. Circuit filed one opinion, [Mennonite Church USA v. DHS](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/11039944/mennonite-church-usa-v-dhs/), No. 25-5209, and it was the day's most consequential ruling.

Judge Florence Pan wrote for the majority, joined by Judge Robert Wilkins; Judge Neomi Rao dissented, as [DC Circuit sides with religious groups in fight to block ICE church raids](https://www.courthousenews.com/dc-circuit-sides-with-religious-groups-in-fight-to-block-ice-church-raids/) reports. The panel reversed Judge Dabney Friedrich's denial of a preliminary injunction, holding that the plaintiffs likely have standing, and remanded for her to reconsider the request. Pan wrote that the plaintiffs "have direct evidence that the government intends to increase immigration enforcement at sensitive locations" and that this was "the whole point of the Rescission Memo." Rao answered that "redressability requires more than conjecture" and that the "guidance they seek to restore has no effect on the executive branch's legal authority or enforcement discretion." The ruling does not decide the policy challenge; it reopens the courthouse door.

Fifteen new complaints were docketed in the District of Columbia. The one that matters most is [DSCC v. Trump](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74994590/dscc-v-trump/), 1:26-cv-03674, filed by the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee, the Democratic Governors Association, the Nevada Democratic Party, Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer, House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries and a naturalized California voter. The defendants are President Trump, Attorney General Todd Blanche, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, the Justice Department and the Department of Homeland Security. The complaint alleges that threats to prosecute election officials, including a reported warning that California election officials would "go to jail for a long period of time," violate section 11(b) of the Voting Rights Act, which forbids voter intimidation. It asks for an order barring the administration from pressuring states to purge voters or withhold ballots.

Other filings of note include three complaints by Protect the Public's Trust against the Departments of Transportation, the Interior and Commerce, Public Citizen v. National Park Service, The Marshall Project v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and the Center for Biological Diversity v. the Department of Energy. The captions are the record so far.

Of the three watched dockets, none recorded a filing in the window: [Beatty v. Trump](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72069932/beatty-v-trump/), the Kennedy Center case; [Phang v. Blanche](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73246595/phang-v-blanche/) in the district court; and the consolidated [Phang v. Blanche appeal](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74696600/katie-phang-v-todd-blanche/) in the D.C. Circuit.

## Markets

Tuesday's close was mixed but firm. The S&P 500 finished at 7,712.57, up 21.84 points or 0.28 percent. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 529.85 points, 1.02 percent, to 52,495.88. The Nasdaq Composite slipped 6.069 points, 0.02 percent, to 26,424.817.

This morning's board is calmer than yesterday's. The VIX was 17.38, up 0.40 percent, as of 6:59:46 a.m. ET. Gold was $4,145.20 an ounce, up 0.06 percent, as of 6:50:15. The dollar index stood at 101.682, down 0.02 percent, as of 7:00:16. Front-month WTI crude was $99.92, up $2.10 or 2.15 percent on the session, as of 6:50:13. The ten-year Treasury was quoted at 5.125 percent, up 0.008, as of 7:00:07.

The crude print needs its context. Yesterday morning the same contract was $110.00. The session change is positive because Tuesday's settlement was lower than Monday's spike; measured from yesterday's morning print, the barrel has fallen about 9 percent. The morning's small gain is a bounce from a lower settlement, not a return to Monday's level.

Treasury's Tuesday curve ran 4.79 percent on the two-year, 5.12 on the ten-year and 5.42 on the thirty-year. The 2s10s spread was +0.33 percentage points, five basis points flatter than Monday's +0.38. Total public debt outstanding was $40,396,401,120,485 on October 19, up $8,892,325,253 from October 16. Debt held by the public was $32,533,474,452,929, and $7,862,926,667,556 of the total was intragovernmental.

## The economy

September is still the newest reference month, and the three readings are unchanged from yesterday's edition. The consumer price index was 336.940, against 334.980 in August and 333.918 in July, a rise of 0.6 percent in the month and 0.9 percent over two. Unemployment was 4.2 percent, up from 4.1. Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 to 159.044 million, after 133,000 in August. Prices at that two-month pace would annualize near 5.5 percent while hiring has slowed almost to a stop. The ten-year at 5.125 percent is the market's answer to that pair, and it is not a generous one.

## Elections and the midterms

Polymarket's midterm book drifted a little further toward the Democrats overnight. The balance-of-power market gave a Democratic sweep 63.5 percent, up from 62.5 percent in yesterday's edition, a Republican Senate with a Democratic House 27.5 percent, and a Republican sweep 8.5 percent. The House control market gave Democrats 91.5 percent and Republicans 8.5 percent. The Senate control market gave Democrats 64.5 percent and Republicans 35.5 percent. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico traded at 64.5 percent and Republican Ken Paxton at 35.5 percent, from 62.5 and 38.5 yesterday. Ohio's Democratic gubernatorial candidate, Amy Acton, stood at 78.5 percent, and Alaska's Senator Dan Sullivan at 25.5 percent.

These are the venues' prices, not this publication's view, and a thin book is not a forecast. [Democrats sue to block Trump admin from threatening the midterms](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/democrats-sue-to-block-trump-admin-from-threatening-the-midterms/) is the part of the election story that no market can price: it asks a court to rule, before the ballots are cast, on whether the administration's pressure on state officials is lawful.

## The world

Steve Witkoff arrived at Pulkovo Airport in St Petersburg on Wednesday and was met by Kirill Dmitriev, the Kremlin's presidential envoy, before a scheduled meeting with President Vladimir Putin. The trip followed Tuesday's talks with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Oslo, which Secretary of State Marco Rubio called "positive." Rubio said Witkoff would go to St Petersburg "armed with those ideas" and added, "let's see if we can make some progress." Jared Kushner took part in the Oslo talks virtually because of illness and was expected to join the Putin meeting remotely.

Zelenskyy said afterward that "very, very important" intelligence from the United States had been restored, after he had accused Washington of suspending intelligence sharing. He also accused Washington of slowing Starshield, the military-grade satellite service, and Rubio rejected that, saying Trump had "nothing to do with" the decisions. Elon Musk disputed that account, saying only the military and the president can make such calls, while the Wall Street Journal reported, citing American officials, that Musk ordered the slowdown.

The fighting did not pause for the shuttle diplomacy. Yegor Kovalchuk, governor of Russia's Bryansk region, said a Ukrainian drone attack killed one person and wounded two. Ukraine's Emergency Service said two people died in a Russian strike on a high-rise in Kyiv's Sviatoshinsky district. No agreement is reported, and the core disputes over intelligence, Starshield and who speaks for Kyiv remain open.
