China's Central Commission for Discipline Inspection is investigating Chen Jining, the party secretary of Shanghai and a member of the Politburo, for suspected violations of party discipline. The Associated Press reported on Wednesday that the commission had announced the investigation on Tuesday and given no further detail.

The Shanghai city government said the Standing Committee of the Shanghai Committee of the Communist Party "expressed firm support" for the decision and called it "a clear and powerful signal that the fight against corruption would continue without pause or retreat," carrying "a firm determination to win."

Mingda Qiu, a senior analyst at Eurasia Group, put the investigation in the campaign's sequence: "The takedown again shows that Xi Jinping's anti-corruption campaign has no limits. This is the fourth Politburo member to fall, and the fourth sitting member brought down in the current cycle. The pattern is unprecedented."

The AP describes Chen as "the latest senior official targeted in President Xi Jinping's long-running anti-corruption campaign, which has shown no signs of letting up in scope or depth," and places the purges "on a scale not seen since Mao Zedong's rule."

At home the day was quiet in the places this report watches. The Federal Register's November 26 issue had not appeared by the collector's 06:00 fetch; the district court in Washington recorded fourteen new complaints; and the bond market flattened while the volatility index fell.

One notice from yesterday's list deserves the line it did not get. ## The economy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' newest readings are the October figures, unchanged from yesterday's pack. The consumer price index was 337.511, against 336.940 in September and 334.980 in August, a monthly rise of 0.17 percent after September's 0.59. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September's 4.2 and level with August. Nonfarm payrolls reached 159,293 thousand, a gain of 165,000 on the month after September's 60,000.

Prices cooled, hiring strengthened and the jobless rate held just above four percent. The three readings describe an economy that is not contracting, and they are a month old.

## The world

Chen's investigation is the fourth time in this cycle that a sitting Politburo member has been brought down, on Qiu's count, and the commission's announcement gave no detail beyond the phrase "suspected violations of party discipline." The AP notes that Xi has run the campaign for more than a decade, with purges "on a scale not seen since Mao Zedong's rule."

The Shanghai government's statement does not defend Chen. Its Standing Committee "expressed firm support" for the investigation and called the decision "a clear and powerful signal that the fight against corruption would continue without pause or retreat." That is a city government endorsing the case against its own party secretary before the commission has published a finding.

The announcement leaves open the conduct alleged, the evidence and whether the case ends with party discipline alone or goes further. The commission gave no timetable and no findings, and that is where the record stands this morning.
