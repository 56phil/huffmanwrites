The White House said Saturday that President Donald Trump will close his trip to Asia next week with a joint meeting with President Xi Jinping of China and President Vladimir Putin of Russia in Shenzhen, putting the leaders of the world's biggest nuclear powers in one room as the treaty that limited American and Russian warheads has lapsed. Olivia Wales, a White House spokeswoman, told reporters that Trump would raise "the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world, whose proliferation President Trump believes is very dangerous." Russia has confirmed it will attend. China has not commented on the three-way session, and no agreement is expected from the meeting.

The weekend's domestic news was smaller in scale and sharper in law. A federal judge stopped the government from closing a Washington golf course and the park around it, one day before the closure was to take effect, and found the administration's reasons for it pretextual. The transportation secretary, meanwhile, named a copper-wire upgrade as the origin of Friday's four-hour ground stop, the longest since 2001, as the airlines worked through a second day of cancellations.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register published nothing in the window, which ran from Saturday into Sunday: no presidential documents, no rules, no proposed rules, no notices. The administration's news of the day arrived at a lectern instead.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy told a news conference that a technical problem following a copper-wire upgrade was behind the four-hour suspension of takeoffs on Friday. A contractor, L3Harris, was converting air traffic control circuits from copper to fibre-optic cable, Duffy said, when a router being worked on "started flooding other air traffic control systems" and broke communications with aircraft. He said the government was trying to establish whether the fault lay with "incompetence" by the company doing the work or with faulty equipment, and that the system had been so old the wire was being retired: "It was such an old wire that it was being discontinued." Bryan Bedford, who heads the Federal Aviation Administration, said at the same briefing that the agency had seen no evidence of anything nefarious. Officials, Duffy among them, had previously attributed the trouble to outdated equipment.

The scale of what followed came into focus with the explanation. More than 15,000 flights were delayed or cancelled on Friday, by the count of flight-tracking sites, affecting an estimated 1.5 million passengers, with another 5,900 cancellations and 7,000 delays on Saturday. The FAA said the schedule should return to normal by the end of Sunday. Duffy warned that the same failure could recur unless Congress pays for the replacement, for which Trump has requested $30.1 billion; Duffy has previously put the need at $50 billion. L3Harris said it had completed more than 70 percent of the work it was engaged to do before the shutdown and was supporting the FAA's investigation.

## The courts

Neither the Supreme Court nor the D.C. Circuit filed an opinion in the window. The district court, by contrast, issued a decision on the eve of the day the government had chosen to act.

On Saturday, Judge Ana Reyes of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia issued a 30-page decision temporarily blocking the government from closing East Potomac Golf Links and the park around it, which the president had said would shut on November 15 for a 15-month renovation. Reyes found that the administration's justifications for closing the course were pretextual and that it had violated at least two federal laws "to meet an arbitrary deadline." "The government proposes to close a historic park, daily enjoyed by Washingtonians of all stripes, indefinitely," she wrote, and the closure "runs counter to the public interest in utilising national parkland." She held that the government need not actively manage the park or the course while the case proceeds, only that it may not close either. Trump had called the existing course "Trash Island." The case is [D.C. Preservation League v. Department of the Interior](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72278895/dc-preservation-league-v-department-of-interior/), 1:26-cv-00477, and the docket this report watches recorded two filings on Saturday, entries 158 and 159.

The record before the court has not been kind to the government. In October the Park Service cut hundreds of trees without notifying Reyes until 15 hours before the work began, and she separately opened an inquiry, to be run with a special master, into whether Justice Department lawyers had "made misrepresentations to the court and otherwise violated ethical rules," including whether the late notice to the court was given in bad faith. The course sits two miles south of the White House and is one of three public courses the Park Service runs in the capital.

CourtListener recorded one new complaint in Washington: [Jones v. Howard University (The)](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75120033/jones-v-howard-university-the/), 1:26-cv-04029. ## Markets

Markets were closed for the weekend, so the board has not moved since Friday's close. The S&P 500 finished at 7,561.67, down 0.40 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 25,136.273, down 0.91; the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 52,347.55, up 0.10. The VIX closed at 19.30, up 3.21 percent; gold at $3,887.60 an ounce; the dollar index at 103.411, up 0.23 percent. CNBC's ten-year print was 5.139 percent at 5:03 p.m. eastern on Friday, against Treasury's own constant-maturity yield of 5.15 for the day. The one quote carrying a November 14 stamp is WTI crude at $88.92 a barrel, up 18 cents.

Treasury's curve for November 13 ran at 4.88 percent for two years, 5.15 for ten and 5.47 for thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.27 against +0.24 the day before. Total public debt outstanding was $40,608,506,856,903 on November 12, up $24,376,776,630 from November 10, the prior business day. Of that, $32,824,386,143,425 was held by the public and $7,784,120,713,478 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

No new release has landed since the October figures, and the three series stand where yesterday's edition left them. The all-items consumer price index was 337.511 for October, a monthly rise of 0.17 percent; unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September; and total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September. Prices cooled in October while the labor market held. One month is not a trend, and there is nothing further to say until the series moves.

## Elections and the midterms

Mississippi's count is closed. The secretary of state, Michael Watson, announced Friday that the official results from all 82 counties were in, with 21,344 affidavit ballots processed and about 73 percent of them accepted. The returns put Senator-elect Scott Colom at 479,404 votes, or 49.3 percent, to 471,817, or 48.6 percent, for Cindy Hyde-Smith, a margin of 7,587, with the independent Ty Pinkins at 20,439. The state declares its result on December 3.

Alaska is the only Senate race the Associated Press has not called. The Division of Elections tallied another 4,290 ballots on Saturday, and the AP now estimates 97 percent of the vote is counted, with Mary Peltola, a former Democratic congresswoman, still short of 50 percent of first-choice votes. The state tabulates ranked choices on November 18 in any race where no candidate won a majority of first-place votes; that is also the deadline for ballots mailed from overseas to arrive. NBC News's Decision Desk put her lead at 3.7 points, just over 10,000 votes, and calculated that she needs 53.8 percent of the remaining ballots to clear 50 percent; she took 58 percent of Friday's batch. Senator Dan S. Sullivan would need about 92 percent of roughly 12,000 outstanding ballots to tie her on first choices; he took 38 percent of Friday's. The ranked-choice route is narrower still: more than 45 percent of the ballots cast for Gerald Heikes, Dan J. Sullivan Jr. or a write-in choice ranked neither leader, so only about 6,600 votes would transfer, fewer than the margin. Certification is set for November 25. A Peltola win would give the Democratic caucus 56 seats and a Sullivan hold 55.

Polymarket's Alaska book has collapsed to one side, Peltola at 100.0 percent on $239,644 of twenty-four-hour volume. The same venue priced Chuck Schumer at 91.5 percent to be the next Senate majority leader and Chris Van Hollen at 7.8. Those are prices, not calls.

## The world

The mechanics of the meeting were set out by the White House. Trump arrives in Shenzhen late on Wednesday for the two-day summit of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum's 21 member states, holds bilateral talks with Xi on Thursday, their fourth meeting since Trump returned to office in 2025, and ends the visit with the trilateral session. He attends a summit of the Association of South-east Asian Nations in the Philippines on Tuesday before that. China's state media reported on Friday that Xi would take part in "bilateral" talks at the summit, citing a foreign ministry spokesman, but has not reported on the trilateral. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told Russian state media earlier this month that there were "signals confirming readiness" for such talks.

The subject the White House named is nuclear arms. The treaty that limited American and Russian warheads expired in February, leaving the two largest arsenals without major guardrails. The United Nations has urged both sides to agree a new framework without delay, and Trump has said he could replace the deal with a better agreement that includes China, which has been building up its arsenal. The BBC reported that Iran may also be discussed, but not Ukraine, and that no agreement was expected, according to a senior American official speaking to the Associated Press and Reuters.

Europe spent the weekend trying to shape what the three decide. Chancellor Friedrich Merz of Germany, at a meeting of European Union leaders on Friday, said, "I want to state our message to the US very clearly once again: please, no surprises." He added: "Stay on Team Europe and Team Ukraine, and let us work together for a genuine peace in Ukraine." President Emmanuel Macron of France warned against forcing "an unjust peace" on Ukraine: "That is the spirit of defeat," he said. "That is a recipe for another war." President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said the meeting must not turn into "Yalta 2, 3, 4," and the historian Timothy Garton Ash called it "a sort of parody" of the 1945 conference, which the Guardian reported was organised after a proposal by Putin last month. In Manila, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese of Australia welcomed the talks: "We think that dialogue is a good thing and we await the outcome of that dialogue."

What to watch is whether Beijing confirms the trilateral before Wednesday, and whether any of the three carries an arms-control proposal into the room.
