Friday's close was the rout toward which the week had been building. The S&P 500 fell 265.44 points, or 3.70 percent, to 6,910.39; the Nasdaq Composite fell 1,261.466 points, or 5.28 percent, to 22,617.733; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949.73 points, or 1.93 percent, to 48,147.33. The VIX closed at 35.89, up 7.45 points, or 26.20 percent. Gold fell 3.98 percent, so the haven that did not bid on Thursday did not bid on Friday either. And before that close, during Russia's overnight assault on Ukraine, two missiles and a jet-powered drone crossed into Poland, where Warsaw called the incursion an intentional provocation and NATO's secretary-general called it a grave violation.

## Markets

The week ended lower on every index. As of the November 20 close, the S&P 500 was 6,910.39, down 3.70 percent; the Nasdaq Composite 22,617.733, down 5.28 percent; and the Dow 48,147.33, down 1.93 percent. The VIX was 35.89, up 26.20 percent. The dollar index was 103.84, down 0.05 percent.

Treasury's official curve for November 20 ran 4.63 percent at two years, 4.92 at ten and 5.31 at thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.29, one basis point wider than Thursday's +0.28. The CNBC ten-year print at the close was 4.95 percent, three basis points above Treasury's own figure; the difference is a live quote against the day's official rate, and the official one is the number to cite.

Gold and crude carried a venue timestamp of 18:47 eastern, later than this edition's writing, so they are reported as the pack fetched them and not as prices this run watched move: gold at $3,539.00 an ounce, down 3.98 percent, and WTI crude at $83.62 a barrel, up 1.43 percent.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,709,780,700,317 on November 19, up $19,011,061,467 from the prior business day. Debt held by the public was $32,901,459,975,089 and the intragovernmental share $7,808,320,725,228.

## The economy

No new release has landed since the October figures, so the three readings stand where yesterday's edition left them. The all-items consumer price index was 337.511 for October, 336.940 for September and 334.980 for August, a monthly rise of about 0.17 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent in October, a tenth below September and level with August. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September. Prices rose slowly, hiring held, and all three are one month's readings.

## The world

Russia's overnight assault on Ukraine crossed a NATO border. Poland's defense minister, Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, told a Warsaw news conference that two missiles and a jet-powered drone came down on Polish soil during the bombardment of November 20: the missiles at Nowy Lipowiec and Rudnik nad Sanem, the drone at Molodycz, all in the southeast. [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/21/russian-missiles-hit-poland-what-happened-and-could-nato-respond) that one missile landed about 35 kilometers from where European troops were holding drills, that a Kh-101 cruise missile came down about 50 kilometers from the Ukrainian border, and that no injuries or damage were reported.

The same night, Zelenskyy said Moscow fired more than 400 drones and at least 110 missiles, 27 of them ballistic and some with cluster warheads. At least eight civilians were killed and more than two dozen wounded.

Warsaw called it deliberate. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it appeared "we are dealing with an intentional provocation," and that the timing likely matched European exercises that began November 9. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called the incursion a "grave violation" of Polish airspace and "clearly dangerous," and was expected in Poland that weekend. Poland asked for additional NATO backing under the existing Eastern Sentry mission, possibly airborne early warning aircraft, and stopped short of invoking Article 4, which it had invoked in September 2025 after Russian drones entered its airspace.

The political reading was set before the strike. Hours earlier, Trump described the incursions as accidents: "Putin has been landing drones in Poland and a couple of little missiles, by mistake." Scott Lucas of University College Dublin said the episode fits a pattern of Moscow "testing how far they can go," and that Article 5 would require "what in effect would be a sustained Russian attack on Poland." Harry Nedelcu of Rasmussen Global said Russia was probing "just under the radar of Article 5," and that "the alliance shows every signal that it does not want to show any teeth."

The same day, Denmark expelled Russia's defense attache over an alleged plot to kill the rapper Kyivstoner near Copenhagen. That is the hybrid half of the same campaign, and the harder half to answer.
