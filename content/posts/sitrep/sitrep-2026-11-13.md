Russia and Ukraine traded roughly 1,200 drones overnight, and a hospital in central Kyiv caught fire after a Russian drone struck it at about 3 a.m. local time. Russia's Defense Ministry said it brought down 1,198 Ukrainian drones across more than fifteen regions; the governor of Volgograd, Andrei Bocharov, said strikes on an industrial zone there killed four people and injured two; and the mayor of Moscow, Sergei Sobyanin, put the number of Ukrainian drones over the capital at 467, according to RIA Novosti. Ukraine's military administration in Odesa said the port had been under attack since early Friday and that at least two people had been killed, and emergency officials in the Zaporizhia region reported six injured. The barrage came as Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, who met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday, briefed President Volodymyr Zelensky in Dublin before a Friday meeting of European leaders. Kushner said the deal could not be concluded without the Europeans at the table. The Kremlin said Moscow had not yet agreed to seat them there. In the markets, the benchmark ten-year Treasury yield traded at 5.137 percent, up 3.4 basis points on the morning, while the S&P 500 had closed 0.35 percent lower on Thursday.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register published 242 documents in the window, which runs from Thursday through Friday: two presidential documents, 28 rules, 19 proposed rules and 193 notices. Several of those were covered in yesterday's edition, including the proclamation establishing the Oval Office as a national monument. What follows is the rest of what moved.

The consequential item is an order from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention under sections 362 and 365 of the Public Health Service Act, [continuing the suspension of the right to introduce certain persons from countries where a quarantinable communicable disease exists](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23114/order-under-sections-362-and-365-of-the-public-health-service-act-continuing-the-suspension-of-the). The order was issued November 10, took effect that day at 5:00 p.m. eastern time, and runs through 4:59 p.m. eastern on December 10. The director may amend or rescind it before then, which makes it a thirty-day instrument rather than a permanent one.

The second presidential document is [Presidential Determination No. 202702](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23233/presidential-determination-pursuant-to-section-1245d4b-and-c-of-the-national-defense-authorization), signed October 28 and published Friday, under section 1245(d)(4)(B) and (C) of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2012. The register carries no abstract for it and this report did not obtain the text, so it cannot say what the determination decides.

Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control published four documents carrying six general licenses: [Iran-related web general licenses EE and FF](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23197/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-licenses-ee-and-ff), [a separate Iran-related general license, W](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23193/publication-of-iran-related-web-general-license-w), [Russian harmful foreign activities general licenses 55F and 115D](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23196/publication-of-russian-harmful-foreign-activities-sanctions-regulations-web-general-licenses-55f-and) and [a Cuba sanctions general license](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23195/publication-of-a-cuba-sanctions-regulations-web-general-license). A general license authorizes transactions the regulations otherwise prohibit. This report did not read what each one permits and does not characterize them.

Among the rules, the Government Accountability Office [updated the statutory citations and the internet address in its bid protest regulations](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23168/update-to-statutory-citations-and-internet-address-in-bid-protest-regulations); the Environmental Protection Agency [withdrew the federal implementation plan for the Detroit sulfur dioxide area](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/12/2026-23026/approval-and-promulgation-of-air-quality-implementation-plans-michigan-withdrawal-of-the-federal); and the Justice Department [placed oveporexton in Schedule IV](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/12/2026-23075/schedules-of-controlled-substances-placement-of-oveporexton-in-schedule-iv). Many of the 193 notices are Interior Department repatriation notices under the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act, and the Defense Department published a [final deauthorization list of water resources projects](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/13/2026-23190/final-deauthorization-list-of-water-resources-projects).

## Markets

Equities closed lower on Thursday. The S&P 500 finished at 7,592.40, down 26.98 points or 0.35 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 25,366.093, down 100.096 or 0.39 percent; the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 52,297.09, down 189.40 or 0.36 percent.

The morning prints, collected at 06:19 CT on Friday: the VIX at 18.23, down 0.47 or 2.51 percent; gold at $3,899.10 an ounce, up $13.80 or 0.36 percent; front-month WTI crude at $88.58 a barrel, down $0.16; and the dollar index at 103.248, up 0.075. The quote service put the ten-year Treasury yield at 5.137 percent, up 3.4 basis points.

Treasury's daily curve now runs through November 12, the first posting since November 10: 4.86 percent for two years, 5.10 for ten and 5.43 for thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.24 against +0.25 on November 10. Both ends rose across the two postings, the two-year by 13 basis points and the thirty-year by 8, so the curve flattened as it climbed. Treasury's series carries no posting for November 11.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,584,130,080,273 on November 10, the latest posting, up $24,333,491,030 from November 9. Of that, $32,800,204,226,579 was held by the public and $7,783,925,853,694 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

The Bureau of Labor Statistics series is unchanged again, and no new release has landed. The October all-items consumer price index was 337.511, a monthly rise of 0.17 percent; unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September; and total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September. Prices decelerated in October while the labor market did not loosen, which is what the three readings said last week. One month is not a trend, and this report has nothing to add until the series moves.

## Elections and the midterms

Friday is the deadline for Mississippi's counties to certify their results. The Associated Press [called the Senate race for Scott Colom](https://wtop.com/us-politics/2026/11/why-ap-called-the-mississippi-senate-race-for-scott-colom/) just before 6 p.m. eastern time on Wednesday, after Jackson County reported 2,173 more votes and put him 4,669 ahead of Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith out of 962,237 counted, a margin of 0.5 of a percentage point. Fewer than 12,000 ballots remained for county officials to review, many of them affidavit ballots, and Mississippi has no recount law, so a challenge would need a court order. AP's account gives Democrats 55 Senate seats, a net gain of eight.

The tracker where this report reads AP's calls, [Votelect](https://votelect.com/), still carries a Senate ledger stamped November 4 and marked "retained; source error," showing 54 Democrats and two races uncalled. That ledger predates Wednesday's call, so this report does not restate a seat count from it.

Alaska is the last Senate race uncalled. AP's percentages as the same tracker carries them were Mary Peltola at 48.6 and Senator Dan Sullivan at 47.5, and the tracker prints no share of the vote counted. [Peltola and JKT strengthen leads in latest Alaska election results](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-11-10/peltola-and-jkt-strengthen-leads-in-latest-alaska-election-results), published November 10, put AP's estimate of the counted vote at 84 percent, reported that the Division of Elections planned to release more results Friday, and said the division tabulates the rankings and declares unofficial winners on November 18.

Polymarket priced the remainder. Peltola stood at 99.6 percent on $244,772 of twenty-four-hour volume, with Sullivan at 0.5 percent. Chuck Schumer stood at 86.5 percent to be Senate majority leader against 13.2 percent for Chris Van Hollen, and Hakeem Jeffries at 95.6 percent to be Speaker. The venue put the Democratic House popular-vote margin between 14 and 16 points at 85.7 percent. Those are prices, not results.

## The world

The American envoys met Putin in Moscow on Thursday and flew on to Dublin, where they briefed Zelensky before a Friday gathering of European leaders that was meant to settle who negotiates for Europe. Kushner's line was that the deal cannot be concluded without the Europeans, and Ursula von der Leyen, the European Commission president, said Europe should be at the table so that "Europe is included in the peace talks to safeguard our interests, but also to support Ukraine." The European states had not settled on a lead negotiator before talks expected next week, and that choice was the Dublin meeting's purpose.

Moscow has not conceded the point. Dmitry Peskov, the Kremlin's spokesman, said Moscow "has not yet agreed to include European mediators in the talks," and added that it could decide on an expanded format once it understood the Europeans' intentions and their willingness to contribute. Russia has repeated its demand for all of Ukraine's Donbas region, and unconfirmed reports said it also sought Finland's exit from NATO and a pledge against basing NATO troops in the Baltics. Kyiv's worry is that broadening the table is a distraction from Russia's refusal to give up occupied territory.

The talks rest on what the two days in Abu Dhabi produced, which was a large prisoner exchange and no ceasefire. Whether the exchange survives this week's fighting is the next thing to watch.
