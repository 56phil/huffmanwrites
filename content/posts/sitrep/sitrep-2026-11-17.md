Ukraine brought down the road span of the Kerch bridge on Tuesday, in what its General Staff described as the final strike of a joint operation several weeks in the making, and the Russian-installed governor of Crimea suspended traffic across the only road and rail link between Russia and the peninsula. The road section is out of service, the General Staff said. The parallel railway section still stands, with its arch damaged. The strike came days before a scheduled meeting of Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Donald Trump in Shenzhen, and Russia answered overnight by hitting two bridges over the Dnieper in Kyiv.

The American record of the day was quieter. Equities fell at Monday's close, the first session since Friday: the S&P 500 lost 1.92 percent to 7,416.59 and the Nasdaq Composite 2.49 percent to 24,509.758. The ten-year Treasury yield held at 5.12 percent, total public debt stood at $40.62 trillion, and the Federal Register published 203 documents, among them a final rule on immigration-court appeals and a proposal to remake the labor-market test for permanent work visas. Alaska's Senate race, still uncalled, goes to a ranked-choice tabulation on Wednesday.

## The administration and the rule of law

The register's 203 documents broke down as two presidential documents, twenty rules, sixteen proposed rules and 165 notices. Both presidential documents were published Monday and carried in yesterday's edition: the [Veterans Day, 2026 proclamation](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23348/veterans-day-2026) and the [presidential determination concerning wildfire suppression activities in the United States](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23349/presidential-determination-concerning-wildfire-suppression-activities-in-the-united-states).

Among the final rules, the Justice Department and the Executive Office for Immigration Review adopted, with minor changes, the [interim rule on appellate procedures for the Board of Immigration Appeals](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23320/appellate-procedures-for-the-board-of-immigration-appeals). The interim version took effect March 9 and drew comments through April 8; the final rule takes effect December 17. The FCC's Wireline Competition Bureau [announced the effective and compliance dates](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23400/protecting-consumers-from-sim-swap-and-port-out-fraud) for the SIM-swap and port-out fraud rules it adopted in 2023, which had been held back pending Paperwork Reduction Act review; those rules take effect today. The same commission [finalized its rewrite of the National Environmental Policy Act rules](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23385/modernizing-the-commissions-national-environmental-policy-act-rules), revising provisions across fifteen parts of the Code of Federal Regulations, with most changes effective December 17 and many individual instructions delayed indefinitely. The NCUA closed a two-day run of credit-union deregulation with a [final rule on associational common bonds](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23392/chartering-and-field-of-membership-for-federal-credit-unions-associational-common-bonds-and): requiring members to buy a good, product or service from a group will no longer by itself disqualify the group from recognized-association status. It takes effect January 4, 2027.

Two proposed rules are worth the reader's time. Labor would [modernize the labor-market test for the PERM immigrant visa program](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23370/modernizing-the-labor-market-test-and-improving-protections-for-us-workers-in-the-perm-immigrant): it would stop administering Schedule A, strengthen recruitment standards, add safeguards for American workers affected by layoffs, including layoffs by third-party staffing firms, expand notice-of-filing requirements, and codify electronic filing and decisions. Comments close January 19, 2027. A second Labor item is a [correction to the respirable crystalline silica proposal](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23390/respirable-crystalline-silica-and-respiratory-protection) published November 12, not a new exposure standard.

Commerce again supplied the bulk of the notices, with more than twenty determinations in antidumping and countervailing-duty proceedings. Two of them impose [antidumping](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23386/hardwood-and-decorative-plywood-from-the-peoples-republic-of-china-indonesia-and-the-socialist) and [countervailing](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23393/hardwood-and-decorative-plywood-from-the-peoples-republic-of-china-indonesia-and-the-socialist) duty orders on hardwood and decorative plywood from China, Indonesia and Vietnam, and a third reports [final results on wood mouldings and millwork from China](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23402/wood-mouldings-and-millwork-products-from-the-peoples-republic-of-china-final-results-of-antidumping).

Interior's Bureau of Land Management [opened scoping](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23368/notice-of-intent-to-prepare-an-environmental-impact-statement-for-the-proposed-western-gateway) on an environmental impact statement for the Western Gateway Pipeline, a 20-inch liquid petroleum products line from Borger to El Paso and a 24-inch line from El Paso to Buckeye, Arizona, crossing Arizona, New Mexico and Texas; comments are due December 17. The Nuclear Regulatory Commission gave the second of four weekly notices on the [construction permit application](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/17/2026-23355/smr-llc-palisades-smr-llc-pioneer-units-1-and-2-receipt-of-construction-permit-application) from SMR, LLC for two SMR-300 units, Pioneer 1 and Pioneer 2, at the Palisades Energy Center in Covert, Michigan, received September 30. And the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services [extended and modified](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/16/2026-23242/medicare-medicaid-and-childrens-health-insurance-programs-announcement-of-the-extension-and) the nationwide moratorium on enrolling new hospices, effective November 12.

## Markets

Monday's close was the first since Friday, and it was lower across the board: the S&P 500 at 7,416.59, down 145.08 points or 1.92 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 24,509.758, down 626.515 or 2.49; the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 51,608.17, down 739.38 or 1.41. The VIX printed 22.42 at 6:59 a.m. eastern, down 0.49. The ten-year yielded 5.121 percent at 7:00 a.m. eastern, against Treasury's constant-maturity reading of 5.12 for November 16. Gold was $3,873.20 an ounce, up 0.52 percent; WTI crude $86.23 a barrel, up 0.59; the dollar index 103.60, up 0.05.

Treasury's curve for November 16 ran 4.83 percent at two years, 5.12 at ten and 5.46 at thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.29, against +0.27 on November 13. Total public debt outstanding was $40,616,678,827,092 on November 13, up $8,171,970,189 from the prior business day. Of that, $32,825,322,014,327 was held by the public and $7,791,356,812,765 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

No new release has landed since the October figures. The all-items consumer price index was 337.511 for October, up from 336.940 in September, a monthly rise of about 0.17 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent, a tenth below September, and total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand, 165 thousand above September. Prices rose slowly, hiring held, and one month is not a trend.

## Elections and the midterms

Mississippi's count is finished. Secretary of State Michael Watson announced the result from all 82 counties on Friday: Scott Colom 479,404 votes, or 49.3 percent, to Cindy Hyde-Smith's 471,817, or 48.6, a margin of 7,587, with 21,344 affidavit ballots processed and about 73 percent of them accepted.

Alaska's Senate race remains uncalled. Alaska Public Media, reading the state's count, put Mary Peltola at 49.98 percent to Dan Sullivan's 45.93, a margin of 4.05 points, after the Division of Elections added about 1,800 ballots on Monday; the Associated Press estimated 98 percent of the vote counted. The state runs ranked-choice tabulation on Wednesday, when overseas ballots are also due, and certification is expected November 25.

Polymarket priced Peltola at 99.8 percent on $74,266 of twenty-four-hour volume and Sullivan at 0.1. The same venue had Chuck Schumer at 90.5 percent to be the next Senate majority leader, Chris Van Hollen at 8.6, and House turnout of 115 to 120 million at 71.0 percent. Those are prices, not calls.

## The world

A Ukrainian drone attack killed at least two people in Russian-annexed Crimea, according to the Moscow-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, who said "traffic has been suspended" after the strike, Al Jazeera reported. It was the second consecutive day of attacks on the bridge, a 19-kilometer road and rail crossing over the Kerch Strait. President Volodymyr Zelensky published footage showing the main arch for cars and part of its roadway collapsed.

The General Staff gave the fuller account. In a Facebook post reported by Ukrinform, it said the arch and a road-section span had collapsed, that "the road section is out of service, and no vehicle can cross it," and that the railway section stands with its arch damaged. It described a multi-stage joint operation of several weeks, in which Ukrainian forces first degraded Russian air defences, electronic warfare and radar, before the final strike with Ukrainian-developed weapons. The bridge had been the main supply artery for fuel, ammunition, equipment and personnel, the General Staff said.

Russia struck back in Kyiv. The defence ministry said it hit military infrastructure, including cruise-missile and drone production facilities, transport, energy, port infrastructure and military ships, and Al Jazeera cited official data that Russian air defences destroyed more than 1,400 drones over 23 regions. Moscow's mayor said more than 800 drones flew toward the capital. Kyiv's mayor, Vitali Klitschko, said the Northern and Southern bridges over the Dnieper were hit and closed, that the subway was damaged, and that water and power were partly cut. Al Jazeera's correspondent reported that heating was cut to hundreds of apartment buildings on the city's Left Bank.

The timing is the part to watch. The strikes came days before Putin, Xi and Trump are to meet in Shenzhen, and US-mediated talks in the United Arab Emirates the previous week produced no breakthrough, Al Jazeera reported. Whether the crossing can be repaired before winter is the question that follows, and the General Staff's own description of it as the main supply artery for fuel, ammunition, equipment and personnel is the reason.
