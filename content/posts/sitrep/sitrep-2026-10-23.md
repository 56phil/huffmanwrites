Thursday's selling was concentrated where a war premium hurts most. The Nasdaq Composite fell 693.999 points, 2.60 percent, to 26,008.859, and the S&P 500 lost 93.74 points, 1.21 percent, to 7,663.43, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed almost exactly where it opened, up 11.89 points at 52,867.98. The VIX rose 4.36 percent to 19.86. Front-month WTI crude was quoted at $100.95 a barrel, up 1.49 percent, as of 6:50 a.m. ET, and the ten-year Treasury yield stood at 4.99 percent as of 7:00 a.m. ET, three basis points above Thursday's official close. Overnight the UAE's environment ministry reported a tanker oil spill off Umm Al Quwain and closed the emirate's beaches, the latest cost of the attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz. In Washington the day's regulatory output was heavy: the comptroller of the currency finalized the GENIUS Act's stablecoin regime, Treasury proposed rules for the housing law's ban on large institutional buyers of single-family homes, and the Justice and Education departments declared seven joint guidance documents unenforceable. At a rally in Iowa, President Trump said he had the right to seek a third term.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register's two-day window holds 178 documents: one presidential document, seventeen rules, nine proposed rules and 151 notices. The presidential document is the women's health executive order published October 22 and reported in Thursday's edition. The rest of what follows is new this morning.

The [Office of the Comptroller of the Currency's final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21706/implementing-the-guiding-and-establishing-national-innovation-for-us-stablecoins-act-for-the) implements the Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act for entities under its jurisdiction. Published at 91 FR 67000 and effective January 18, 2027, it covers the issuance of payment stablecoins and certain related activities, and it fixes the compliance date on which the industry's calendar now turns.

Treasury's [proposed rule on the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21675/21st-century-road-to-housing-act-prohibition-on-large-institutional-investor-purchases-of) at 91 FR 66799 would clarify section 1001 of the statute, which prohibits large institutional investors from purchasing single-family homes subject to exceptions. The proposal defines "large institutional investor," explains how the exceptions apply, and describes the enforcement mechanism, all before the statute takes effect. Comments are due November 23.

The [Justice and Education departments' notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21685/clarification-on-department-of-justice-and-department-of-education-guidance-documents-regarding) at 91 FR 66840 is the window's clearest statement of legal position. Seven guidance documents the two departments issued jointly are "not enforceable," the notice says, and the departments "will not rely upon these guidance documents" in enforcing Titles IV and VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Equal Educational Opportunities Act of 1974, the Americans with Disabilities Act, and Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act. They plan to revisit the guidance in light of *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, the 2024 decision that ended judicial deference to agency interpretations of ambiguous statutes.

Agriculture's Food and Nutrition Administration [proposed changes to SNAP electronic benefit transfer rules](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21658/supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-electronic-benefits-transfer-card-security-measures) at 91 FR 66958, and comments are due December 22. The rule would modernize EBT technology and align it with consumer payment processing standards, which the agency says will protect benefits from thieves and keep them accessible to approved households.

The Nuclear Regulatory Commission [docketed a petition for rulemaking](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21690/irradiated-fuel-reprocessing-plants), PRM-74-1, from Howard L. Hall, Ph.D., dated August 14. It asks the commission to remove the exclusion of irradiated fuel reprocessing plants from its material control and accounting requirements and to write requirements for that facility class. Comments are due November 23. The SEC also [published its annual list of rules scheduled for review](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21657/list-of-rules-to-be-reviewed-pursuant-to-the-regulatory-flexibility-act) under section 610 of the Regulatory Flexibility Act, with comments due the same day.

The remainder is routine: fisheries actions in the Gulf of Alaska and off the West Coast, Class E airspace changes in Illinois and Texas, airworthiness directives for Boeing, General Electric and MD Helicopters, and approval of state environmental programs in Virginia and Nevada.

## Markets

Thursday's close was weak beneath a flat headline number. The Dow's 11.89-point gain, 0.02 percent, hides a session in which the S&P 500 fell 1.21 percent and the Nasdaq fell 2.60 percent. That divergence is the signature of selling concentrated in long-duration growth stocks rather than spread across the market.

This morning's board carries the risk forward. The VIX was 19.86, up 0.83 or 4.36 percent, as of 6:59:46 a.m. ET. Gold was $4,038.60 an ounce, down $21.80 or 0.54 percent, as of 6:50:20. Front-month WTI was $100.95, up $1.48 or 1.49 percent, as of 6:50:20. The dollar index was 101.788, up 0.181 or 0.18 percent, as of 7:00:22. The ten-year was quoted at 4.99 percent, up 0.037, as of 7:00:08.

Treasury's official Thursday curve ran 4.65 percent on the two-year, 4.96 on the ten-year and 5.29 on the thirty-year, a 2s10s spread of +0.31 points, one basis point flatter than Wednesday's +0.32. The whole curve fell from Wednesday: sixteen basis points at the two-year, seventeen at the ten-year, thirteen at the thirty. Bonds rallied on the same day equities sold, which is what a flight to safety looks like. This morning's live ten-year quote, back to 4.99, says the rally did not hold overnight.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,413,639,012,306 on October 21, down $25,907,003,733 from October 20. Debt held by the public was $32,566,346,587,338, and $7,847,292,424,968 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

September remains the newest reference month and the three readings are the same ones Thursday's edition reported. The consumer price index was 336.940, against 334.980 in August and 333.918 in July, a rise of 0.6 percent in the month. Unemployment was 4.2 percent, up from 4.1. Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 to 159.044 million. Prices are rising at roughly a 7 percent annualized pace on the month's move while hiring has nearly stopped, and the ten-year near 5 percent is the market's reading of that combination. Nothing published in this window changes the picture.

## Elections and the midterms

Polymarket's balance-of-power market put a Democratic sweep at 67.5 percent, unchanged from Thursday's edition, on $876,911 of twenty-four-hour volume. A Republican sweep traded at 7.5 percent on $1,208,789, the heaviest book in the set, and a Republican Senate with a Democratic House at 25.5 percent, up two points. The chamber markets gave Democrats 67.5 percent in the Senate, up one point, on $673,433, and 92.5 percent in the House, unchanged, on $317,135.

The state races moved at the margin. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico traded at 66.5 percent and Republican Ken Paxton at 33.5, a one-point shift toward Paxton. Michigan gave Democrat Abdul El-Sayed 71.5 percent against Republican Mike Rogers at 28.5. Kansas gave Republican Roger Marshall 61.5 percent against Democrat Adam Hamilton at 38.5, and Idaho gave Republican Jim Risch 96.5. These are the venues' prices, not this publication's view, and a thin book is not a forecast.

The day's political news was one no market prices. At a rally at Sioux Gateway Airport in Sioux City, Iowa, on Thursday, [President Trump said he had the right to seek a third presidential term](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/23/trump-says-he-has-the-right-to-seek-a-third-presidential-term), Euronews reports. The article says he repeated his claim that the 2020 election was rigged and stolen from him, and that he acknowledged the law leaves little interpretive room while arguing he should still be allowed to run. The Constitution limits a president to two elected terms. A claim of a right to a third is not a policy proposal; it is a statement of intent that tests whether the two-term limit is treated as a rule or as a norm.

## The world

The UAE's Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said Friday that authorities [detected a tanker oil spill affecting Umm Al Quwain's beaches](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/23/oil-spill-hits-uae-beaches-as-tanker-attacks-multiply-near-hormuz). Response teams began containment, cordoning, clean-up and remediation, the ministry said, and the emirate closed its beaches. No volume was given and no vessel was formally identified as the source. Residents in northern areas, including parts of northern Dubai, had reported petroleum-like odors on social media in the preceding days.

The spill follows an attack. The UK Maritime Trade Operations office reported on October 9 that a vessel was struck by a projectile off the UAE coast and caught fire, and the maritime security firm Vanguard identified it as the Panama-flagged oil tanker GEM NO.2. Euronews does not state that the GEM NO.2 caused the spill, and this report does not either. What the sequence establishes is that the war's shipping campaign has reached the shoreline. Iran's Revolutionary Guard has said it targets tankers using what Tehran calls an unauthorized route through the strait's southern section, near Oman's coast. About a fifth of global oil passed through Hormuz before the war began with US-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February, and both Tehran and Washington imposed blockades in the strait.

The prediction markets read the situation with more confidence than the shoreline suggests. Polymarket gave the US-Iran ceasefire continuing through October 24 a 92.5 percent chance and through October 31 a 77.5 percent chance, with a NATO-Russia military clash by October 31 at 20.5 percent. A spill with no identified source and a ceasefire the market still expects to hold are not contradictory. The attacks are being priced as harassment inside a ceasefire, not as the ceasefire's collapse.
