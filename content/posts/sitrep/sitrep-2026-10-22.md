Russia released a jailed American schoolteacher and told the president's envoy that it was willing in principle to restart talks on Ukraine, and then struck Kyiv with missiles that killed at least one person and injured twenty-two, four of them children. Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin at Constantine Palace in St Petersburg on Wednesday, praised him as a humanitarian for the release, and told him Washington was grateful for a separate arrangement under which Russia would sell diesel to the United States. President Trump, who said he had delayed new sanctions to secure the teacher's freedom, said the war "may be as close as it's ever been to ending." Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, answered that "appeasement, servility, and flattery will not stop this war." The same day, AIPAC told its members that it would stop endorsing candidates and making direct PAC contributions, a retreat by the largest pro-Israel lobby in American politics, and the White House published an executive order that rebuilds the federal program of research into women's health.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register's two-day window holds 159 documents: two presidential documents, fourteen rules, eight proposed rules and 135 notices.

The new presidential document is [Executive Order 14437](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/22/2026-21666/advancing-womens-health-to-improve-outcomes-in-all-phases-of-life), signed October 19 and published this morning at 91 FR 66779. It finds that "many diseases and conditions that disproportionately or uniquely affect women have not received the level of research" they warrant, and it sets federal policy to "prioritize translating research into improved clinical care that benefits women and girls," from infancy through old age. Its machinery is two bodies and one deadline. The Secretary of Health and Human Services, acting through the director of the National Institutes of Health, must establish a Women's Health Division inside a suitable institute or center within ninety days, the order's only fixed date. The division is directed to widen scientific review of understudied conditions, run replication studies, improve the inclusion of women and girls in trials, and hold grant recipients to the NIH policy requiring research to account for sex. A White House Women's Health Task Force, co-chaired by the HHS Secretary and the President's chief of staff, is to coordinate the rest across the departments of Agriculture, Labor, Education and Veterans Affairs, the EPA, the FDA, the CDC, ARPA-H and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

The revocation is the substantive half. The order withdraws [Executive Order 14120 of March 18, 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/21/2024-06123/advancing-womens-health-research-and-innovation) and the November 13, 2023 memorandum that created the [White House Initiative on Women's Health Research](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/11/17/2023-25632/white-house-initiative-on-womens-health-research), and replaces their architecture with a division inside an existing institute and a task force answerable to the White House. The order creates no right enforceable at law or in equity.

Trade enforcement produced the window's most consequential rule. [Customs and Border Protection extended the compliance date](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/22/2026-21600/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry) for two provisions of its postal entry regulation, 19 CFR 145.12(a)(2)(v) and (vi), to January 20, 2027, part of the indefinite suspension of the de minimis exemption for mail shipments. A companion notice [modified the test of the new electronic informal entry process for mail](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/22/2026-21570/modification-of-the-test-of-the-new-electronic-informal-entry-process-for-mail). Read together, the two documents push the operational date for mail-based informal entry into next year while the formal-entry requirements adopted in June stand.

The rest of the window is routine. Homeland Security set safety zones at the Brownsville Ship Channel and around the tank vessel Denise Foss in Honolulu, and security zones for military cargo in Puget Sound. Transportation established or revoked Class E airspace at Havana and DeKalb in Illinois and Santa Elena, Texas. Commerce reallocated Pacific cod in the Gulf of Alaska and took inseason action on the West Coast salmon fisheries. The EPA approved Virginia's coal combustion residuals permit program, codified Nevada's underground storage tank program, and proposed primacy for Utah over Class VI injection wells. Treasury proposed a partial withdrawal of guidance under section 355, and the National Credit Union Administration issued three proposals on member notification, a nonconforming investment special reserve and conversion to a state charter. The Graham sanctions order, published yesterday, appears again in this window; it was reported in Wednesday's edition.

## Markets

Wednesday's close was firm across the board. The S&P 500 finished at 7,757.17, up 44.60 points or 0.58 percent. The Nasdaq Composite added 278.041 points, 1.05 percent, to 26,702.858. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 360.21 points, 0.69 percent, to 52,856.09.

This morning's board is unsettled. The VIX was 18.22, up 1.54 or 9.23 percent, as of 6:59:46 a.m. ET, on the morning after the indexes rose. Gold was $4,100.20 an ounce, down $49.80 or 1.20 percent, as of 6:50:17. Front-month WTI crude was $99.19, up $2.04 or 2.10 percent, as of 6:50:15. The dollar index stood at 101.688, down 0.092 points or 0.09 percent, as of 7:00:21. The ten-year Treasury was quoted at 5.065 percent, down 0.081, as of 7:00:20.

Treasury's official Wednesday curve ran 4.81 percent on the two-year, 5.13 on the ten-year and 5.42 on the thirty-year, with a 2s10s spread of +0.32 percentage points, a basis point flatter than Tuesday's +0.33. That 5.13 is Treasury's official close for Wednesday; this morning's 5.065 is a live pre-market quote.

Total public debt outstanding was $40,439,546,016,039 on October 20, up $43,144,895,554 from October 19. Debt held by the public was $32,566,198,492,043, and $7,873,347,523,996 of the total was intragovernmental.

## The economy

September remains the newest reference month, and the three readings are unchanged from Wednesday's edition. The consumer price index was 336.940, against 334.980 in August and 333.918 in July, a rise of 0.6 percent in the month and 0.9 percent over two months, which annualizes near 5.5 percent. Unemployment was 4.2 percent, up from 4.1. Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 to 159.044 million, after 133,000 in August. Prices are accelerating at the same time that hiring has nearly stopped, and the ten-year above five percent is the market's answer to that pair. Nothing published in this window changes the picture.

## Elections and the midterms

Polymarket moved further toward the Democrats overnight. The balance-of-power market gave a Democratic sweep 67.5 percent, up four points from Wednesday's edition and on $424,117 of twenty-four-hour volume, a Republican Senate with a Democratic House 23.5 percent, down from 27.5, and a Republican sweep 7.5 percent on $1,241,012 of volume, the heaviest book in the set. The House control market gave Democrats 92.5 percent and Republicans 7.5. The Senate control market gave Democrats 66.5 percent, from 64.5. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico traded at 67.5 percent and Republican Ken Paxton at 32.5, from 64.5 and 35.5. Ohio's Democratic gubernatorial candidate, Amy Acton, stood at 79.5 percent against Vivek Ramaswamy's 19.5, and Nevada's Aaron Ford at 66.5. These are the venues' prices, not this publication's view, and a thin book is not a forecast.

The day's political news was one that no market prices. [AIPAC to halt direct campaign contributions and candidate endorsements](https://www.jta.org/2026/10/21/politics/aipac-to-halt-direct-campaign-contributions-and-candidate-endorsements) reports the lobby's letter to members: "Endorsements, direct PAC contributions, and earmarking will no longer be part of our approach." It will instead "prioritize increasing direct, organized contributions from individual Americans to candidates" and use "emerging political fundraising tools." AIPAC's spokesperson, Deryn Sousa, said the mission and strategy are unchanged, and that "AIPAC members gave more than $90 million directly to candidates this cycle" with organized giving set to continue and increase. The group says it helped 290 candidates win and kept 29 candidates it calls "detractors" from winning. Its super PAC, United Democracy Project, remains active. The shift reduces the lobby's public footprint at a moment when its endorsements have become a liability in Democratic primaries, and it keeps the money moving through AIPAC's members rather than through its PAC.

## The world

A Russian missile strike destroyed a residential house and started a fire in Kyiv overnight, and damage to metro infrastructure at the Pivdenny Bridge over the Dnipro was reported by Mayor Vitali Klitschko. Ukraine's national emergency service put the toll at one dead and twenty-two injured, four of them children. Moscow's mayor, Sergei Sobyanin, reported two wounded in the region after Ukraine launched 630 drones at it, 163 of them toward Moscow, most intercepted. The governor of Tula said seventy drones were destroyed there, and the governor of Lipetsk reported another man wounded. The exchange came after the Kremlin said it was willing "in principle" to restart American-mediated talks while finding little "substance" in the Ukrainian and European proposals on the table.

The diplomacy and the fighting are not two stories running in parallel; each side is using the one to shape the other. Witkoff's praise of Putin as a humanitarian, and Washington's gratitude for a diesel arrangement, are the terms on which the administration is asking Moscow to negotiate. Sybiha's answer is that flattery is the wrong instrument. The Hubbard release is real and worth having. It is also a gesture that costs Moscow little and buys a delay in sanctions, and Ukraine read it that way.
