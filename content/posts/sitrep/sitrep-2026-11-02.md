On the morning before the midterm elections, Iran's foreign ministry put a two-day clock on reopening the Strait of Hormuz. A spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, said Tehran could restore the passage within two days if Washington accepts its terms, shortening the seven-day timeline Iran first proposed through Qatar in September. The conditions are an end to the war on all fronts, the unfreezing of Iranian assets abroad, the lifting of sanctions on Iran's oil sector, and an end to the American naval blockade of Iranian ports. Crude fell 4.47 percent to $99.47 a barrel in early electronic trading, down from $102.95 in the weekend's last print. A deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, was in Doha on Monday to help draft a preliminary agreement, with Qatar mediating, and China's special envoy, Zhai Jun, was moving between Tehran, Muscat and Riyadh. None of that is a deal. The same spokesman said any attack would meet a response he called powerful, immediate and comprehensive, and the vote is Tuesday with the House already priced at 94.5 percent Democratic.

## The administration and the rule of law

The register's two-day window carried 102 documents, all dated November 2: two presidential documents, eight rules, five proposed rules and 87 notices.

The two presidential documents were a memorandum delegating authority under section 8335 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2026 and an adjustment of imports of robotics, industrial machinery and their parts and components into the United States. The memorandum carries no abstract in the register, so it can be named here and not described. The import adjustment is a trade action, and it arrives in a register that ran heavily to trade enforcement.

Among the eight rules, Labor's final rule on pharmacy benefit manager fee disclosure is the one with dates a reader can hold. It requires pharmacy benefit management service providers, and their affiliated brokerage and consulting providers, to disclose compensation to fiduciaries of large self-insured ERISA group health plans. It takes effect January 4, 2027, and applies to plan years beginning on or after July 1, 2027. The register describes pharmacy spending as a major driver of employers' overall health care costs, which is the rule's premise rather than its finding.

HHS proposed a phased update to infant formula nutrient specifications and labeling that would amend minimum or maximum levels for four required nutrients; comments are due March 2, 2027. The Nuclear Regulatory Commission closed a rulemaking rather than opened one: its proposal on alternatives to the use of credit ratings is discontinued effective today. On the trade side, the International Trade Commission opened antidumping and countervailing duty investigations into shopping carts and parts from China, and Commerce amended its preliminary determination in the less-than-fair-value investigation of truck bed covers from China.

## Markets

Equities have not traded since Friday, and Friday was a losing session: the S&P 500 closed at 7,580.07, down 140.32 points or 1.82 percent; the Nasdaq Composite at 25,436.999, down 747.725 or 2.86 percent; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 51,770.31, down 572.25 or 1.09 percent, all as of October 30.

Early Monday, the VIX was 19.14, down 0.28 or 1.44 percent, at 7:01 a.m. Eastern, which is 6:01 Central. Front-month WTI crude fell $4.65, or 4.47 percent, to $99.47 at 6:50 a.m. Eastern; gold rose $13.90, or 0.36 percent, to $3,912.80 at 6:51; and the dollar index was 101.673, down 0.206 or 0.20 percent, at 7:01. The live ten-year Treasury yield was 4.929 percent, unchanged on the session, at 7:00.

Treasury's curve for October 30 ran 4.56 percent at two years, 4.94 at ten and 5.31 at thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.38. October 29 ran 4.61, 4.98 and 5.32, or +0.37, so the short end fell five basis points and the spread widened by one. Total public debt outstanding was $40,593,637,262,883 on October 29, up $42,582,410,521 from October 28. Of that, $32,712,190,190,967 was held by the public and $7,881,447,071,916 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

The BLS published no release in the window, so the newest readings remain September's and are unchanged from yesterday's edition. The consumer price index is 336.940 on the 1982-84 base, against 334.980 in August and 333.918 in July, a one-month move of 0.59 percent in September. Unemployment was 4.2 percent in September, against 4.1 percent in August and 4.1 in July. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,044 thousand in September, 29 thousand above August's 159,015 thousand, which was itself 133 thousand above July's 158,882 thousand.

Taken together, the readings say prices accelerated at the end of the third quarter while hiring slowed to about a fifth of August's pace, and the ten-year yield near five percent answers neither.

## Elections and the midterms

The midterms are Tuesday, and Polymarket's board is close to a verdict on one chamber and contested on the other. The House contract is 94.5 percent Democratic on $1,740,839 of twenty-four-hour volume, against 5.5 percent Republican on $737,957. The Senate contract is 72.5 percent Democratic on $852,765, against 27.5 percent Republican on $1,525,205. A Democratic sweep is 71.5 percent on $818,947; a Republican sweep is 5.5 percent on $1,052,197, the row carrying more volume than the Democratic sweep at a fraction of the price, which is the shape of a hedge rather than a forecast. These are the venue's prices, not this publication's view.

In the states, Michigan has Abdul El-Sayed at 81.5 percent on $912,724 against Mike Rogers at 18.5 percent on $609,094. Texas moved toward the Democrat since yesterday: James Talarico is at 75.5 percent, up five points, against Ken Paxton at 24.5 percent, down five, though the heavier pool remains Paxton's, $1,052,155 against $405,984. The Texas governor's race has Greg Abbott at 63.5 percent on $380,982.

The swing-state map carries the day's heaviest Senate pool. Its None outcome, meaning Republicans win none of the listed states, is 31.5 percent on $1,250,833, up seven points from yesterday. Iowa and Kansas together are 6.5 percent on $513,570, and Iowa, Kansas and Michigan together are 0.9 percent on $564,185.

## The world

Tehran's offer is the day's one story that moved beyond the register and the board. Baghaei put the reopening at two days if Washington meets the four conditions, and he said messages were still moving through mediators. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, in an Al Jazeera interview released Sunday, added three more: American acceptance of a new Iran-Oman transit route through the strait, fewer American troops in the region, and the removal of sanctions imposed since August. Gharibabadi's trip to Doha is where the drafting stands: a preliminary agreement, with Qatar mediating. China's role widened: Xi Jinping said the Gulf must not become an arena for great-power rivalry, and Beijing and Muscat urged a return to the Islamabad memorandum of understanding.

No American answer to the offer has been reported. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said he had received a letter from Vice President JD Vance and had not replied, and that the United States must first meet Iran's terms. President Trump said Saturday that he still had to deal with one or two targets, naming Pickaxe mountain in central Iran, and said Sunday that his talks with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had gone very well. CENTCOM rejected Iran's claim that Iranian strikes had hit only military targets, saying the attacks killed at least 20 people and struck residential areas, civilian centres and civilian sailors in international waters. Anwar Gargash, an adviser to the United Arab Emirates, told the Manama Dialogue that Abu Dhabi did not want a prolonged crisis but that trust had been damaged.

The war began in late February. Washington imposed a naval blockade and, from August, what it called economic asphyxiation. The midterms are Tuesday and the results arrive Wednesday, the anniversary of the 1979 seizure of the American embassy in Tehran. Polymarket prices the American-Iranian ceasefire at 97.4 percent to hold through today, 76.5 percent through November 8 and 57.5 percent through November 15. A contract on Washington announcing an end to the blockade is 2.2 percent by today, 13.5 percent by November 15 and 41.2 percent by December 31. The venue puts an American invasion of Iran before 2027 at 16.5 percent on $484,961 of volume. Read as a term structure, the board says the truce probably survives the vote and is barely favored to survive the first half of the month.
