The D.C. Circuit affirmed on Tuesday that the director of Voice of America cannot be removed without the approval of a majority of the International Broadcasting Advisory Board, and it rejected the government's arguments on both jurisdiction and Article II. Chief Judge Srinivasan and Judges Pillard and Pan heard the case; Judge Pillard wrote. October entered the Bureau of Labor Statistics price series at a 0.17 percent monthly rise, against 0.59 percent in September, and the Abu Dhabi talks on the Russia-Ukraine war ended with an agreement on a large prisoner exchange, no ceasefire, and a Kremlin negotiator's statement that the two sides had reached understanding in some places and not in others.

## The administration and the rule of law

The Federal Register published 108 documents in the window and no presidential documents. Yesterday's edition reported the November 10 batch, among them the Treasury's special measure against MEXC Global, the two Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program proposals, the Housing Department's reorganization notice and the Medicare and Medicaid proposal. Two items in that batch went unreported.

The Labor Department [proposed a national minimum weekly work-search requirement](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/10/2026-22903/unemployment-insurance-program-national-minimum-weekly-work-search-requirements) for unemployment compensation. The rule would amend 20 CFR part 604 to require states to make each claimant complete at least five qualifying work-search activities a week, or the equivalent under a state points system; to have the claimant report those activities as part of certification before payment issues; and to count qualifying activities beyond direct employer contacts. The department's stated purpose is to reduce variation among state requirements that local labor markets do not explain. Comments close January 11, 2027.

The Trade Representative [extended all 178 current product exclusions](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/10/2026-22925/notice-of-product-exclusion-extensions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology) from the Section 301 investigation of China's technology-transfer and intellectual property practices through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027. The notice extends products already excluded; it adds none.

## The courts

The Supreme Court filed nothing in the window. The D.C. Circuit filed one opinion.

In [Michael Abramowitz v. Kari Lake](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2026/11/25-5314.pdf), No. 25-5314, the panel affirmed a permanent injunction barring the U.S. Agency for Global Media from removing Michael Abramowitz as director of Voice of America without the approval of a majority of the International Broadcasting Advisory Board. Judge Pillard disposed of the government's jurisdictional argument first: Congress did not intend claims under 22 U.S.C. § 6205(e) to run through the Civil Service Reform Act's Merit Systems Protection Board, because that scheme cannot award preliminary relief, and channeling the claim there "would effectively nullify Congress's decision to make the Broadcasting Board the guardian of the independence of the networks' leadership." The provision, she wrote, "is not a standard employment law, but a durable barrier against any breach of the firewall protecting the journalistic integrity of Voice of America and the affiliate networks."

On the merits, the court held the removal restriction consistent with Article II. Abramowitz is an inferior officer, the Board is a department head, and the President keeps control because the Board's members serve at his will. His power to remove Abramowitz survives, the court said, "albeit more indirectly than defendants would like." The opinion notes that six of the seven seats on the Broadcasting Board remain empty.

The morning prints, collected around 06:00 CT on Wednesday: the VIX was 18.50, down 0.96 or 4.93 percent; gold was $4,056.80 an ounce, up $26.60; front-month WTI crude was $86.59 a barrel, up $0.55; and the dollar index was 102.526, up 0.295. Those are pre-market quotes, taken before the cash market opened.

Treasury's November 10 row ran 4.73 percent at two years, 4.98 at ten and 5.35 at thirty, a 2s10s spread of +0.25 against +0.32 on November 9. The two-year rose 0.17 of a point and the ten-year 0.10, so yields rose across the curve and the curve flattened. The quote service had the ten-year at 5.00 percent as of 5:05 p.m. eastern time on November 10, up 0.138 of a point; Treasury's official daily curve put it at 4.98. Total public debt outstanding was $40,559,796,589,243 on November 9, up $7,045,570,151 from November 6; $32,763,773,414,012 of that was held by the public and $7,796,023,175,231 was intragovernmental.

## The economy

October is now in the Bureau of Labor Statistics series. The all-items consumer price index read 337.511, which is 0.571 points above September and 2.531 above August. Computed from the index levels, September-to-October is a rise of 0.17 percent, against 0.59 percent from August to September. That is the first deceleration in the three months reported here, and it is the number yesterday's edition said would test the divergence between prices and the labor market.

The employment side did not move. Unemployment was 4.1 percent in October, a tenth below September's 4.2. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,293 thousand in October, 165 thousand above September and 225 thousand above August.

Taken together, the three readings now say something different from what they said yesterday. Prices rose at a markedly slower pace in October while unemployment held at 4.1 percent and payrolls grew by 165,000. The earlier gap between a firm labor market and a hot price index has narrowed from the price side, in one month, and one month is not a trend.

## Elections and the midterms

AP's ledger, as [Votelect](https://votelect.com/) displays it, read 253 Democrats to 166 Republicans in the House with 16 races uncalled, stamped 11:09 a.m. Pacific time on November 10. That is one seat more for each party and two fewer uncalled than the stamp this report used yesterday. The Senate ledger, unchanged since 12:27 a.m. Pacific time on November 4, read 54 Democrats to 44 Republicans with two uncalled.

Mississippi is one of the two. AP's percentages, as Votelect carries them, had Scott Colom at 49.1 and Cindy Hyde-Smith at 48.8. [WLBT reported Wednesday](https://www.wlbt.com/2026/11/11/mississippi-us-senate-race-undecided-week-after-election-day/) that Colom led "by more than 2,800 votes in unofficial results" and that counties have until Friday to certify. The race is uncalled, and this report will not infer a call from a margin.

Alaska is the other. AP had Mary Peltola at 48.6 percent and Senator Dan Sullivan at 47.5. The state's ranked-choice tabulation [is set for November 18](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2026/11/05/despite-bloodbath-election-night-republicans-sen-sullivan-confident-hell-win-senate-race/), and it occurs only if no candidate clears 50 percent; Alaska accepts absentee ballots for fifteen days after Election Day.

Polymarket priced both. Colom stood at 99.2 percent on $51,156 of twenty-four-hour volume and Hyde-Smith at 0.5 percent on $44,304; Peltola at 99.0 percent on $100,343 and Sullivan at 0.9 percent on $98,578. The chamber contracts put Chuck Schumer at 85.5 percent to be Senate majority leader, against 13.7 percent for Chris Van Hollen, and Hakeem Jeffries at 94.3 percent to be Speaker. Schumer stood at 91.0 percent in yesterday's edition, so the book moved while the count did not. Those are the venue's prices, not this report's view.

## The world

The trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi ended Wednesday with an agreement on a large prisoner exchange. [TASS reported](https://tass.com/politics/2216133) that Russia's chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said the sides had reached "a fundamental agreement on another large-scale prisoner exchange," with details to follow; he gave no number and no timetable. [The Moscow Times reported](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/11/11/russia-and-ukraine-agreed-to-prisoner-exchange-after-latest-peace-talks-kremlin-adviser-says-a94227) that the American envoy Jared Kushner confirmed the exchange, that no figure or category of prisoner had been disclosed, and that distrust remains a major obstacle. Medinsky said of the security discussions, "In some places we reached an understanding, in other places we did not."

No ceasefire was announced. The Moscow Times reported that post-war security arrangements for Ukraine and Europe, Europe's participation, and the territorial question all remained unresolved. Medinsky said Moscow did not understand what status the European officials had, and he argued that European military support for Kyiv kept Europe from being a serious negotiator.

Kushner and Witkoff were to fly to Moscow on Thursday to brief Vladimir Putin, then meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Ireland. The prisoner exchange, if it holds, would be the first concrete result of the two-day round.
