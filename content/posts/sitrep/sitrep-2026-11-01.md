A federal judge in Miami dismissed President Trump's defamation suit against The Wall Street Journal on Saturday, with prejudice, ending his second attempt at the case and barring another. The refiled complaint sought $10 billion. U.S. District Judge Darrin Gayles had thrown out an earlier version in April, and the order holds that the president did not plausibly allege actual malice, the standard that requires knowledge a statement was false or reckless disregard for whether it was. The same weekend, Qatar's liquefied natural gas carrier *Al Mayeda* was struck by what its owner called unidentified objects in the Gulf on Friday evening, a company statement said on Sunday. No crew member was hurt and no party has claimed the attack. Crude closed the week above $102 a barrel, and a struck gas carrier in the Gulf is the kind of fact an oil market prices at Monday's open.

## The courts

The Supreme Court and the D.C. Circuit filed no opinions in the window. Two new matters were docketed in the District of Columbia on Saturday: [Stoekl v. United States Citizenship and Immigration Services](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75055065/stoekl-v-united-states-citizenship-and-immigration-services/), 1:26-cv-03839, and [District of Columbia v. National Park Service](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75053865/district-of-columbia-v-national-park-service/), 1:26-cv-03838. Both are captions and numbers. The dockets carry no nature-of-suit field, so this report can say who sued whom and not why. They follow eleven matters docketed on October 30.

Of the five watched dockets, one moved. [D.C. Preservation League v. Department of the Interior](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72278895/dc-preservation-league-v-department-of-interior/), 1:26-cv-00477, recorded three entries on October 31, two of them minute entries and the third docket entry 119, the next number after the twelve entries of October 30 that ran through 118. The East Potomac case has now moved four days running, after entries on October 28, 29 and 30. [Beatty v. Trump](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72069932/beatty-v-trump/), the Kennedy Center matter, [MSW Media, Inc. v. Department of Justice](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70999320/msw-media-inc-v-department-of-justice/), and Phang v. Blanche in both the district court and the consolidated D.C. Circuit appeal recorded nothing.

Outside the district, the ruling that matters is Judge Gayles's. The 20-page order dismissed the amended complaint against the Journal, its parent News Corp, Rupert Murdoch and others, and because it was the second dismissal it came with prejudice. The holding rests on the pleading standard: the president did not plausibly allege that each defendant published a defamatory statement with actual malice. The paper had argued that its article reported the president's denial, that the letter was made public by the House Oversight Committee, and that a bawdy letter to a friend is not inherently defamatory. A spokesperson for the president's legal team said the team would keep pressing the case, and a source familiar with its strategy told CBS that an appeal is likely. A dismissal with prejudice is a judgment about the pleading, not a finding about the letter, and the difference between the two will be the whole of any appeal.

## The economy

The BLS published no release in this window, so the newest readings are September's and stand as they did yesterday. Unemployment was 4.2 percent in September, against 4.1 percent in August and in July. Total nonfarm payrolls were 159,044 thousand, 29 thousand above August's 159,015 thousand. The three taken together say what they said a week ago: prices rising a little faster month over month than they were at midsummer, hiring flat, and a ten-year yield near five percent that is neither fighting the first nor comforted by the second.

## The world

Qatar Gas Transport Company said in a statement published through the Qatar Stock Exchange on Sunday that its carrier *Al Mayeda* was struck by unidentified objects on Friday evening. The company said no crew member was hurt and no environmental harm was reported, that the vessel was in safe and operational condition, and that the effect of the incident was still being evaluated. No government or group claimed responsibility, and the report names no suspect. The details are the owner's own and nothing more has been confirmed.

The context is the blockade. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz during the war and has warned ships passing without its authorization that they face destruction, and attacks on shipping rose after the United States reimposed a blockade on Iran's southern ports in July, according to the same report. More than 120 commercial vessels have been hit since the war began in February, and October was the worst month of it, with at least 29 ships struck against 20 in March. A Qatari gas carrier is a long way from an American blockade, and the weekend put the two in the same water.

The prediction markets give the truce two more days. The contract on the US-Iran ceasefire continuing through November 2 stands at 96.6 percent, and the Israel-Iran ceasefire through the same date at 98.2 percent. A contract asking whether Washington announces an end to the Iranian blockade by November 2 is at 2.9 percent, down from 12.5 percent on yesterday's board, which is a repricing in the direction the weekend's news points. The market on the Iranian regime falling before 2027 is at 4.5 percent. The venue also prices Israel's next prime minister, with Benjamin Netanyahu at 37.0 percent, and a Bulgarian presidential election, with Iliana Iotova at 86.5 percent.
