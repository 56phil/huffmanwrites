*This is satire. The statement is a device. Every line item is real, dated, and sourced.*

**STATEMENT OF ACCOUNT**

Account holder: the United States
Opened: February 28, 2026
Status: effectively over
Balance: carried forward

On November 9, after a closed-door meeting with the prime minister of Qatar, the President came out and closed the account. "Iran militarily is effectively over," he said. What remained was housekeeping: "We're just keeping it the status quo, and we're doing that really at the behest of countries that are their neighbours."

A status quo is something a government keeps, the way it keeps a fleet or a ledger. The week's record is the itemization.

**Item: the blockade.** The President described the arrangement as "a continuation of the greatest naval blockade in the history of our country." The defense secretary, speaking the same week, put it in the present tense: "The ironclad blockade, UNBROKEN." The secretary of state explained why no combat is required: "The economic campaign [against Iran] is so devastating that right now there is no need for major combat operations." On the administration's account the war is over, and its chief instrument is continuing.

**Item: the fleet.** On Monday, a U.S. official told Al Jazeera that the USS Theodore Roosevelt had arrived in the Middle East, which brings the total number of American carrier strike groups operating in the region to three. The USS George Washington and the USS George H.W. Bush are the other two. A second official said the Roosevelt arrived on November 15, and the Pentagon and CENTCOM have not formally confirmed it. A conflict that is over is one in which a third carrier group arrives as a report rather than an announcement.

**Item: the shipping.** Within a 24-hour span, Iran's IRIB reported four tankers "halted" in the Strait of Hormuz. Two more were hit on November 10, one said to be under U.S. escort. A tanker was struck by a projectile off Oman.

**Item: the neighbors, at whose behest.** Saudi Arabia reported attacks since early October that "killed 20 people and closed Riyadh's airport for two weeks." These are the countries for whose sake the status quo is kept.

**Item: the nuclear program.** The defense secretary reported that "by every measure, we achieved a historic MILITARY VICTORY," and that more than 80 percent of Iran's nuclear industrial base is gone. United Nations experts believe Iran retains "about 972 pounds of 60% enriched uranium." Eighty percent destroyed and 972 pounds retained are two different accounts, and only one of them is a victory.

**Item: the offer.** The United States offered $20 million for information about Iran's nuclear program.

**Item: the deal.** Last week the President told Vice President Vance that "We don't need a deal with Iran." In a Truth Social post, he summarized the balance: "We've got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we've got their Strait!"

A war that is over does not usually need to be kept. This one is kept by a naval blockade, a task force for the land routes and the Caspian, three carrier groups, and a standing offer of twenty million dollars for information. The word for that is not peace. The word in the statement is status quo, which, in this statement, is the term for a balance that has not been settled.

The itemization is not an editorial flourish. It is the government's own description of what it is doing: the blockade described as a continuation, the fleet as a rotation, the war itself as the status quo. Each line is a claim that the thing is finished, submitted alongside the charge for continuing it.

The President's phrase was "effectively over." The statement is itemized; the balance is carried forward.
