Whether the second Trump administration has damaged American self-government is no longer a matter of opinion. It is a matter of measurement, and the people who measure it have reported. The open questions are narrower and harder: how much of the damage is structural, how much is reversible, and what a citizen who wants to oppose it can do on an ordinary Tuesday. This essay takes up the record first, because the argument rests on it, and then the work, because a record is not a plan.

## The Verdict Is In

The broadest measurement comes from V-Dem, the democracy index built at the University of Gothenburg from the work of more than four thousand scholars. Its 2026 report records a threshold that had not been crossed in half a century. "The USA loses its long-term status as a liberal democracy," the report states, "for the first time in over 50 years." Democracy in the United States, by V-Dem's own measure, "has fallen back to the same level as in 1965." The sharpest single collapse is in the constraint on the executive: Legislative Constraints, the report finds, is "losing one-third of its value in 2025" and has reached "its lowest point in over one hundred years." Civil rights and freedom of expression are at their lowest levels in sixty years ([V-Dem Institute, 2026](#sources)).

The rankings agree with the index. V-Dem lowered the United States from twentieth to fifty-first among 179 countries, placing it between Slovakia and Greece. Bright Line Watch, which surveys hundreds of American political scientists, concluded that the system now sits "nearly midway between liberal democracy and dictatorship." Freedom House recorded the United States among the largest single-year declines in political rights and civil liberties of any free country ([Langfitt, 2026a](#sources)).

Staffan Lindberg, V-Dem's founding director, put the speed in comparative terms: "Under the Trump administration, democracy has been rolled back as much during just one year as it took Modi in India and Erdogan in Turkey 10 years to accomplish, and Orban in Hungary four years." Consider what that sentence means. The leaders he names spent a decade or more dismantling the referees of their own systems. One American year matched them.

The White House response was to dismiss the researchers rather than the finding. A spokeswoman called the study "a ridiculous claim made by an irrelevant, blatantly biased organization," and President Trump has said of the charge, "I don't like a dictator. I'm not a dictator." Note what the reply does not contain: no rebuttal of a fact, only an attack on the standing of the person who measured it.

## The Mechanism, Not the Adjective

What is happening is not a coup. It is an accretion, and it has a name. Steven Levitsky, who has spent a career studying how democracies die, calls it competitive authoritarianism. "I would argue that the United States in 2025-26 has slid into a mild form of competitive authoritarianism," he told NPR. "I think it's reversible, but this is authoritarianism" ([Langfitt, 2026b](#sources)).

The name matters because it describes a specific machine. In a competitive authoritarian regime, elections still happen, and the opposition can still win one. What changes is the field. The incumbent turns the state's own referees, its prosecutors, its regulators, its police, against the people who are supposed to hold him to account. Competition survives; fairness does not. That is the distance between a country that has had a bad government and a country that has lost the ability to remove one.

## The Instruments of Capture

Each of the following is a documented use of state power, not a policy disagreement.

**The justice system.** The Department of Justice has opened investigations and prosecutions against the president's perceived opponents, among them the former FBI director, a state attorney general, the chair of the Federal Reserve, and multiple sitting senators and representatives. It has investigated civil-society organizations aligned with the opposition, blacklisted major law firms, and withheld federal research funding from universities ([Levitsky, Way, and Ziblatt, 2026](#sources)).

**The map.** The president pressed Republican-controlled states into mid-decade redistricting in order to manufacture House seats, an effort openly aimed at securing a majority that the voters might otherwise withhold. An internal party document projected a net gain of ten seats from the scheme.

**The press.** The administration sued major news organizations and leaned on the broadcast regulator, the Federal Communications Commission, which opened investigations into the licenses of PBS, NPR, ABC, CBS, and NBC. It briefly forced a late-night host off the air by threatening the licenses of a network's local stations. "We can do this the easy way or the hard way," the commission's chairman warned a corporate parent ([Langfitt, 2026b](#sources)). Several large media owners responded by softening their coverage, which is precisely the point of the pressure.

**The police.** Immigration and Customs Enforcement, in the space of a year, has come to resemble a paramilitary force more than a law-enforcement agency: masked, heavily armed agents operating in American cities, detaining and deporting people at a scale without recent precedent. In the same period the president pardoned roughly sixteen hundred January 6 defendants, including people convicted of assaulting police officers. The message sent to anyone inclined to act on the president's behalf is not subtle.

## The Cost in Blood

On January 7, 2026, a federal immigration agent shot and killed Renee Good, a thirty-seven-year-old American citizen, in her car in Minneapolis during an enforcement surge ([Evans and Hickman, 2026](#sources)). Eighteen days later, on January 24, Border Patrol agents shot and killed Alex Pretti, a thirty-seven-year-old ICU nurse and a United States citizen, on a Minneapolis street ([Sanchez et al., 2026](#sources)). A third person, a Venezuelan immigrant, was shot and wounded in the same operation. An immigration official had warned colleagues weeks earlier, in a meeting documented by CNN, that "someone's going to get killed" ([Tapper, 2026](#sources)).

The conduct of the investigations is its own indictment. Minnesota and Hennepin County sued the administration for withholding evidence. State investigators say federal agents first agreed to cooperate at the scenes, then took control of the physical evidence; at the Pretti shooting, federal authorities physically blocked state investigators from reaching the scene. Months later, the evidence had still not been turned over, and no federal officer had been charged ([Anderson, 2026](#sources); [Associated Press, 2026](#sources)). Two citizens are dead at the hands of the federal government, and the government's answer has been to keep the file closed.

## The Bill That Comes Due

The damage is not confined to institutions. It reaches the household budget, and it does so in the ordinary, unremarkable arithmetic that citizens meet every month.

In late February 2026, the United States and Israel launched military operations against Iran. Iran retaliated by attacking shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a quarter of the world's seaborne crude oil and petroleum products pass ([Congressional Research Service, 2026](#sources)). Economists at the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas modeled the result: West Texas Intermediate crude peaking near ninety-four dollars a barrel, headline inflation lifted by roughly six-tenths of a percentage point for the year ([Kilian et al., 2026](#sources)). A separate Dallas Fed analysis found that the shipping disruption from the closed strait "completely undoes" the disinflationary effect of tariffs the Supreme Court had struck down, so that the two shocks cancel and leave the price pressure standing ([Reyes-Heroles and Aanenson, 2026](#sources)).

The cost then arrives where people live. In September, consumer prices rose 3.7 percent over the year, and the wage-earner price index behind Social Security's annual adjustment rose 3.9 percent ([Horsley, 2026](#sources)). The Social Security Administration announced a 2027 cost-of-living adjustment of 3.6 percent ([Social Security Administration, 2026](#sources)). A retiree receiving $2,017 a month will get seventy-two dollars more, and that increase is meant to cover a year of prices that rose faster than the increase itself. This is the arithmetic of a government that spends its authority on a war it cannot finish and sends the bill to people who cannot refuse it.

## Why "Fascist" Is the Wrong Word

Many people, watching all of this, reach for the strongest word available and call it fascism. The instinct is understandable. The word is wrong, and it matters that it is wrong.

Fascism is a term of analysis with a definition, not a synonym for tyranny. Roger Griffin defines it by its mythic core, a promise of national rebirth out of decadence. Robert Paxton defines it by behavior: a mass party of committed nationalist militants, working in concert with traditional elites, abandoning democratic liberties and pursuing redemptive violence without legal restraint. Michael Mann places paramilitary organization at the center of the concept. Each of these definitions describes a movement that seeks to abolish electoral competition and replace the state with a party. What the United States has now is a different thing: a government that has hollowed out the state's impartial machinery while leaving the machinery, and the elections, formally in place.

Competitive authoritarianism is not a softer charge than fascism. It is a more specific one, and it fits the evidence. Precision is not a courtesy to the accused. It is what keeps a true description from being dismissed as hyperbole, which is the fate of every accurate word spent too freely.

## The Defense

Here is the part that matters more than the diagnosis, and the part the record itself supports. Competitive authoritarianism is not a finished state. It is a contest, and it is contested. V-Dem's report notes that "electoral components of democracy, however, remain stable, for now." The last two words are load-bearing. The elections still work, which means the citizens still hold a mechanism, and the mechanism is the one that has never yet been unavailable.

What can be done, beginning today:

**Vote, and do more than vote.** The 2026 midterms are the nearest lever, and they are real. Opposition parties have won unfair elections in Malaysia, Zambia, Poland, Botswana, and Hungary within the past decade, because a tilted field is not a closed one. Show up, and then do the work that surrounds a vote: register others, work as a poll worker, serve as a poll watcher, drive people to the precinct, and run for something small, because the people who run for school board and county clerk today are the people who defend the machinery of elections tomorrow.

**Use the courts and the states.** The judiciary has not been captured. The Supreme Court itself struck down the tariffs; Bright Line Watch's co-directors credit the courts with preventing a further slide ([Langfitt, 2026a](#sources)). Minnesota's attorney general and Hennepin County's attorney general sued the federal government over withheld evidence in the killings, and they are still in court. American elections are administered by thousands of local officials, not by the president, and that decentralization is the hardest part of the system to seize.

**Make Congress a check.** One house is enough to hold hearings, compel testimony, subpoena documents, and refuse to fund what it does not authorize. V-Dem found legislative constraint at its lowest point in a century, which is the measure of how much room there is to recover.

**Fund the counterweights.** Independent local journalism, civil-liberties organizations, and universities are the institutions the administration is pressuring precisely because they still resist. Subscribe to the reporting that does the work. Give to the organizations that litigate. The pressure is a compliment to their effectiveness.

**Refuse the demoralization.** An authoritarian project runs on the belief that nothing can be done, that the game is fixed, that participation is pointless. Every one of those claims is falsified by the record: the reversals are real, the courts have held, the opposition has won unfair elections elsewhere, and the regime is still behaving as though it has not yet won, because it has not.

## What This Asks For

The founders of this country assumed the worst about power and designed a system to survive it. They did not design a savior. They designed a habit: the habit of a citizenry that pays attention, shows up, and refuses to be worn down. That habit is the defense, and it is not located in Washington. It is located in the precinct, the courtroom, the newsroom, and the checkbook, in the ordinary places where a republic is actually maintained.

The record compiled here is damning. It is also incomplete, and the incompleteness is the only hopeful thing in it. A government that had fully captured the country would not need to sue the press, redraw the map, and hide the evidence from a state investigator. It does all three because it has not won, and because it knows that it has not. What it has is a head start. Head starts are exactly what an engaged citizenry is good at erasing.
