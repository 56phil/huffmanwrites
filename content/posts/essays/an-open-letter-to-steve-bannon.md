Mr. Bannon:

I am writing to you directly because you have spent a decade insisting that your opponents argue with you rather than about you, and because the case I want to make is not that you are a villain. It is that you are losing, that you have been losing for some time, and that the manner of the losing has cost the country more than a clean defeat would have.

I will start with what you got right. A letter that opens with contempt is a letter you are entitled to throw away.

## What you got right

You said the Republican Party would break into civil war the day after the midterms, and it did, inside the week ([Financial Times, 1 November 2026](https://www.afr.com/world/north-america/steve-bannon-republicans-will-erupt-in-civil-war-after-midterms-20261101-p61aci)). You said Ken Paxton was "in trouble" in Texas when the party's own numbers said otherwise ([Benzinga, 1 November 2026](https://www.benzinga.com/news/politics/26/11/62765377/steve-bannon-predicts-gop-civil-war-after-midterms-says-elon-musks-spending-is-a-wake-up-call-for-trump)), and he lost the seat by twelve ([Council on Foreign Relations, 10 November 2026](https://www.cfr.org/articles/2026-election-by-the-numbers)). You called a House majority a "miracle" while the consultants were still billing for a comfortable hold, and you were closer to the truth than they were.

Your theory of the midterm was also the correct theory. Midterms turn on the base, and the base is decided by whether low-propensity voters come out. That is a real mechanism, and you named it before the people paid to name it. It simply ran against you this time, at a scale for which your model had no room.

You also went to four months in prison for refusing to testify before a committee of Congress ([CBS News, 11 October 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/steve-bannon-on-the-gop-and-the-midterms-we-cant-afford-to-lose/)). Whatever one thinks of the refusal, it was not the act of a man hedging. Nothing in what follows is written by someone who thinks you lack conviction. The problem is not that you have too little of it.

## The ledger of requests

Consider the year as a list of requests.

You asked the administration to declare a national emergency over the midterms and to station armed immigration agents at polling places, first in February and then repeatedly through the summer, in the phrase you used yourself, an "up-armored ICE presence" ([Newsweek, 18 August 2026](https://www.newsweek.com/trump-ally-demands-national-emergency-to-place-ice-at-midterm-poll-stations-12338733)). It did not happen. You asked Congress to pass the SAVE America Act. It has not passed. In the final weeks you told your audience that the polling was a "psyop" designed to depress conservative turnout ([Media Matters, 5 November 2026](https://www.mediamatters.org/election-denial-voter-fraud-and-suppression/election-denial-movement-failed-it-will-be-back)), and the polls then erred in the one direction that flattered your opponents: the closing averages had Democrats ahead by nine, and they won by thirteen ([Council on Foreign Relations, 10 November 2026](https://www.cfr.org/articles/2026-election-by-the-numbers)).

You asked, above all, that the count be treated as an enemy. The count arrived anyway.

There is a kind of political figure whose demands are refused and who is therefore irrelevant. You are not that kind, which is why this letter is uncomfortable to write. You are the harder kind: the figure whose diagnosis is accepted, whose remedy is declined, and whose name is then left off the announcement.

## The night it became visible

On election night, told that NBC's exit polling had men splitting 49 to 48 for the Republicans, after Donald Trump had won them by twelve two years earlier, you said: "That can't be right" ([Daily Beast, 4 November 2026](https://www.thedailybeast.com/steve-bannon-stunned-by-trumps-maga-disaster-among-men)).

That is the sentence to which I keep returning. Ten years of a movement built on the premise that the numbers are rigged against it, and when the numbers came for your own coalition the first reflex was still disbelief. The Wall Street Journal's analysis of the same exits put the Republican advantage among men at a single point. Not a wobble. A collapse in the one demographic your theory of politics could not survive losing.

## The arithmetic

Here is what the country did, in aggregate, on 3 November.

Turnout was 49.1 percent of the voting-eligible population, the second highest in any midterm since 1914. Democrats won the national House vote by an estimated thirteen points, the second largest margin in any midterm since 1942, and did it while being outspent. They won every major demographic group except white voters without a college degree. Voters aged 18 to 29 moved 32 points toward the Democrats in two years, and Asian and Latino voters moved 21. Republicans spent the cycle on a mid-decade redistricting meant to be worth fifteen seats, and won two of the fifteen ([Council on Foreign Relations, 10 November 2026](https://www.cfr.org/articles/2026-election-by-the-numbers)).

That is not a mood. A mood removes incumbents, and moods revert. Nebraska is the control group: Dan Osborn ran as an independent on the explicit theory that the Democratic label was the liability rather than the policies, and lost by six points, in a state where no Democrat was on the ballot to punish ([PollCheck, 2026 midterm results](https://www.pollcheck.com/midterms-2026/results)). Voters were not angry at officeholders in general. They were angry at one party, and where that party's name was missing from the ballot the incumbent walked through.

A sympathetic columnist described your method earlier this year in exactly these terms: the total capture of the party's internal operations, meaning primaries, committees, donor pipelines, and candidate vetting ([Boston Globe, 23 February 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/02/23/opinion/steve-bannon-maga-after-trump-elections/)). You captured it. It then lost by thirteen points in a midterm whose turnout was the second highest since 1914.

## The thing you said you were focused on

In January you told Axios that you were not running for anything in 2028, that your actual work was a third term for Donald Trump, and that "you can drive a Mack Truck through the 22nd Amendment" ([Axios, 10 January 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/10/steve-bannon-2028-campaign-maga)). You were helping with a book making the constitutional case, and you said on television that on the afternoon of 20 January 2029 the president would be serving his third term ([IBTimes, 27 March 2026](https://www.ibtimes.com/steve-bannon-working-five-six-plans-keep-trump-office-third-term-3800415)).

In October you told CBS that he appeared uninterested, "or it just won't happen" ([CBS News, 11 October 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/steve-bannon-on-the-gop-and-the-midterms-we-cant-afford-to-lose/)). The president had said a year earlier that it was "pretty clear" he was not allowed to run ([NBC News, 29 October 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/trump-rule-out-third-term-constitutional-amendment-rcna240438)).

So the project you named as the reason for everything, the one you were building toward, was withdrawn by the principal and then conceded by you, on the record, three weeks before the election you were trying to win. Movements rarely survive their founders admitting that the succession plan is void, in the middle of a campaign, in a tone of resignation.

## The succession

You called for Susie Wiles to be removed, and she was gone the same day ([The Hill, 6 November 2026](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6204435-bannon-calls-wiles-removal/)). That looks like power. Read the second half of it. The job went to Stephen Miller, three days after the election ([BBC News, 8 November 2026](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm3r5gnxqz3jo)). You were the man whose complaint was answered and whose name was left out of the answer.

The terms of the postmortem were set by the vice president. JD Vance blamed the Iran war, called for a recalibration of the relationship with Israel, and did it in a statement the White House knew was coming ([The Independent, 7 November 2026](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/jd-vance-iran-war-susie-wiles-trump-b3081105.html)). You spent the same week blaming the economy and saying, "I don't think we implemented the Maga programme" ([BBC News, 8 November 2026](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm3r5gnxqz3jo)). Those are two accounts of one defeat, and the one that will organize the 2028 primary is not yours.

A strategist who is consulted and not obeyed is a strategist in decline. That is close to a definition, and it is yours now.

## The microphone

You will say the audience settles the question. It is worth looking at the audience.

The New York Times assembled several years of audience data across podcasts, YouTube, X and Rumble for twelve conservative creators, you among them, and reported that far more of them have lost audience since 2024 than gained ([AOL, 8 August 2026](https://www.aol.com/articles/maga-influencers-audiences-dwindle-donald-132900000.html); [Nieman Lab, 3 August 2026](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/the-maga-media-machine-is-fracturing/)). The measurement is contested, and the disagreement is itself the story: the tracker Podscan puts the podcast at roughly 196,000 monthly listeners, against a team claim of a following "well into the millions," on a show distributed through a partisan network, Real America's Voice, and a podcast feed ([Edgepedia, 17 September 2026](https://www.edgepedia.org/war-room-podcast)).

Seven years, four hours a day, six days a week, and the number is not the size of the movement you describe. That is not an insult; it is the arithmetic of a medium that rewards intensity over reach. Intensity is real, and it is why your listeners act. It is also why the medium cannot substitute for reach when the question is how a country votes, and the country voted.

## The wrong side

Now the part you will reject, and the part about which I would most like to be wrong.

You are on the wrong side of history, and the proof is not that you lost. Everyone loses. The proof is what you are teaching your audience about how to lose.

In October you said, on camera, that "the core belief of the Maga movement is we won the 2020 election." Reminded that more than sixty courts had rejected the claim, you answered: "We can go debate this another time, but we didn't have standing" ([CBS News, 11 October 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/steve-bannon-on-the-gop-and-the-midterms-we-cant-afford-to-lose/)). A movement whose founding proposition is that the count is a lie cannot read a true count when one arrives. That is why the week after a thirteen-point defeat produced a hunt for traitors instead of a diagnosis, and why election night produced a search for someone to blame while a dozen races were still being counted ([Associated Press, 10 November 2026](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-elections-mississippi-alaska-congress-vote-count-90ce111a971a0f70ba938d27fe69f65d)).

A republic is not kept by the side that wins. It is kept by the side that loses and comes back, and that only works if the side that loses believes the counting. You have spent a decade telling millions of people that the counting is the fraud. Whatever you intended, the effect is a country in which one coalition will accept a result and one will not, and a movement that cannot afford to admit it has been beaten, because being beaten is the one fact its theory cannot absorb.

That is the wrong side. Not because the future belongs to your opponents, which nobody can know, but because you have made yourself into the man who cannot be told the answer.

## What I would ask

I am not asking you to change your politics. That would be a strange request in an open letter, and I do not believe you would grant it.

I am asking one thing, and it is smaller than it sounds. When the next count goes against you, say so plainly, in the same voice you use for the victories, and say it before the lawyers assemble. It costs you a week of your audience's affection. It buys the country something it currently cannot get from anyone in your position: a demonstration that a man who spent his life on one side of an argument can accept a verdict on the other without pretending the referee was bought.

You told Ted Koppel that this president belongs with Washington and Lincoln, that Washington was the birth of the nation and Lincoln its rebirth. Lincoln lost elections. He lost them, conceded them, and ran again. What made his side right in the end was not that it never lost. It was that it kept believing the count while it lost, which is the only reason the other side can ever be asked to do the same.

You are seventy-two. The Congress that convenes in fifty-three days is not the one you wanted. There is still time to be on the right side of one thing, and it happens to be the thing you have spent the longest getting wrong.

Sincerely,

Philip
