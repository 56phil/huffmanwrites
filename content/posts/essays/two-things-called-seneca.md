A reader who comes here looking for Seneca finds a Roman who could not reliably breathe, who tutored an emperor, and who died on that emperor's order. A reader who comes to the news looking for Seneca finds a federal political committee that bought advertising in Pennsylvania last summer and is raising money for the midterms. Both exist. They share a word and nothing else.

That is worth setting down once, in one place, because the collision is live: one search returns both, and a sentence written about one reads perfectly well as a sentence about the other. A reader who has just heard the name on a podcast has no reason to know which one is meant.

## What the filing says

The organization's legal name is The Seneca Project Inc. Its registration with the Federal Election Commission, dated 23 April 2024, classifies it as a quarterly super PAC, independent-expenditure-only, and unauthorized. The last word is not a reproach. It means no candidate authorized the committee, so it spends on its own account rather than as anyone's campaign arm.

The committee was formed for the 2024 presidential cycle, when a statement issued five days before the election described it as "a bipartisan superPAC, led by women, dedicated to mobilizing women voters in key swing states in support of Kamala Harris' election" ([statement on the Madison Square Garden rally](https://www.senecaproject.us/news/statement-from-the-seneca-project-on-donald-trumps-hate-rally-in-nyc), 28 October 2024). Setmayer, a former Republican communications director on Capitol Hill who left the party in November 2020, is its chief executive. Both founders worked at the Lincoln Project before this, and Setmayer describes her co-founder as a Democrat ([Two Years](https://www.marytrump.org/p/two-years)).

## Seneca Falls, 1848

The name is not the philosopher's, and the committee has said where it comes from:

> The Seneca Project came from the birthplace of the Women's Rights Movement in Seneca Falls, New York in 1848, the Seneca Falls Convention.

Tara Setmayer, [Two Years](https://www.marytrump.org/p/two-years), The Good in Us, 24 June 2024.

Seneca Falls is a town in upstate New York where, in July 1848, several hundred people met to demand the vote for women. The committee borrows the town. The Stoic material on this site belongs to a different man, in a different century, with no relationship to the organization.

## What it produces

Advertising, mostly. The committee's account of the 2024 cycle centers on a music-driven spot called "American Girl," released in the last week of the presidential campaign, which it says drew more than two million views in its first forty-eight hours ([Webby submission](https://www.senecaproject.us/webby-american-girl)). Jonathan Last, writing in The Bulwark three days before the election, put it this way:

> This video from the Super PAC Seneca Project is probably the best ad of the cycle.

Jonathan V. Last, [Women, for the Win](https://www.thebulwark.com/p/women-for-the-win), The Bulwark, 1 November 2024.

In July 2025 the committee ran a digital buy in Pennsylvania's 8th Congressional District, timed to a visit by the vice president, and said the money was part of a broader effort against the administration's second-term agenda ([press release](https://www.senecaproject.us/news/the-seneca-project-counters-jd-vances-big-beautiful-bill-tour-with-scathing-new-ad-big-beautiful-betrayal)). In April 2026 Setmayer introduced the committee to her own audience in plainer terms:

> We are an award winning, content and messaging, women-led pro democracy superPAC fighting the good fight against the MAGA regime.

She added that the committee is "gearing up for the midterms" ([Uncompromised with Tara Setmayer](https://tarasetmayer.substack.com/p/live-the-seneca-projects-the-stakes), 17 April 2026).

## What the money shows

The filing period running from 1 January 2025 through 14 October 2026 shows receipts of $505,452.14 and disbursements of $514,527.78, of which $511,158.34 was operating expenditure. Reported independent expenditures were $0.00, and cash on hand at the close was $5,015.68 ([FEC](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00877027/)).

Those columns describe a production and distribution operation rather than a campaign treasury. Money comes in, most of it goes out as the cost of making and placing content, and within this window nothing is reported as spending for or against a named candidate. That is a description of the ledger, not a judgment of the work: the committee's own account is that it makes messages and puts them where voters are, which is a different job from financing a campaign.

## What it is not

It is not a Stoic society, a publisher, or a charity. It is not connected to this site, and no part of this site's Seneca material is connected to it. A contribution through its site goes to ActBlue as a political contribution rather than as a charitable gift. And it is not a candidate committee, since the filing says no candidate authorized it.

## Telling them apart

Three questions separate the two names. Does the thing have a filing? The committee has an FEC number and quarterly reports; the philosopher has a Loeb edition. What is its address? senecaproject.us belongs to the committee, and the letters belong to every library. What does it make? The committee makes video, while the man wrote prose that has outlasted nineteen centuries without a budget.

Both are legitimate objects of attention, and the purpose here is not to rank them. It is that a name is a claim about identity, and a claim can be checked. The check costs one search and one filing, and it is the same habit that keeps a quotation attached to the right author.
