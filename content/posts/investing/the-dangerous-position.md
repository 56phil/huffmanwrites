Anthropic priced its shares at $83.00 on Wednesday ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering), November 18, 2026). The stock opened Thursday at $111.50, closed the day at $97.17, and closed Friday at $88.91 ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/19/nx-s1-6042558/anthropic-ipo-breaks-records-100-billion), November 19; [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/anthropic-ipo-tech-selloff.html), November 21). In three sessions it traveled from euphoria to fair value and stopped there. The offering itself closed with 1,127,000,000 shares sold, including the underwriters' full option, for gross proceeds of $93.5 billion, plus a concurrent private placement of 313,253,007 Class N shares to seven suppliers and customers at the same $83.00 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close), November 20, 2026).

Around that number the market fell apart. The Nasdaq lost ten percent for the week, its worst since March 2020, and finished the year negative. Nvidia fell 23 percent and closed out its longest weekly losing streak since 2008. The S&P 500 fell 3.70 percent on Friday to 6,910.39, its seventh consecutive loss and the longest streak since February 2020; the Dow joined both indices in correction; the volatility index rose from roughly 20 on Monday to 37 at Friday's close; and among eleven sectors only consumer staples closed higher on Friday ([Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/market-volatility), November 20, 2026).

Anyone holding an artificial intelligence position has to decide what to do about that week. This essay argues that the decision is not a forecast, that the sector is not one thing, and that one part of it is already past the point of cheap repair.

## The test that was administered

The offering was supposed to be the sentiment event of the year. Instead it became the measurement. Morningstar's published fair value for the shares is $92 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/anthropic-ipo-stocks-market.html), November 20, 2026), so by Friday the market had walked a record listing to within four percent of the number a research house had already derived from the prospectus. The first pure-play artificial intelligence lab to reach public markets ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/5-ways-anthropics-ipo-matters-market), November 17, 2026) is now priced on the business rather than on the story, and the business is good: revenue in the first half of the year reached $16.3 billion, up 1,300 percent, with adjusted operating income of $1.6 billion against a loss of $1.8 billion a year earlier ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/19/nx-s1-6042558/anthropic-ipo-breaks-records-100-billion), November 19, 2026). Anthropic reports $1.72 of revenue for every dollar of computing power it buys, against 68 cents a year ago ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/anthropic-ipo-tech-selloff.html), November 21, 2026).

That is not what a bubble looks like at its centre. It is what a durable business looks like with an expensive share price. The distinction matters because the danger in an AI position is not located where most holders think it is.

## One phrase, five exposures

"Reduce AI exposure" is not an instruction anyone can follow, because it names five unrelated risks. They have different owners, different balance sheets, and different failure modes.

| Layer | Who | What the tape now says | What would actually hurt it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leveraged builders | CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN | About half the value lost in a month across the group | A refinancing that clears above 20 percent |
| Chip and memory suppliers | Nvidia, AMD, Micron, Broadcom, SanDisk | Nvidia fell 46 percent over seven weeks and lost more than $2.5 trillion of value since its October 6 record | The accounting question described below |
| Hyperscalers | Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta | Negative free cash flow, growth above 40 percent, self-funding | A cut to the 2027 capital spending level |
| Power, energy, and utilities | Generation, transmission, equipment | Rotating inward as the buildout's binding constraint | Nothing observable yet |
| Index and pension exposure | Any broad fund | The ten largest stocks are about a third of the index | A broad de-rating, which is what just happened |

The first two rows have already repriced, the fifth has just done so, and a single instruction to sell "AI" cannot be correct across all five, and a reader who treats the sector as one asset is making five decisions with one sentence.

## The book, not the price

Here is what changed this week, and it is not the share price of Anthropic.

The Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's accounting. Enforcement staff were reported to be scrutinizing potential accounting issues at the company, with the underlying investigation first reported by The Wall Street Journal on November 17 before Reuters established its focus a day later ([Quartz](https://qz.com/sec-probes-nvidia-accounting-practices-reuters-112026), November 20, 2026; [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-stock-extends-25-trillion-rout-as-sec-probe-reportedly-investigates-its-accounting-194945274.html), November 20, 2026). The Justice Department is separately reviewing Nvidia's $12.9 billion agreement to buy Hugging Face. Nvidia's receivables have stretched to 64 days of sales, the longest since 2022. Interim chief executive Colette Kress told analysts on the November 17 call that the company would be more selective about financing deals across the industry.

Washington is not waiting for the inquiry to conclude. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said of the board and outside counsel: "I think we need answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-stock-extends-25-trillion-rout-as-sec-probe-reportedly-investigates-its-accounting-194945274.html), November 20, 2026).

The market's read was narrower than a sector view and much more useful. Baird's Ted Mortonson, on Yahoo Finance, before the Reuters report: "Every single deal that Nvidia has done on circular finance is going to be put under the microscope" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-stock-extends-25-trillion-rout-as-sec-probe-reportedly-investigates-its-accounting-194945274.html), November 20, 2026).

That is the sentence to keep. The financing of this buildout runs through Nvidia's balance sheet and its customers' debt, and the collateral standing behind a large part of that debt is the hardware itself, borrowed against in chip-backed facilities ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html), November 10, 2026). A question about the accounting of the collateral's supplier is therefore not a legal footnote. It is a question about the value of the security under the loans that fund the least solvent part of the industry. An equity holder who has not asked that question in those terms has not priced the risk he owns.

## What the credit market already says

Equity markets report sentiment. Credit markets report the price of default, and they have been moving for six weeks.

| Instrument | Reading | As of |
|---|---|---|
| CoreWeave 2031 and 2032 bond yields | Above 16 percent | November 6 |
| CoreWeave five-year credit default swaps | 1,289 basis points at the peak; above 1,000 for weeks | November 6 to 10 |
| Nvidia five-year swaps | About 139 basis points, up 76 percent in a month | November 6 |
| Broadcom and Amazon swaps | Up 49 percent and 44 percent in a month | November 6 |
| Oracle swaps | About 298 basis points | November 6 |
| Hyperscaler bond issuance this year | $296 billion, with $440 billion forecast for next year | November 6 |
| CoreWeave debt and cash | $35 billion of debt, $5.5 billion of cash, leverage at 702 percent | November 10 |
| CoreWeave principal due through 2028 | About $15 billion, including $1.3 billion at 15 percent due March 2028 | November 9 to 10 |

Sources for the table: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html), November 6, 2026; [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/coreweave-debt-credit-risk-neoclouds.html), November 9, 2026; [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html), November 10, 2026.

The coverage describes that level as distressed, and CoreWeave has held it through two separate widening episodes. The investment-grade names are nowhere near it: their swaps moved from a low base, and a large percentage move is not the same credit risk as 1,289 basis points. What matters is the direction and the gap. The strong names are paying more to insure, and the weak name is paying what a distressed borrower pays.

Jefferies' Christopher Wood described the mechanism precisely, in a note quoted by CNBC: "The most plausible way the AI capex arms race will end is that the credit card will be taken away" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html), November 6, 2026). It has not been taken away. The rate on it has been raised, twice, in public.

## Two clocks

This is where the honest answer splits, and the split is the whole argument.

**In the fringe, the change is late.** The leveraged builders have already delivered the loss. CoreWeave and Nebius have each lost about half their value in the last month ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/coreweave-debt-credit-risk-neoclouds.html), November 9, 2026). Gartner expects 80 percent of neocloud companies either to be absorbed or to cease operations by 2030 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/microsoft-google-ai-capex-estimates.html), October 29, 2026). Anyone who intended to reduce exposure to that layer and did not has already paid the price of waiting, and a five-year swap spread at 1,289 basis points says the refinancing will be expensive rather than impossible.

**In the core, the change is early.** Nothing about Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, or Meta has broken. Microsoft trimmed its capital spending forecast to $170 billion from $175 billion on delivery delays, which is a trim and not a retreat ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/microsoft-google-ai-capex-estimates.html), October 29, 2026). Hank Smith of Haverford Trust told NPR that the trade may simply "take a little bit of time to heal," and warned that the market has concluded it is "not going to be like the previous sell-offs" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/21/nx-s1-6048729/ai-stock-markets-anthropic-nvidia), November 21, 2026). He could be right, and the argument below does not depend on his being wrong.

**In the portfolio, the change is due now.** Not because the next six months are knowable, and not because the price will fall further. Because the action that matters can only be taken while the market is functioning.

That is the distinction that makes "it is time, if not past time" a defensible sentence rather than a prediction. Sizing a position is a decision you can execute at a price you choose. Deleting one is a decision the market executes at a price it chooses. A holder waiting for certainty about the AI trade will be doing his arithmetic in the week a credit event sets his price, which is the one week in which his arithmetic does not matter.

## What prudence looks like

Five moves, none of which requires a view on whether the bubble bursts.

**1. Measure the exposure you did not choose.** If part of the position is an index fund, the ten largest American companies account for about a third of the index, and the exposure arrived without a decision. Anthropic itself is almost none of it: about five percent of its shares float, which puts it near 0.08 percent of a broad total-market index ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/5-ways-anthropics-ipo-matters-market), November 17, 2026). The danger in an index fund is not the lab; it is the hyperscalers that sell computing to it and own part of it.

**2. Cap the position so a single gap does not change the plan.** Nvidia fell 14 percent in one session this week and 46 percent across seven weeks ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039305/nvda-down-sec-investigation-accounting), November 20, 2026). A position whose size requires the holder to be right about the next Tuesday is too large at any price.

**3. Separate the lender from the borrower.** A share of a company that generates cash and a share of a company that borrows against hardware are different instruments with the same label. The first can wait out a repricing. The second is the repricing.

**4. Read the instrument that prices default.** The five-year swap spread, the bond yield, the private credit fund that gates redemptions. The relevant threshold is public and specific: a CoreWeave refinancing that prices above 20 percent, or a syndication that fails outright. Anything above that line is a different industry from the one being priced today.

**5. Write the boundary down before it is tested.** Decide the maximum percentage of the portfolio that may sit in the fringe layer, and the maximum that may sit in any one AI-adjacent name, and hold those numbers as a rule rather than as an intention. The Stoic term is premeditation of adversity, and it has one practical advantage over analysis: it works from memory rather than from judgment, and judgment is the faculty that fails in a falling market.

## What would prove me wrong

Fairness requires the falsifiers, and there are four.

Hyperscalers raise their 2027 capital spending guidance in January and February rather than trimming it. CoreWeave refinances below 12 percent, which would say the credit market has priced the collateral correctly and the equity market has not. The SEC inquiry closes without an enforcement action and Nvidia restores its usual filing cadence. And the Nasdaq recovers within a few months the way it did after April 2025, when a policy shock resolved quickly because the reality did not match the fear.

Any one of those would move the fringe argument from late to early, and I would say so plainly. What none of them would change is the first principle, which is that a position in a leveraged builder and a position in a cash-generating supplier are not the same risk and should not carry the same size.

This is the third essay in a sequence. [The 2027 Wall](/posts/investing/the-2027-wall/) set out the arithmetic of the buildout and the date by which it must be paid. [What Breaks First](/posts/investing/what-breaks-first/) set out the order in which an unwind would proceed. This one is about the only part of the sequence a reader controls: the size and shape of the position he holds while the order of operations plays out. The credit market is where the schedule is written. The portfolio is where the reader answers it.
