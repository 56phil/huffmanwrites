## Ninety Days From Today

This is the fifth installment of a series I started on September 3. Same calendar discipline, same premise, new window. Ninety days from this morning is **February 22**, a Monday.

The furniture in the room first. Thanksgiving on **November 26**; an OPEC+ meeting on **November 29**; November employment on **December 4**; the Federal Reserve on **December 8 and 9**, with a fresh Summary of Economic Projections; November inflation on **December 10**; a government funding deadline on **December 11**; the White House hearing on Governor Lisa Cook on **December 14**; a Christmas on **December 25**; a new Congress on **January 3**; December employment on **January 8** and December inflation on **January 13**; the Fed again on **January 26 and 27**, with no projections; and the Treasury's next refunding in early February.

The premise is unchanged: no improvement in the war, no breakthrough, no formal reopening of the strait. Brent is a quarter cheaper than it was at the end of October anyway, because supply adapted faster than the strait could close. That divergence is the background to everything below.

Last month this series set a base case of **7,200 to 7,850** for the old window, with an escalation branch down to **4,050**, **5,700**, or **6,260** depending on which historical analog you chose, and a paradox branch above **8,300**. The index breached the base case on the way down and finished three weeks later just inside it. The escalation branch was not reached. The paradox branch was not reached. The call was roughly right for the wrong reason, and the reason is the story.

The previous installment called Nvidia's November 17 report the single dated event that would decide the year. It was not. Nvidia reported revenue of **$115.8 billion**, up **103%**, and guided the next quarter to **$130 billion** ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). The stock fell **14%** on Friday anyway, and the index had its worst week since early 2020. What moved the market was not the income statement. It was who was running the company, and who asked for him back.

## Where the Market Stands Today

The numbers as of the November 23 close.

- The **S&P 500 closed at 7,237.17**, up **326.78 points**, or **4.73%**, its best session since April 2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/22/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). It is **4.5% below** its October 30 close of 7,580.07, **7.4% below** its record close of **7,818.93**, and up about **5.7%** for the year.
- On **November 20** the index closed at **6,910.39**, down **3.70%**, its seventh consecutive lower close and **11.6% below** the record. That was the low of the month and it put the index below every Wall Street year-end target ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/21/the-sp-500-is-now-below-every-wall-street-year-end-target-for-2026/)).
- The **Nasdaq Composite closed at 24,089.56**, up **6.51%**, and the **Dow at 49,595.31**, up **3.01%**.
- The **VIX closed at 26.62** on November 23, down from **35.89** on November 20, when it traded as high as **40.03** intraday ([CBOE](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/vix/vix_historical_data)).
- **Nvidia rose 31%** on November 23 after the board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, adding roughly **$1 trillion** of market value. Micron rose **19%**, CoreWeave **22%**, and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF more than **16%** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/22/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).
- The **forward 12-month P/E is 17.7** on FactSet's November 5 reading, the lowest since November 2023 and below the five-year average of **19.8** and the ten-year average of **19.1**. The forward EPS estimate is **$415.82** ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/lowest-forward-12-month-p/e-ratio-for-the-sp-500-in-more-than-3-years)). On that estimate, Monday's close implies about **17.4 times**.
- **The earnings yield is about 5.75%** against a **10-year Treasury at 4.92%**, a gap of about **83 basis points**. On October 30 the same comparison was about **41 basis points**.
- **Third-quarter blended earnings growth was 31.4%** with **84%** of companies beating, and FactSet's measure of the aggregate surprise is the smallest since the fourth quarter of 2022. The fourth quarter is expected to grow **28.4%**, calendar 2026 **33.3%**, and calendar 2027 **16.1%** ([FactSet Earnings Insight](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_110626A.pdf)).
- The **2026 strategist consensus is 7,600**, with a range from **7,100** at Bank of America and Ned Davis to **8,100** at Oppenheimer. Friday's close was below every one of them, and Monday's rally lifted the index back above the two lowest. The **2027 consensus is 8,638**, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley at **8,700**, Ned Davis at **8,000**, and Yardeni at **9,000** ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/21/the-sp-500-is-now-below-every-wall-street-year-end-target-for-2026/); [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/no-bear-in-sight-major-wall-st-banks-set-2027-sp-500-targets-5041570)).
- **Energy is the cheapest sector at 11.7 times forward earnings**, and information technology is at **17.3**, its lowest since March 2020 ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/lowest-forward-12-month-p/e-ratio-for-the-sp-500-in-more-than-3-years)).
- The **consumer is still split**. October headline inflation was **0.4%** and core **0.3%**, the third straight month at that core pace, while October payrolls rose **165,000** and unemployment fell to **4.1%**. Underemployment rose to **7.9%** and the average duration of unemployment reached **27.1 weeks** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/cpi-inflation-report-october-2026.html); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/jobs-report-october-2026.html)).

Put the sixth bullet and the eleventh side by side. The index trades at **17.4 times** forward earnings, a discount to its own five-year average, and the gap between its earnings yield and the ten-year has widened from 41 to 83 basis points in a month. That is the bull case, and it is arithmetic rather than sentiment. The bear case is that the "E" in that multiple is the most optimistic number in the market, and the market just spent a week proving it does not believe the people who produce it.

## The Keystone Was Not the Earnings

The November 1 installment called Nvidia the keystone, the one stone through which everything the arch carries must pass. The month proved the metaphor right and the stone wrong.

Nvidia's business had an extraordinary quarter. Revenue doubled to **$115.8 billion**, data center revenue rose **110%** to **$107.6 billion**, gross margin held at **74.8%**, and the company guided the January quarter to **$130 billion** ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). If the year had turned on the earnings, the year would have turned up.

Instead, the chief executive, Jensen Huang, had stepped down earlier in November amid reporting on personal conduct ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/22/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). On Friday, on a press report that regulators were examining the company's accounting, the stock fell **14%** and **23%** over the week, its worst week since 2008 ([Yonhap Infomax](https://en.infomaxai.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=149582)). The index followed it into correction.

Then, on Monday, the board reinstated Huang. The stock rose **31%** and added roughly a trillion dollars of value in a session. President Trump said he spoke with the board and "convinced" them, and that Huang "did nothing wrong" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/22/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).

Read that sequence slowly, because it is the most important thing that happened to this index in 2026. A company reported the best quarter in its history and lost a fifth of its value on a governance story. Then a board reversed its own decision, reportedly at the urging of the president, and the company regained a trillion dollars. The earnings were never in doubt. What moved the market was who runs the company and who asked for him back.

That is not a valuation event. It is a political event wearing a ticker. And it tells you what the "corruption premium" in this series has always meant: not that a company is dishonest, but that the rules governing it can change for reasons that have nothing to do with its business. When the president of the United States can say publicly that he convinced a board to reinstate a chief executive, the market has to price the possibility that the next call goes the other way.

The same week supplied the other half of the AI financing story. **Anthropic went public on November 19** in the largest IPO ever, pricing at **$83**, opening at **$111.50**, and closing at **$97.17**, for a fully diluted valuation of about **$1.8 trillion** and gross proceeds of **$81.3 billion** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-ant-live-updates.html)). That is the equity market doing, in public, the financing the previous installment described: AI earnings and AI spending are the same number viewed from two ends, and the spending has to be funded by someone. For a decade the someone was private capital. Now it is the public market, and the public market gets a vote every morning. Anthropic popped 34% at the open, faded to a 17% gain, and fell about 3% on Monday while the rest of AI ripped. The appetite is real. So is the discrimination.

## The Math That Changed Sign

Here is the part that ought to make a careful investor uncomfortable, because it cuts against both the bulls and the bears.

Start with the earnings yield. An index at **17.4 times** forward earnings yields about **5.75%**. The ten-year Treasury pays **4.92%**. The difference is about **83 basis points**, roughly double the 41 basis points of a month ago. The gap widened because the index fell and the earnings estimate did not, which is the mechanical way a valuation cushion appears.

That is a genuine improvement in the price of equities relative to bonds, and it is the strongest argument the bulls have. But two things have to go right for it to pay: the earnings have to arrive, and the discount rate has to stay where it is or fall.

The first condition is now the whole question, and it is narrower than the index. FactSet's forward estimate has the S&P 500 earning **$415.82** over the next twelve months, a figure that assumes calendar 2026 growth of **33.3%** and calendar 2027 growth of **16.1%**. The 2026 number is being delivered. The 2027 number depends on the AI capital-spending cycle decelerating gracefully rather than stopping. UBS estimates global AI investment will reach **$1.4 trillion** in 2027, and Goldman Sachs expects earnings growth to slow to about **11%** from roughly **30%** this year, with the multiple stuck near **18 times** ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/no-bear-in-sight-major-wall-st-banks-set-2027-sp-500-targets-5041570)). Goldman's own stated downside risk is not 2027 at all. It is 2028, when a reversion to trend earnings would collide with a multiple that has not come down.

The second condition is the Fed, and it is less of a threat than it looks. A December hike is priced at **98%** and would take the funds rate to **4% to 4.25%**. The market puts roughly even odds on a second hike in January ([Kalshi](https://kalshi.com/economics/fed-rate-odds)). Against a ten-year at 4.92%, a quarter-point move in the policy rate is not the event. The event is what the two hikes say about 2027, and the answer so far is: a Fed that stops at 4.25% or 4.5% and sits there while inflation falls.

What changed in November is more subtle than either. For most of this year the index was priced against a rising long bond, and every escalation in the Gulf reached the model twice, once through energy earnings and once through the discount rate. In October the ten-year fell thirty-five basis points and the index still lost ground. In November the ten-year took a thirty-one basis point round trip and ended two basis points lower. The discount rate has stopped being the swing factor. The swing factor is the credibility of the earnings, and the credibility of the people who report them.

## The Corruption Premium

The standing question in this series is whether the quality of the government writing the rules has started showing up in the cost of capital. November answered it with a clearer example than any audit request.

**The president intervened in a public company's choice of chief executive, and the stock rose 31%.** That sentence is not analysis. It is the CNBC account: Trump said he spoke with Nvidia's board and "convinced" them to reinstate Huang, and that Huang "did nothing wrong" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/22/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The market's response was to add roughly a trillion dollars of value. Whatever one thinks of the decision, the precedent is the point. A market that has learned the president will intervene to support a large company's share price has learned something that does not appear in any earnings model.

**The tariff refund is now a $128 billion distribution made by filing date.** Customs had certified and sent **$128.4 billion** to the Treasury as of November 5, with **18,072 refunds worth about $737 million** held up for missing bank details ([J.M. Rodgers](https://www.jmrodgers.com/news/tariff-watch-november-16-2026-ieepa-refunds/)). The pool is about **$166 billion** collected from roughly **330,000 importers**, and the Justice Department is still arguing that relief belongs only to importers who sued, while a class certified on October 8 opens a path for those who did not ([Representative Jill Tokuda](https://tokuda.house.gov/media/press-releases/tokuda-warnock-and-sherman-call-for-federal-audit-of-refunds-of-166-billion-in-illegal-trump-tariffs); [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/customs-import-compliance-blog/the-customs-and-trade-world-turns-october-2026)).

**The exemption channel remains pharmaceutical.** Patented drugs from most countries face duties of up to **100%**, while nine specialty categories pay **0%** from one of nineteen named jurisdictions or on a case-by-case "urgent health need" finding ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/23/2026-19498/guidance-and-procedures-for-implementing-tariff-adjustments-for-specialty-pharmaceuticals-and)). The *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* measured what that kind of discretion was worth in the older program: a median of about **$51 million** in firm value per grant, with contributions to the party in power raising the odds of approval ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6777AC0EB32328B3E45A617F9B3E07E5/S0022109024000437a.pdf/div-class-title-the-political-economy-of-tariff-exemption-grants-div.pdf)).

**And the Fed fight has a date.** The White House moved its hearing on Governor Cook to **December 14**. Cook's lawyer wrote that she has "no legal obligation to participate in an unfair hearing" and called the panel "a façade designed to obscure a predetermined decision" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/lisa-cook-trump-fed-committee-hearing.html)). The proceeding is inside this window.

The midterm result adds the variable the index has not priced. Democrats won the House and the Senate and will control Congress in January ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-election-house-senate-democrats-republicans-trump-f126e12e05b579a327b98481c0ae2226)). Divided government is usually good for equities because it means less legislation. But the oversight that comes with it will keep the refunds, the waivers, and the Fed in the news, and the market has to price a transition rather than a gridlock. At **83 basis points** over the risk-free rate, the index is charging very little for any of this. That is the number to remember when the next governance headline arrives.

## The Ninety-Day Map

Take the premise seriously: no improvement in the war, no breakthrough, no formal reopening of the strait. Here is the path from November 24 to February 22.

**November 26 through December 4.** Thanksgiving, OPEC+ on the twenty-ninth, and November employment on December 4. A second strong payroll print would confirm that October was not a blip, and would make the January hike a base case. A weak one would revive the soft-landing trade that the index rallied on in October.

**December 8 through December 14.** The cluster. The Fed hikes on the ninth and publishes a dot plot. November inflation lands on the tenth. The funding deadline is the eleventh, with another short-term stopgap into early 2027 the likeliest outcome. The Cook hearing is the fourteenth. A rate decision, an inflation print, a funding cliff, and a removal proceeding in one week. Any one of them can move the index; together they are the densest stretch in the window.

**January 3 through January 27.** A new Congress, December employment and inflation in the second week, and a Fed meeting with no projections. This is the stretch that decides whether November's correction was a repricing or the start of a bear market, and it has no scheduled catalyst in it. A market with nothing ahead looks backward.

**February.** The refunding, the last of the winter data, and the window closes on the twenty-second.

That is the base case, and it lands the index between **6,900 and 7,700**: a market that has recovered from correction, kept most of the year's gain, and spent the winter discovering that its largest company trades on governance as much as on earnings.

**The escalation branch.** A full closure of the strait, or strikes that draw Iran's neighbors in directly, is the 1973 scenario. From its January 1973 peak to its October 1974 trough, the S&P 500 fell **48.2%** over 630 days ([History of Market](https://historyofmarket.com/articles/sp500-maximum-drawdowns)). Measured from the October 6 record, that is roughly **4,050**. The Volcker precedent, where the funds rate was pushed toward 20% to break a supply shock, took the index down **27.1%** from November 1980 to August 1982, which from the record is roughly **5,700** ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidmarotta/2017/10/11/volkers-bear-the-bear-market-of-1982/)). And the milder 1990 analog, a **19.9%** fall from the July peak, is roughly **6,260** ([A Wealth of Common Sense](https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2018/12/the-forgotten-bear-markets/)). The index has already given back 7.4% from the record, so the 1990 analog is only about 13% below Monday's close. That is the branch that is closest, and it does not require a new war. It only requires the AI earnings to disappoint.

**The paradox branch.** The earnings are real, the multiple is below its five-year average, and the 2027 consensus is **8,638**. If Nvidia's reinstatement holds, the fourth quarter delivers something close to the 28.4% the analysts expect, and the Fed stops after January, the index has a reason to buy that has nothing to do with the war. A reclaim of the **7,819** record before February 22 is the first step, and a push toward **8,300** is not a fantasy in that world. It is what happens when a de-rating reverses and the earnings never showed up in the denominator to justify it.

The point of the map is not that the bear case is certain. It is that the base case now depends on a company's board and a president's phone call as much as on a semiconductor order book, and the melt-up depends on both of those plus the Fed and the consumer behaving at once.

## What a Careful Investor Does

The playbook rhymes with the bond article's, because it is the same storm seen through a different window.

- **Do not sell the core; rebalance the concentration.** The index sits 7.4% below a record set seven weeks ago, and the sector that carried it can lose and regain a trillion dollars in three sessions. Rebalancing into strength is not timing. It is the only free lunch in a market where the largest seven companies are more than a third of the index.
- **Know which side of the earnings split you own.** The 2027 consensus assumes the AI leaders' growth slows and the rest of the index catches up. If that is right, leadership broadens and the money in the crowded trade has to go somewhere. That is where the opportunity in this window lives.
- **Own the businesses that get paid now.** An 83 basis point earnings-yield premium over the ten-year is not compensation for duration risk in a company whose cash flows arrive in 2031. It is compensation for owning a company with current cash flow, pricing power, and modest debt. Energy, at **11.7 times** forward earnings, is the cheapest sector in the index and the one that pays you if this article's premise is right.
- **Treat Nvidia as a position, not a religion.** Whatever you own, size it so that a 20% gap on a governance headline does not change your plan. November proved that a 31% move can come from a board meeting rather than an earnings call.
- **Demand a margin for the governance risk.** The 83 basis point gap is the market's entire charge for the possibility that a rule changes because someone made a call. That is thin for a market that has now seen it happen. Own a little more cash, and keep it earning 4% while it waits.

## The Long Way of Saying It

The S&P 500 is not priced stupidly, and that is what makes this winter dangerous. At 17.4 times forward earnings it trades below its own five-year average, because earnings grew faster than prices for four straight quarters. The bears have to argue against that number, and the number is stubborn.

What the index is priced for is narrower than the multiple suggests. It is priced for one company's leadership, one president's willingness to intervene, one discount rate that has stopped deciding the outcome, one consumer who is employed but unhappy, and one set of rules stable enough that nobody has to think about them. November tested every one of those assumptions in three weeks, and the arch held. But it held because a board reversed itself and a president made a call, not because the stone was sound. An arch repaired that way is not stronger. It is an arch whose seam you have now seen, with a gold brace over it and someone else's hand on the brace.

Storms do not send invitations, but they do keep appointments. This window has one on December 9, one in the second week of December, and a long quiet January in which the calendar offers nothing at all. Build the defenses before the lightning strikes, and remember that the keystone is the stone someone else can replace.
