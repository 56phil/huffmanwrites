description: |
  John F. Kennedy's *Profiles in Courage* named the only two directions a person can move when pressure arrives: courage, or compliance. He set the words against each other and left them there, because there is no third one. This book is about the second, and about what happened to the price.

  It is not a book about a bad man. Thousands of people, most of them decent and most of them competent, have been asked a version of Kennedy's question and answered the other way. They kept their seats and their salaries, and that is the point. The apparatus is the subject, taken one institution at a time: the law firms, the universities, the press, the civil service, the military, the corporations, the courts, the election machinery, and the cabinet, where the standard meets its smallest unit.

  The finding was not the expected one. Where the country had structure, it held, and it held without requiring anybody to be brave: sixty courts slowed or blocked initiatives, thirty-eight states refused a request for voter data, a press corps walked out rather than become an outlet for approved information, eight prosecutors resigned rather than file a motion they believed was corrupt. Where the country had a single point of decision, it did not hold. Refusing costs the career and complying costs nothing, so the bill is handed to someone with less standing, less access, and less voice. The remedy is not better people. It is a structure in which decency is affordable.
