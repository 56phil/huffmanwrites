### Maintenance — September 20, 2026 — Em-dash limit amended: quotations and ranges exempt, and now enforced

### Maintenance — September 19, 2026 — Translation citations: link requirement added + corpus audit

- **New `CLAUDE.md` convention (per Philip's directive):** quotations from translated works must name the **translator, translation, and year** — `Author, *Work*, §N (trans. Name, Year)`. ## Aphorism Attribution Audit — September 19, 2026

- **Triggered by "Are you sure there are no fabrications in the site?" The honest answer was no**, and the specific reason is structural: `check-quotes.py` exempts **68 pre-rule attributions**, so those have **never been checked by anything**. One of them (the Seneca epigraph) was already proven fabricated. The baseline records *unverified* attributions, not verified ones.
- **16 of the 68 are attributed to sources where misattribution is the norm** — Lincoln, Churchill, Einstein, "Chinese Proverb" and similar. Those were audited against the named authors' primary corpora and against the two established quote-forensics authorities (Quote Investigator; Snopes; plus the Churchill Project for Churchill). **Three of four audited were misattributed.**
- **Corrected (all three were shipping as fact):**
  - **"Nearly all men can stand adversity…give him power" — not Lincoln.** No locus in his writings or speeches. It is a compression of **Robert G. Ingersoll's** tribute to him: "Most people can bear adversity. But if you wish to know what a man really is, give him power. This is the supreme test" (*Abraham Lincoln: A Lecture*, 1895, p. 52). Now credited as "said *of* Lincoln by Ingersoll", with the primary source linked. Two occurrences in the same file, both fixed.
  - **"If you're going through hell, keep going" — not Churchill.** Catalogued under "Red Herrings: False Attributions" in *Churchill By Himself*; the Churchill Project and Quote Investigator both classify it as not his. Earliest trace is an anonymous 1943 exchange — two decades before it was first attached to him. Now labelled a misattribution, with both forensics sources linked.
  - **"The best time to plant a tree…" — not a Chinese proverb.** Quote Investigator found "no substantive evidence" for a Chinese origin; Barry Popik confirms no such proverb is known. Earliest trace: a Cleveland newspaper, March 1967, where a city councilman repeated it and *declined credit*. The "Chinese proverb" label only appears from 1985. Now labelled an anonymous modern adage.
  - **Genuine, and left attributed: "Travel is never a matter of money, but of courage" — Paulo Coelho, *Aleph* (2011).** Confirmed in six scanned editions via Open Library's search-inside, which returns the exact wording. Worth recording because the online suspicion about Coelho is *wrong* here: it is one of the few lines of his that is actually his. Now carries a citation.
- **The gate caught the follow-on work correctly.** Editing a baselined attribution withdraws its exemption, so all four files immediately failed the quote gate for lacking a source link. That is the design working: the debt is collected as each file is touched. All four now carry links, and the gate passes.
- **A measurable signal that the fix was real:** the gate's own attribution tally dropped from **66 to 63**, because three phantom attributions are no longer parsed as genuine authors. The count falling is the evidence that misattributions were removed rather than relabelled in place.
- **Still outstanding, and stated rather than implied:** 52 baselined attributions remain exempt, including the remaining high-risk set (Carl Sagan ×8, and others). They are listed and can be audited the same way. **Until each is checked, they are unverified — not verified-clean.**
- **Operational lesson: verifying a deploy needs a cache-busted fetch, and the first fetch can lie.** After the attribution fix deployed successfully (run `35449419970`, both jobs green), a plain `curl` of the live page still returned the **old** attribution, in both the rendered body and the JSON-LD. That looked like a failed deploy. It was **CDN propagation lag** — the same URL with a cache-busting query string returned the corrected page, and three consecutive busted fetches all showed the fix. **Conclusion: never conclude a deploy failed from a single uncached-buster fetch; re-fetch with a unique query parameter before treating live output as evidence.** This is the third time in this session that an unverified assumption about state produced a wrong claim.

## Render Integrity — September 19, 2026

- **Found while asking a different question** ("are you sure there are no fabrications?"). Auditing the site rather than trusting the gates surfaced a **user-visible defect that no check in this repo could see**: every mobile hero image on `/books/stoic-backgammon/` was broken.
- **The mechanism.** The Stoic Backgammon hero files have a **space in their filenames** (`sb-gtl 16x9.webp`). Hugo percent-encodes a space in `src` but **not** in `srcset`, so `<source srcset="/img/articles/sb-gtl 4x5.webp">` hit Go's template URL safety filter and rendered the sentinel `srcset=/#ZgotmplZ`. The mobile hero never loaded. The desktop `<img>` loaded fine, which is exactly why it went unnoticed. **Six of the seven heroes on that page were affected, and they had been broken on the live site.**
- **Why every existing gate missed it.** The source markdown is valid, the asset exists, the build succeeds, the page returns **200**, and the frontmatter is correct. The defect exists only in the rendered output — a class nothing was checking.
- **Fixed at the source** in `layouts/shortcodes/section-hero.html` by percent-encoding both URLs in the shortcode, rather than depending on the caller to pass pre-escaped paths. Verified: `ZgotmplZ` count across the whole build went from 6 to **0**, and all 7 heroes now decode at mobile width (`naturalWidth` 1024, lazy-loading forced by scrolling).
- **New gate: `scripts/check-render-integrity.py`, wired into CI after the build.** It fails on (a) Go template sentinels (`ZgotmplZ`, `<no value>`, `%!s(` …) and unrendered `{{< shortcode >}}` in the output, and (b) local `src`/`srcset`/`href` references that do not resolve in `public/`. Negative-tested: re-injecting the defect fails the gate; restoring it passes.
- **Deliberately not scanned: bare `{{` / `}}`.** Those are template delimiters but also legitimate minified-JS syntax, and scanning for them flagged **340 healthy pages**. A gate that cries wolf is worse than no gate — it trains the reader to ignore it. Unrendered shortcodes are matched precisely by shape instead.
- **Scope:** `section-hero` is used in exactly one file, so the bug was contained to that page. No other page in the build contains a sentinel.
